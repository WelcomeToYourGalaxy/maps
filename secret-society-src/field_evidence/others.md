# Evidence: every other outside party (movements, religious groups, banks, lodges, elite networks, lenders)

## How this was gathered

This replaces an earlier file built from headlines only. Everything below comes from a page I opened, using 25 WebFetch and 6 WebSearch calls. WebFetch returns a summary, not the full page, so each figure should be checked against the source before it is published. Several key items rest on Wikipedia or a mirror of it, and these are marked.

Calculations marked "my calculation" are mine. No figure comes from memory.

## Political and armed movements

### Paramilitaries, Colombia: 45 members of Congress convicted
- **Figure:** 45 congress members and 7 governors convicted by the Supreme Court; 4 congress members absolved. Investigations since 2006 implicated "over 11,000 Colombians". AUC (paramilitary) leader Vicente Castaño said in 2005 that "paramilitary groups count more than 35 percent of the Congress as friends".
- **Measures:** criminal convictions for paramilitary ties in Colombia since 2006. The date of the count and the number of seats were not stated. The 35% is a boast by the party concerned, not a finding.
- **Source:** "45 Colombian congress members convicted over paramilitary ties since 2006", Colombia Reports, date not captured. https://colombiareports.com/colombia-congress-members-convicted-parapolitics/amp
- **Directness:** direct. These are court convictions, the strongest kind of evidence in this file.
- **Steering:** convictions show ties. This page did not address whether paramilitaries decided elections or laws.

### Gülen movement, Turkey: about 160,000 removed, but not individually proven
- **Figures:**
  - Over 160,000 suspended or dismissed, about 10% of roughly 2 million public employees.
  - A cumulative 300,000 by February 2025.
  - 31,000 police and 15,000 military removed (Erdoğan, April 2019).
  - 2,745 judges removed in July 2016, "about 36% of judges".
  - About 500,000 detained by March 2019 (government data).
- **Measures:** removals by the Turkish state after the 2016 coup attempt. The page's own totals conflict (527,000 and 300,000 arrested). The removals are not limited to Gülen suspects: the same page lists Kurdish teachers and HDP members. Some totals include people outside this brief's institution list, such as teachers and health staff.
- **Against:**
  - The Venice Commission said collective dismissals "did not refer to verifiable evidence, related to each individual case".
  - The UN human rights office described "arbitrary mass dismissals".
  - A Turkish official, Erhan Afyoncu (February 2025), reportedly acknowledged that many of those dismissed were not directly linked to the coup, and pointed to intelligence reports and a software tool called "Fetometre".
- **Sources:**
  - "Purges in Turkey following the 2016 Turkish coup attempt", Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purges_in_Turkey_following_the_2016_Turkish_coup_attempt
  - "Turkey: state of emergency justified but measures taken excessive", Council of Europe, 2016. https://www.coe.int/en/web/portal/news-2016/-/asset_publisher/StEVosr24HJ2/content/turkey-had-good-reasons-to-declare-the-state-of-emergency-but-went-too-far-with-the-emergency-measures-venice-commission
- **Directness:** weak proxy. This is an upper limit set by the accuser. The excerpt of the Council of Europe page I read did not mention the Gülen network's presence in state bodies, so the true number inside the state remains unmeasured.

### RSS, India: 71% of BJP ministers, with control disputed
- **Figures:**
  - 38 of 53 BJP ministers have a Sangh background, about 71%. In 2014 it was 41 of 66, about 62%.
  - 146 of 303 BJP Lok Sabha MPs (48%) have RSS affiliations.
  - About one in five BJP MPs with RSS links became a minister, against about one in fourteen without.
- **Measures:** India, article dated 27 January 2020. No data source or method is given. "Background" is not defined and is counted broadly.
- **Against:** an RSS functionary said "The RSS has no role in deciding tickets for the party or deciding the portfolio". The political scientist Suhas Palshikar said "it is difficult to say who is controlling whom".
- **Steering:** the article gives policy examples: a separate animal husbandry ministry, Sangh suggestions to the draft education policy, and a request to speed up the Ayodhya case after RSS and VHP pressure. It proves causation for none of them.
- **Source:** "RSS in Modi govt in numbers: 3 of 4 ministers are rooted in the Sangh", ThePrint, 2020. https://theprint.in/politics/rss-in-modi-govt-in-numbers-3-of-4-ministers-are-rooted-in-the-sangh/353942/
- **Directness:** weak proxy. It is an open background in the governing party's parent body, so it is largely not "outside" entry.

### Far-right networks, Germany: under 1% of security forces
- **Figure:** the share of far-right-sympathising personnel was under one percent, across police, security services and military, federal and all 16 states. Seehofer: "we have a small number of cases." Haldenwang: "Each of these cases is a case too much."
- **Measures:** report by the domestic intelligence agency (BfV), 2020. The article gave no case count or staff total.
- **Source:** "Far-Right Extremism Found in Less Than One Percent of German Security Forces: Report", Algemeiner, 2020. https://www.algemeiner.com/2020/10/08/far-right-extremism-found-in-less-than-one-percent-of-german-security-forces-report/
- **Directness:** close proxy. It is one of the few official shares of an institution, but it was produced by a security agency about security agencies.
- **Steering:** none shown. It describes chatrooms and threatening emails, treated as individual cases.

### Taliban, Afghanistan: the official share fell from half to a quarter
- **Figures:**
  - 96 insider attacks and 152 coalition deaths, 2008 to 17 June 2017. The peak was 44 attacks and 61 deaths in 2012.
  - Gen. John Allen said about half were Taliban infiltrators (May 2012), then "approximately 25%" due to "Taliban infiltration and/or coercion" (August 2012).
  - NATO commanders first attributed an estimated 90% to "cultural differences and personal enmity".
- **Note:** the authors argue the Taliban role is larger than NATO admitted. The page's 2015 share of deaths ("80%") looks wrong and should not be used.
- **Source:** Long War Journal, "Green-on-blue attacks" data page, updated 2017. https://longwarjournal.org/archives/2012/08/green-on-blue_attack.php
- **Directness:** close proxy. It counts attacks, not infiltrators, and the source of the attribution was a party to the war.

### Al-Shabaab, Somalia: UN confirms infiltration but gives no count
- **Finding:**
  - "Al-Shabaab has also infiltrated Federal Government of Somalia institutions" (para 11).
  - It "has infiltrated Mogadishu's port institutions" and shipping agents, gaining access to cargo manifests (para 31).
  - One agent was told to pay over $70,000 in tax on one shipment (para 32).
  - A suicide bomber employed at the Benadir Regional Administration since May 2018 under a false identity killed the Mayor and at least nine others in July 2019 (para 11).
- **Against:** 315 of 678 logbook soldiers appeared in the 2019 biometric roll (para 94), but the Panel does not attribute the gap to infiltration.
- **Source:** UN Security Council document S/2019/858 (Panel of Experts on Somalia), 2019. https://www.ecoi.net/en/file/local/2019947/S_2019_858_E.pdf
- **Directness:** direct in kind, with no share. "The report gives no count" of compromised staff. Steering is shown at the port, where the access was used to tax imports.

### Baath Party, Iraq (for comparison)
- **Figure:**
  - About 30,000 ex-Baathists fired from ministries, of whom roughly 15,000 were later allowed back.
  - 5,000 to 10,000 top leaders could not appeal.
  - The "500,000-member" military and intelligence services were dissolved.
  - About 9,000 ex-Baathists were estimated to be back in the defence and interior ministries and intelligence services (Kazimi).
  - The party had about 2 million members in a population of roughly 24 million.
- **Source:** Council on Foreign Relations backgrounder on Iraq's debaathification, February 2005 (archived). https://www.cfr.org/backgrounder/iraq-debaathification
- **Directness:** weak proxy. This was a ruling party removed by an occupier, not an outside party entering. The figures do show how far a party purge can overcount or be reversed: half of the ministry dismissals were undone on appeal.

## Religious movements

### Unification Church, Japan: 179 of 379 lawmakers, mostly light ties
- **Figure:** 179 of 379 LDP national lawmakers (about 47%) reported links. Of these, 96 attended events, 20 made speeches, nearly 50 paid money at events, 29 accepted donations, and 17 accepted followers as campaign volunteers.
- **Measures:** the party's own survey, Japan, September 2022. It excluded Abe and local assembly members.
- **Source:** "Half of Japan governing lawmakers tied to Unification Church", AP via News4Jax, 2022. https://www.news4jax.com/news/world/2022/09/08/half-of-japan-governing-lawmakers-tied-to-unification-church/
- **Directness:** close proxy for ties, weak for steering. Self-reported, so it could be low. The heaviest listed tie, campaign volunteers, covers 17 of 379, about 4.5% (my calculation).
- **Steering:** the article does not show the church steering policy. The claim that followers are "active in influencing policies" in local assemblies comes from critics, without detail.

### Unification Church, South Korea: 3,500 suspected of joining before party votes
- **Figure:**
  - About 3,500 followers joined the People Power Party around its 2023 leadership race and the 2024 election (about 3,100 between October 2022 and March 2023, about 400 in January to April 2024).
  - About 120,000 church members were on the party list, found by matching it against a 1.2 million church database.
  - Texts show a former church official asking about "the exact position of the president" and noting "11,101 new members".
- **Against:** the party says the overlap is natural, since about 10 percent of Koreans are party members. 120,000 of 1.2 million is also 10% (my calculation). Charges would need proof of coercion, and none over the overlap were reported.
- **Source:** "Probe into PPP collusion finds 3,500 Unification Church members joined party ahead of key events", Korea JoongAng Daily, date not captured. https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/probe-into-ppp-collusion-finds-3500-unification-church-members-joined-party-ahead-of-key-events/12289147
- **Directness:** direct in kind, because it describes an attempt to enter a party and sway its vote. These are investigators' suspicions, not findings.

## Secret societies and lodges

### P2, Italy: 962 names, including all secret service heads
- **Figure:** 962 names, found 17 March 1981. These include all secret service heads, 195 armed forces officers, 44 members of parliament, 3 ministers and 1 party secretary. The page says at least 1,000 more may be secret, because the numbers start at 1,600. The officer sub-counts it gives do not add up to 195.
- **Finding:** the parliamentary commission concluded P2 was a secret criminal organisation that caused "the pollution of the public life of a nation". The page does not document specific decisions P2 steered. Gelli and Musumeci were condemned for trying to mislead the Bologna massacre investigation.
- **Source:** "Propaganda Due", Wikiwand mirror of Wikipedia. https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Propaganda_Due
- **Directness:** direct in kind, for one country. This is the best-documented lodge case read here, and it is 45 years old.

### Afrikaner Broederbond, South Africa: top offices held, but steering is contested
- **Figure:**
  - A list of about 7,500 members was published in 1978.
  - The page says every prime minister and state president from 1948 to 1994 was a member, yet it also lists P. W. Botha as having left.
  - It gives no share of cabinet or civil service. Several claims carry "citation needed".
- **Against, from a paper on state companies 1948-60:**
  - Iscor's new board appointees were "not all nationalists" and, with one possible exception, not Broederbond members.
  - At Escom in 1959, nine of the 33 top management posts were held by Afrikaner nationalists.
  - Appointments were made by ministers. The paper cites Lazar's conclusion that the Broederbond did not dominate the National Party until the late 1950s.
  - From 1960-61 Diederichs, a former Broederbond chairman, did appoint members to Iscor's board.
- **Sources:**
  - "Afrikaner Broederbond", Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaner_Broederbond
  - Tim Cross, "The Afrikaner takeover, nationalist politics and the colonisation of South Africa's Parastatals 1948-1960", SAS-Space, date not captured. https://sas-space.sas.ac.uk/4255/1/Tim_Cross_-_The_Afrikaner_takeover%2C_nationalist_politics_and_the_colonisation_of_South_Africa%27s_Parastatals_1948-1960.pdf
- **Directness:** close proxy. Holding office is well shown. The one archival paper read shows steering arriving late and through a minister, not through orders from the organisation.

### Freemasons, London police: about 316 declared, about 1% of officers
- **Figure:** "about 316" officers and staff disclosed membership of Freemasonry or similar organisations. The Met has "over 30,000" officers. That is about 1% (my calculation, and the two figures do not cover the same people).
- **Measures:** declarations under a compulsory rule, one force. Masons were not counted separately. The article cites no specific steered case, only "other investigations and misconduct alleging Masonic influence".
- **Source:** "Over 300 Masonic membership among Met police sparks corruption concerns and legal challenge", via inkl, date not captured. https://www.inkl.com/news/over-300-masonic-membership-among-met-police-sparks-corruption-concerns-and-legal-challenge
- **Directness:** weak proxy under this brief, since membership alone is excluded. It is useful as a rare compulsory count.

## Banks and banking families

### Federal Reserve Banks, US: 36% from banking, and no evidence directors made the decisions
- **Figure:** 73 of 202 head office directors (about 36%) came from banking, 2006-2010. Of 91 survey respondents in 2010, at least 56 had financial experience. Cases covered the Goldman Sachs, Lehman Brothers and Merrill Lynch ties during the 2008 crisis.
- **Against:** "we did not find evidence that Reserve Bank boards of directors participated directly in making any decisions" about the emergency programmes. The concern was the appearance of conflict and reputational risk.
- **Source:** GAO-12-18, US Government Accountability Office, data 2006-2010. https://gao.gov/assets/a585808.html (I read the first 100,000 of about 125,000 characters; Appendix I on affiliated firms was not read.)
- **Directness:** close proxy, and it cuts against the obvious answer. It is an official audit that looked for this and did not find it.

### Finance backgrounds of governors and ministers, 32 OECD countries: a link, not proven cause
- **Figure:**
  - About 30% of governors (49 of 165) and 16% of finance ministers (63 of 392) worked in finance before office, 1973-2005.
  - Average annual deregulation was about 0.0276 under governors with that background and 0.0157 without.
  - Finance ministers showed no such effect from past experience, but those who deregulated more were more likely to take finance jobs afterwards (about 6.4 percentage points per standard deviation).
- **Note:** the author says results are not causally identified.
- **Source:** Elisa Wirsching, "The revolving door for political elites: policymakers' professional background and financial regulation", EBRD Working Paper 222, 2018. https://www.ebrd.com/content/dam/ebrd_dxp/assets/pdfs/office-of-the-chief-economist/working-papers/working-papers-2018/WP-222.pdf
- **Directness:** weak proxy for infiltration, because it measures careers rather than banks placing people. It is the only multi-country statistical test of decisions in this file.

## Elite clubs and policy networks

### Corporate and policy boards: ties measured, decisions not
- **Figure:** a study of the 500 largest global firms and 11 policy boards (including Bilderberg, the Trilateral Commission and the World Economic Forum), 1996 and 2006. It found people holding at least one policy-board seat rose about 50%. Those sitting on several boards fell 11.3% to 887.
- **Note:** the summary says the study "measures network ties and structural position, not decision-making outcomes". Its claim of steering is interpretive.
- **Source:** summary of Carroll and Sapinski (2010, *International Sociology*), "The Global Corporate Elite and the Transnational Policy-Planning Network, 1996-2006", LET site. https://let.iiec.unam.mx/print/2799 (secondary; check against the original.)
- **Directness:** weak proxy. I found no study showing that one of these bodies changed a specific decision through members inside an institution.

## Lenders

### Creditors in control, 1870-1913: 8 of 43 defaults
- **Figure:** 43 default episodes in 25 countries. 12 (28%) brought a supersanction, covering about 40% of defaulted debt. By my reading of the paper's Table 2 (a classification from the fetched summary, not stated in the paper), 8 were fiscal or customs control: Tunis, Egypt, Turkey, Servia, Greece, Morocco, Santo Domingo, Liberia.
- **Note:** the paper's text names only six fiscal-control cases, so it is inconsistent with its own table. Defaults under supersanction fell in risk and yield, and the authors present this as effective enforcement.
- **Source:** Mitchener and Weidenmier, "Supersanctions and Sovereign Debt Repayment", NBER Working Paper 11472. https://www.nber.org/papers/w11472.pdf
- **Directness:** direct for its period, and the only count of its kind found.

### Ottoman Public Debt Administration: 9,000 staff, run by creditors
- **Figure:** a council with one representative each of British, French, German, Austrian, Italian, Dutch and Ottoman creditors, plus one for the state. It started with 5,000 officials and peaked at 9,000 employees, "more than the Ottoman finance ministry". It collected the salt and tobacco monopolies and other taxes, from 1881.
- **Source:** "Ottoman Public Debt Administration", Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Public_Debt_Administration
- **Directness:** direct, single historical case.

### GEMAP, Liberia: foreign co-signature, 2005
- **Figure:** foreign experts were placed with "co-signature authority on government spending" at bodies including the Central Bank, the Bureau of Customs and Excise, the National Port Authority and the Forestry Development Authority, for three years, extendable. It was signed under threat of lost aid; the World Bank representative said no more reconstruction funding until corruption was addressed.
- **Source:** American Society of International Law, Insights vol. 9 issue 38, 2005. https://asil.org/insights/volume-9-issue-38/
- **Directness:** close proxy. It was an open agreement that may fall under the brief's exclusion of public conditions, though the agents did sit inside state functions. The page does not say whether they overrode decisions, and I read no count of modern cases.

## Adjacent items (may belong in another part)

### Communist Party organisations in Chinese firms
- **Figure:** 48.3% of sampled private firms had Party organisations in 2018, up from 35.6% in 2012 (CPES survey, 3,973 firms). Top 500 private enterprises: 92.4%. Foreign-invested firms: 12%, in official data for Hangzhou only. Whether these bodies affect strategy "remains unclear".
- **Source:** MacroPolo article on Party committees in China's private sector (title and date not captured). https://macropolo.org/party-committees-private-sector-china/
- **Directness:** close proxy for presence, no measure of steering. It could be the largest count anywhere in this study, depending on how the caller classifies a ruling party inside companies.

### Stasi in the West
- **Figure:** 3,000 to 3,500 informers working in West Germany and West Berlin, of whom about 1,550 worked for the HVA, the Stasi's foreign intelligence branch. About 12,000 West Germans worked for the Stasi over its existence. Inside East Germany, about 624,000 informal collaborators over its lifetime.
- **Note:** the page gives no evidence of placement in West German government or parliament.
- **Source:** "Unofficial collaborator", Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unofficial_collaborator
- **Directness:** weak proxy here. It belongs under foreign spy services, and domestic informers are a state's own, not outsiders.

## Reading

1. Among these parties, armed and political movements show the only evidence that combines large numbers, court or UN confirmation and spread across regions, with Colombia and Somalia the strongest cases.
2. The biggest numbers are the least reliable: Turkey's 160,000 or more are people a government accused, and the Venice Commission said the dismissals did not refer to verifiable evidence in each case.
3. High shares mostly shrink when ties are separated from control: Japan's 47% includes only 17 lawmakers who took church volunteers, and India's 71% is a party's own background with control denied.
4. Where someone looked for steering directly, the results often cut against the obvious story: GAO found no evidence that Reserve Bank boards made the crisis decisions, and the Taliban share of insider attacks dropped from half to a quarter in official statements.
5. Lodges have one strong historical case (P2) and one partly contested one (Broederbond), while the one recent compulsory count is about 1% of a police force, with no steered case given.
6. Elite clubs have good data on who sits where and almost none on decisions, so under this brief's definition they score near zero on proof, which is not proof of zero effect.
7. Lender agents plainly ran state functions before 1914, in 8 of 43 defaults by one reading of the table, but today's cases are few in what I read and mostly open agreements.
8. Guess: within these six parties, movements hold about 60-70% of infiltration, religious movements about 10%, banks about 10%, lodges about 5-10%, elite clubs under 5% and lenders under 5%.
9. I hold the order at low confidence and the percentages at very low confidence, because they rest on single-country cases, and because banks and elite networks may be undercounted since their influence rarely leaves a court record.
10. Missing: any multi-country survey for these parties, anything on banking families, world Masonic membership, a count of modern lender agents, Nippon Kaigi, and almost anything from Central Asia, Southeast Asia or Latin America beyond Colombia.
