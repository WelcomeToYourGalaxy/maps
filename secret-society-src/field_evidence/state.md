- Leads from the earlier attempt that I did not open are dropped from the findings and listed at the end.

## Findings

### 1. Stasi agents in West Germany
- **Figure:** "Nach Schätzungen waren zuletzt 3.000 bis 3.500 Bundesbürger Agenten" (an estimated 3,000 to 3,500 West German citizens were agents toward the end), "sicher jedoch darunter 1.553 allein in Diensten der HV A" (of whom 1,553 were certainly in the service of the HV A, the foreign directorate alone). By Stasi statistics, science and technology 39%; ministry administrations and political parties 19% each as summarised; military institutions 8%; Interior Ministry, its agencies, police and intelligence 5%. Most worked in industry and universities. Named placements reach the Chancellery (Guillaume), the Bundestag (Steiner, bribed with 50,000 DM to abstain in the 1972 no-confidence vote), NATO (Rupp) and RWE's personnel chief (Glocke).
- **Measures:** Citizens of one country working for one neighbouring service, in the final years of East Germany. Stasi's own statistics. The summary's wording on the 19% is ambiguous, and the shares listed add to 90%.
- **Source:** "Spione beim Klassenfeind", Bundeszentrale für politische Bildung (bpb), year not seen. https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutsche-teilung/stasi/222253/spione-beim-klassenfeind/
- **Directness:** Direct. The best evidence here on where agents sat, because the service's records survived and were opened. No equivalent exists for any living service.

### 2. Americans who spied
- **Figure:** 148 offenders, 1945 to 1998. "half of the American spies have been military personnel, half civilian" (a table shows 72 and 56). "87 offenders (62%) were volunteer spies"; 24 recruited by family or friends; 29 recruited by foreign intelligence. Money was the motive for 72.7% of volunteers, 56.0% of family or friend recruits and 53.5% of foreign-intelligence recruits. "26% of all offenders were caught before they could do any damage." Separately, PERSEREC holds 139 case summaries, 1975-2008, and says "most offenders were trusted insiders, not foreign agents."
- **Measures:** Caught US cases only, drawn from public record. Includes people working for friendly states. Cases, not unknown spies.
- **Sources:** "Treason 101 by the numbers", archived Hanford copy, author and year not seen. https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20121014011956/http:/www.hanford.gov/files.cfm/Treason101_bythenumbers.pdf ; *Espionage and Other Compromises of National Security*, PERSEREC, July 2009 edition. https://www.dhra.mil/PERSEREC/Espionage-Cases/
- **Directness:** Direct for one country. It cuts against the picture of spy services planting people: most walked in.

### 3. Ukraine: treason suspects and insiders in security bodies
- **Figure:** The SBU has served "more than 3,200 people with a notice of suspicion of treason" since 2014: almost 500 in 2014-2021, with indictments against more than 350; more than 2,700 from early 2022 to end 2025, with indictments against more than 2,500. The SBU says the law does not require tracking cases by professional affiliation. Separately, on 17 July 2022, Zelensky reported "651 criminal proceedings have been registered regarding treason and collaboration activities" among employees of prosecutor's offices, pre-trial investigation bodies and other law enforcement, and "more than 60 employees of the prosecutor's office and the Security Service of Ukraine remained in the occupied territory."
- **Measures:** Suspects and proceedings in one country at war. The 651 is the closest thing here to a count of true insiders, though it is a count of proceedings, in the first five months of the invasion, and may include people who stayed under occupation rather than people recruited beforehand. The article summary did not break it down by agency.
- **Sources:** LIGA.net, 25 Dec 2025. https://news.liga.net/en/politics/news/over-the-past-10-years-more-than-3200-people-have-been-suspected-of-high-treason-sbu ; CNN via ABC17, 17 July 2022. https://abc17news.com/news/national-world/cnn-europe-mideast-africa/2022/07/17/zelensky-fires-top-officials-over-staffers-collaboration-with-russia/
- **Directness:** 651 is a close proxy for institutional insiders. 3,200 is a weak proxy, because the share with any post is unknown and the SBU says it is not tracked.

### 4. Taiwan
- **Figure:** "In 2025, Taiwan's national security services estimated there were over 5,000 spies working for China in Taiwan", with no method given, cited to a 19FortyFive report. 64 prosecuted in 2024, against 16 in 2021. 159 indicted since 2020, 60% of them active or retired military. In 2024, 28 active service members (43%) and 15 veterans (23%). The Mainland Affairs Council spokesperson said "85% of current incidents related to national security are involved with retired military and police", with no sample; the article does not reconcile this with the 66%. Four Democratic Progressive Party staff in government posts, including the foreign ministry, were charged.
- **Measures:** Prosecutions by the National Security Bureau, and an estimate with no stated basis. On my arithmetic the 5,000 is about 31 times the 159 indicted.
- **Sources:** Bill Sharp, Asia Times, 18 Aug 2025. https://asiatimes.com/2025/08/eyes-on-spies-countering-chinese-espionage-in-taiwan/ ; RFA, 24 Jan 2025. https://rfa.org/english/china/2025/01/24/china-taiwan-retired-military-security/
- **Directness:** Prosecution shares are direct, for one target. The 5,000 is an unverifiable claim. Retired staff are used to recruit serving staff, so "retired" does not mean irrelevant.

### 5. Canada: two official bodies, one set of evidence
- **Figure:** NSICOP (June 2024) described "an unknown number" of parliamentarians as "witting or semi-witting", including one alleged to have given confidential information to "a known intelligence officer of a foreign state"; others allegedly took cash or favours from diplomats. The Western Standard summarises NSICOP's number as "a few". The Hogue inquiry (Jan 2025) said: "Nor have I seen any evidence to suggest that there are currently so-called traitors in Parliament", and "the evidence does not show any MPs complying with foreign states against Canada's interest". She found interference "did not impact the outcome of the last two general elections", though it may have affected some ridings or nominations.
- **Measures:** Legislators in one G7 state, 2019 and 2021 elections. No counts or names.
- **Sources:** Global News, 3 June 2024. https://globalnews.ca/news/10541842/politicians-witting-participants-foreign-interference ; Western Standard, 28 Jan 2025 (a partisan outlet, used for its quotes of Hogue). https://www.westernstandard.news/amp/story/news/breaking-china-inquiry-found-no-evidence-of-traitors-despite-damning-nsicop-report/61621
- **Directness:** Direct in kind, no scale. It shows that even with classified access, officials disagree on whether this counts as infiltration.

### 6. Chinese espionage directed at the US
- **Figure:** 224 reported instances since 2000, through February 2023. 54% sought commercial technologies, 29% military technology, 17% information on civilian agencies or politicians (categories overlap). 49% involved Chinese military or government employees, 41% private Chinese citizens, 10% non-Chinese actors, "usually U.S. persons recruited by Chinese officials." 46% involved cyber espionage. "Derived from open-source material and likely does not reflect the full number of incidents."
- **Measures:** Public cases, one target. No share for insiders, and the 10% is not the same as an insider share.
- **Source:** "Survey of Chinese Espionage in the United States Since 2000", CSIS, 2023. https://www.csis.org/programs/strategic-technologies-program/survey-chinese-espionage-united-states-2000
- **Directness:** Close proxy on target mix. Mostly outside hacks and outsiders, which the definition here excludes, so true infiltration is a small part of the 224.

### 7. South Korea: technology leaks
- **Figure:** 179 technology leakage cases in 2025, up 45.5%. 33 involved attempted transfer out of Korea; China 18 of those (54.5%), Vietnam 4, the US and Indonesia 3 each. China's share of outbound cases: 50% in 2022, 68.1% in 2023, 74.1% in 2024. "Current or former employees were responsible for 148 of the 179 cases, or 82.7 percent." 378 individuals arrested as stated, six held in detention.
- **Measures:** Police-detected leaks in one country, 2025.
- **Source:** Korea JoongAng Daily, citing the National Office of Investigation. https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/china-linked-to-over-half-of-koreas-outbound-technology-leaks-last-year/12304361
- **Directness:** Weak to close proxy. The article does not say any leak was state-directed, and it mentions a Chinese company and job brokers, so these may belong under "companies and industries". 146 of 179 cases did not go abroad at all.

### 8. North Korean IT workers inside companies
- **Figure:** No sourced total. CrowdStrike: 304 incidents in 2024. In one Arizona case a woman with 60 stolen identities helped workers get jobs at 300 companies, per court documents, including Fortune 500 banks, an aerospace manufacturer and a car manufacturer. The UN estimates $250 million to $600 million a year since 2018; the US Treasury, State and FBI say "hundreds of millions each year since 2018". The article's phrase "thousands of software engineers" in the Fortune 500 is its own, not sourced.
- **Measures:** A state revenue scheme, using real jobs held under false identities, chiefly at US firms. One founder's claim that about 95% of his résumés are North Korean is an anecdote.
- **Source:** Fortune, 7 Apr 2025. https://www.fortune.com/2025/04/07/north-korean-it-workers-infiltrating-fortune-500-companies
- **Directness:** Close proxy. They are truly on payrolls, the clearest case of one government's people inside many companies. The aim is mainly salary, not steering decisions.

### 9. Iran recruiting in Israel
- **Figure:** At least 60 Israelis charged, "since 2023" or "since October 2023" (the article gives both), attributed to a CNN investigation of 11 May 2026. The Shin Bet's annual report says 25 Israelis and foreign residents were indicted in 2025. Examples: a 14-year-old paid about £900 in cryptocurrency; an IDF reservist asked to kill his commander; an Iron Dome reservist paid about £770; unnamed "active-duty soldiers and air force technicians". No breakdown by post.
- **Source:** IBTimes UK, 12 May 2026. https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/iran-espionage-network-telegram-spying-1796352
- **Directness:** Weak proxy. Many are paid outsiders hired for small tasks.

### 10. Pakistan-linked arrests in India
- **Figure:** 457 "Pakistan-linked spies or persons linked to them" arrested "since Operation Sindoor in May 2025", across 17 states and union territories. The body says 457 in 2025 and 17 more to March 2026. Profiled arrestees: a YouTuber, a factory security guard, a cleric. No category totals.
- **Source:** The Tribune, 9 May 2026. https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/top-headlines/457-spies-nabbed-since-operation-sindoor-punjab-police-flag-surge-in-isi-activities/
- **Directness:** Weak proxy. Arrests by police in a conflict period, with "linked to" in the definition.

### 11. Iran: arrests during the June 2025 war
- **Figure:** "as many as 21,000 'suspects'" arrested during the 12-day war, per a police spokesperson. He did not say what they were suspected of. Of the figures he gave, 261 were espionage suspects and 172 accused of unauthorised filming. Seven men executed for spying for Israel since the end of June. No figure on state posts.
- **Source:** Al Jazeera, 12 Aug 2025. https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/8/12/iran-says-it-arrested-21000-suspects-during-12-day-war-with-israel-us
- **Directness:** Weak proxy. On my arithmetic the espionage suspects are about 1.2% of the 21,000. A warning against reading mass-arrest counts as infiltration.

### 12. Russian sabotage in Europe: the outsider recruits
- **Figure:** 151 known cases, February 2022 to February 2026 (Poland 31, France 20, Lithuania 15, Germany 15, UK 12, Estonia 11). 172 perpetrators. About 95% were "ordinary citizens without any formal affiliation to Russian intelligence agencies". One case involved security service operatives and nine more involved people with ties to them. 44 (26%) had a criminal record. Money was the main motive.
- **Source:** "More of the same? Russia's crime-terror nexus: criminality as a tool of hybrid warfare revisited", ICCT, 2026. https://icct.nl/publication/more-same-russias-crime-terror-nexus-criminality-tool-hybrid-warfare-revisited
- **Directness:** Not a measure of infiltration. It is included to show that recent recruit counts are largely people outside any institution. No split for officials or soldiers.

### 13. Russian officers under diplomatic cover
- **Figure:** MI5's Ken McCallum said in November 2022 that "400 of the 600 Russians officials" expelled from Europe were judged to be spies. "Over 150" expelled in 2018. Swiss intelligence (2023): of about 220 accredited staff at Russian missions in Geneva and Bern, "probably at least a third work for the Russian intelligence services"; Switzerland has among the most such officers in Europe partly because it hosts international organisations, including the UN's European headquarters. Chinese spies at Chinese missions there: "dozens", and most Chinese agents use non-diplomatic cover.
- **Sources:** Wikipedia, "Diplomatic expulsions during the Russo-Ukrainian war". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_expulsions_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_war ; Al Jazeera, 27 June 2023. https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2023/6/27/switzerland-says-country-a-hotbed-for-russian-chinese-spies
- **Directness:** Weak proxy. These officers are outside the target institution. It sizes the handlers, not the people handled. The Swiss report implies interest in international bodies but counts no one inside them.

### 14. Foreign electoral interventions
- **Figure:** 938 competitive national executive elections in 148 countries, 1946-2000. The US intervened in 81, the USSR or Russia in 36, about 68% of them covert. The page says "about one in nine", though 117/938 is about 12.5%. An intervention for one side raised its vote share by about 3% on average.
- **Source:** Wikipedia, "Foreign electoral intervention", summarising Dov Levin. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_electoral_intervention
- **Directness:** Close proxy. It is the only global denominator found here: a real share of national elections. But it covers two powers only, and much intervention is campaign money and not a person placed inside an institution.

### 15. US covert regime change
- **Figure:** "ten times more covert than overt regime changes — 64 covert versus 6 overt", during the Cold War, from archival research. Success rates are not on the page.
- **Source:** Lindsey O'Rourke, *Covert Regime Change*, author's page. https://sites.google.com/site/lindseyorourke/Manuscript
- **Directness:** Weak proxy. Coups and aid to dissidents go beyond infiltration, and a count of operations says little about people inside.

### 16. Russian money to foreign politicians
- **Figure:** A State Department cable alleges Russia "has covertly spent more than $300 million since 2014" on politicians and officials in "more than two dozen countries", through front organisations including European think tanks and state firms in Central America, Asia, the Middle East and North Africa. The cable does not explain the total or name recipients. A senior official cited Albania, Bosnia and Montenegro. On my arithmetic this is about $37 million a year.
- **Source:** AP via News4Jax, 13 Sept 2022. https://www.news4jax.com/news/politics/2022/09/13/us-russia-spent-300m-to-covertly-influence-world-politics/
- **Directness:** Direct for money, but a rival government's unevidenced claim. The sum is small beside the corporate and criminal money other parts may report.

### 17. Payments to foreign heads of state and officials
- **Figure:** The CIA paid Karzai's office "tens of millions of dollars" in cash over more than a decade (citing the New York Times, 2013). It reportedly bankrolled members of Afghanistan's National Security Council. Cash payments to heads of state from Angola to Zaire (citing the New York Times, 1977). Bribery of foreign officials is called "standard practice" and "longstanding and global", with no count.
- **Source:** Moran (2014) and Eubank (2019), Global Anticorruption Blog. https://globalanticorruptionblog.com/tag/state-sponsored-bribery/
- **Directness:** Direct in kind, no scale, and second-hand: the blog relays press reports.

### 18. EU Parliament cash scandal ("Qatargate")
- **Figure:** €1.5 million in cash seized. Four initially charged, three more arrested, and MEPs including Tarabella, Cozzolino and Arena charged. States named: Qatar, Morocco, Mauritania. Allegations include killing six resolutions on Qatar's human rights record and advancing visa-free travel. Panzeri took a plea deal and Giorgi confessed. As of 2025 undecided, with no convictions reported, the judge stepped down and critics doubt the investigation.
- **Source:** Mirror of Wikipedia, "Qatar corruption scandal at the European Parliament", March 2026 snapshot. https://wiki.krisyotam.com/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic_2026-03/Qatargate_corruption_scandal
- **Directness:** Direct, a single case, and the only one read here inside an international body where specific decisions were allegedly steered.

### 19. Cuba inside Venezuela's military intelligence
- **Figure:** Two agreements signed 26 May 2008. Cuba would oversee restructuring of the Directorate of Military Intelligence; groups of up to 40 officers trained in Havana for up to three months; a committee of eight Cuban "military experts" inspected units. Cuban instructors reportedly told agents their mission was to infiltrate and control the military. The service grew from a few hundred agents to at least 1,500. At least 200 military officials detained, per the National Assembly. No total for Cubans.
- **Source:** Angus Berwick, "How Cuba taught Venezuela to quash military dissent", Reuters, 22 Aug 2019 (PDF copy). https://profmex.org/cronicasinfin/noticias/Special_Report-How_Cuba_taught_Venezuela_to_quash_military_dissent.pdf
- **Directness:** Direct in kind but by government agreement, so it may not count as infiltration. A small number of foreigners reshaped a whole service, which is why headcounts can mislead.

### 20. Russian forces in African states
- **Figure:** Central African Republic: about 175 instructors in late 2017, about 2,100 reported in 2021, about 1,500 in early 2026, reportedly protecting and advising the president. Mali: as many as 2,500 in early 2026, with a late-2021 deal reportedly costing $10 million a month. Equatorial Guinea: trained presidential guard forces reportedly. Smaller presence reported in Niger, Burkina Faso, Sudan and Libya. Over 40 African countries reportedly have military cooperation agreements with Russia, most without openly partnering with Wagner or Africa Corps.
- **Source:** *Russia's Security Operations in Africa*, Congressional Research Service IF12389, updated 8 Apr 2026. https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF12389.html
- **Directness:** Weak proxy. Largely invited and paid-for deployments, so often not infiltration. CAR's presidential protection is the closest to the definition.

## Reading

No source counts foreign-state infiltration across the world, and the only true global denominator read is Levin's: the US and USSR or Russia intervened in 117 of 938 national executive elections from 1946 to 2000, which is mostly money and not people inside.

Large recent numbers shrink when you ask who was inside. Iran's 21,000 contains 261 espionage suspects, the European sabotage recruits are about 95% ordinary citizens, India's 457 and Israel's 60 include teenagers and hired civilians, and Ukraine's 3,200 cannot be split because the SBU does not track occupation.

The smaller counts are the ones that show true insiders: 28 serving soldiers prosecuted in Taiwan in 2024, 651 proceedings in Ukrainian law enforcement in 2022, and 148 US offenders in about 50 years, 62% of them volunteers.

The strongest single measure is old: 3,000 to 3,500 agents in West Germany in the Stasi's final years, spread across science, ministries, parties and the military. It shows what one service could do against one neighbour, and it became known only because that state collapsed and its files opened, so caught-case counts probably understate living services by a large and unknown factor.

Evidence on targets suggests technology and companies draw the most agents by number (Stasi 39% in science and technology, CSIS 54% commercial, North Korean workers in hundreds of firms), then military and intelligence, then ministries and parties. Heads of state and international bodies are few in number but high in effect, as with Guillaume, Karzai's office and Qatargate.

Guess: foreign states are a minority of all infiltration worldwide by number of people or institutions, perhaps 10 to 20%, because they focus on rivals and secrets while crime and companies work through very many local offices. This range is judgement and is not calculated from these figures.

Guess: their share is much higher inside military and intelligence bodies, and may be the largest there outside places of heavy criminal capture of police and customs. It is also relatively high among heads of state, because intervention, bribery and adviser arrangements aim at the top.

Evidence cutting against foreign states mattering most: volunteers outnumber planted agents in the US data, Korean leaks show no stated state direction, and Canada's public inquiry found no MPs acting against the national interest.

I am moderately sure of the direction, that counts overstate insiders and that the effort concentrates on a few rival targets, and not sure of any share. Missing: agents per thousand officials anywhere, breakdowns by post for the big recent counts, a count of foreign-placed staff in any international body, and all uncaught cases.
