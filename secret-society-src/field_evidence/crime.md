Second pass. This replaces the headline-only file. Everything below was read through WebFetch, which returns a summary of the page, not the page itself. Figures should be checked against the source before publication. Calls used this pass: 25 WebFetch, 4 WebSearch.

## Global

### 1. Global Organized Crime Index 2025: criminal actor scores
- **Figure:** Global averages on a 0-10 scale: state-embedded actors 6.04; foreign actors 5.68 (+0.14 since 2023, +0.40 since 2021); criminal networks 5.67; private sector actors 4.89 (+0.13 since 2023); mafia-style groups 3.95. Private sector actors by region: Western Asia 6.43 (highest), Central America 6.19, North America 6.06, South-eastern Asia 5.82, Southern Europe 5.35, Polynesia 1.17 (lowest).
- **Measures:** Scores for how much each actor type shapes crime, 193 countries, 2025. These are scores, not counts. The method was not in what I read.
- **Source:** Global Organized Crime Index 2025: Crime at a Crossroads, Global Initiative Against Transnational Organized Crime (GI-TOC), November 2025. https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Global-Organized-Crime-Index-2025-Crime-at-a-Crossroads-GI-TOC-November-2025.pdf
- **Directness:** Close proxy, and the only worldwide comparison found. It cuts against the obvious answer: crime from inside the state scores higher than mafia-style groups. A state-embedded actor is an official committing crime, so no outside party needs to have got in. The score cannot separate the two.

### 2. Global Organized Crime Index 2023: state-embedded actors
- **Figure:** Global average 5.95 out of 10, up 0.20 from the 2021 Index. 55 of 193 countries (about 28%) score 8 or higher. Of the most affected countries, 54% are authoritarian regimes under the Economist Intelligence Unit's 2022 Democracy Index. Described as "criminal actors that are part of or act from within the state apparatus".
- **Measures:** Same scoring, 193 countries, 2023.
- **Source:** GI-TOC analysis of state-embedded actors, posted 27 May 2024. https://globalinitiative.net/analysis/state-embedded-actors-criminality-ocindex/
- **Directness:** Close proxy, with the same limit as finding 1.

### 3. Global Corruption Barometer 2017
- **Figure:** Police and elected officials tied for the highest share describing them as "highly corrupt", at 36%. By region: Sub-Saharan Africa police 47%; the Americas police and elected representatives 46% each; Middle East and North Africa elected representatives, tax officials and government officials 45%; Asia Pacific police 39%; Europe and Central Asia elected representatives 31%. "Nearly one in four" paid a bribe for a public service in the past 12 months.
- **Measures:** Public perception and reported bribery. 162,136 adults in 119 countries, territories and regions, March 2014 to January 2017.
- **Source:** Global Corruption Barometer 2017, Transparency International. https://www.transparency.org/en/gcb/global/global-corruption-barometer-2017/results/analysis
- **Directness:** Weak proxy. This measures corruption in general, not ties to crime groups. It does show that police and elected officials are the most suspected worldwide.

## Citizen surveys

### 4. Afrobarometer: police and criminal activity
- **Figure:** 46% say "most" or "all" police are corrupt, the worst rating of 11 institutions and leaders. 29% say police "often" or "always" engage in criminal activities, and another 27% say "sometimes". Highest Lesotho 58%, lowest Morocco and Tanzania 11% each. 36% of those who asked police for help were asked for a bribe, gift or favour (Liberia 78%, Cabo Verde 2%). 37% of those who met police in other situations were asked to pay to avoid a problem (Liberia 70%, Cabo Verde 1%). Police are seen as the most corrupt actor in 19 of 39 countries.
- **Measures:** Round 9, 39 African countries, 53,444 interviews, fieldwork 2021/2023.
- **Source:** Afrobarometer Pan-Africa Profile PP90, January 2024. https://www.afrobarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/PP90-PAP6-Africans-cite-corruption-and-lack-of-professionalism-among-police-failings-Afrobarometer-26jan24.pdf
- **Directness:** Close proxy. The 29% is the nearest citizen measure found, but police committing crime is not the same as police working for a crime group.

### 5. AmericasBarometer: Caribbean bribe requests
- **Figure:** "Has a police officer asked you for a bribe in the last twelve months?" Dominican Republic 17.4%, Bahamas 10.8%, Guyana 9.5%, Haiti 6.8%, Jamaica 5.1%, Trinidad and Tobago 3.8%, Suriname 2.9%, Dominica 2.2%, Antigua and Barbuda 2.0%, St. Lucia 1.3%, St. Vincent and the Grenadines 1.3%, Barbados 1.2%, St. Kitts and Nevis 1.2%, Grenada 0.4%.
- **Measures:** 2016/17. Samples listed include Dominican Republic 1,518, Jamaica 1,515 and Guyana 1,576.
- **Source:** Caribbean Briefing on Selected AmericasBarometer Results, LAPOP/Vanderbilt, October 2017. https://www.vanderbilt.edu/lapop/CaribbeanBriefingonSelectedAmericasBarometerResultsOct2017.pdf
- **Directness:** Weak proxy. This is a bribe request, not a crime tie. No AmericasBarometer question on police working with criminals was read. An archived LAPOP report was blocked by a bot check.

### 6. Global Corruption Barometer 2013: Latin America
- **Figure:** Bribes to police, as reported by InSight Crime: Mexico 61%, Venezuela 57%, Bolivia 52%. The summary describes these as shares of those who paid a bribe. Overall bribe-paying in the past 12 months across eight services: Bolivia 36%, Mexico 33%. Brazil: 60% distrust police.
- **Measures:** 2013. Sample not given in the article. The article does not say the survey asked about police working with criminals.
- **Source:** "Survey Highlights Latin America Police Corruption", InSight Crime. https://insightcrime.org/news/brief/survey-highlights-latin-america-police-corruption/
- **Directness:** Weak proxy, and second-hand.

### 7. Western Balkans: POINTPULSE
- **Figure:** "Over 90%" believe their police are corrupt and about 5% see them as uncorrupt. Serbia only: 47% believe police take bribes from criminals; 23% believe regular citizens bribe police the most; 81% believe police work in the interest of ruling parties; 6% think internal control is eradicating police corruption.
- **Measures:** Perception survey in Serbia, Bosnia, Kosovo and Montenegro. Serbia sample 1,205 adults; others not given. Dated 23 July 2015. The 47% and 23% seem to answer "who bribes police most", so 47% may not be a stand-alone share. I could not confirm the question wording.
- **Source:** "Balkan Countries See Police as Corrupt, Report Says", on the Regional Anti-corruption Initiative archive, reporting POINTPULSE. https://archive.rai-see.org/?p=3949
- **Directness:** Close proxy for Serbia's 47%; weak for the regional figure. Second-hand and old.

## Companies

### 8. Europol: 821 networks
- **Figure:** 821 criminal networks identified as the highest threat to EU internal security. 86% "can extensively infiltrate and misuse legal business structures". 71% engage in corruption to facilitate crime or obstruct law enforcement or judicial proceedings. 76% are present in two to seven countries. 68% have members of several nationalities. 34% have been active for over 10 years.
- **Measures:** Europol's selected top-threat networks, EU, 2024. Not all networks, and not a share of companies or officials.
- **Source:** "Key Features of Most Threatening Criminal Networks in the EU", eucrim, reporting Europol's "Decoding the EU's most threatening criminal networks". https://eucrim.eu/news/key-features-of-most-threatening-criminal-networks-in-the-eu/
- **Directness:** Close proxy. It is strong evidence that the method is common among serious networks, but the selection leans toward the most capable. It gives no count of infiltrated bodies.

### 9. Europol: legal business structures
- **Figure:** "86% of the EU's most threatening criminal networks abuse LBS". The article says abuse spans "all business sectors" and runs from fully criminal firms to legitimate companies that unknowingly facilitate crime. The greatest threat named is high-level infiltration or criminal ownership.
- **Measures:** Follow-up on the same 821 networks, published 18 December 2024. No split between infiltrating and creating a company was given.
- **Source:** "Abuse of Legal Business Structures through Criminal Networks", eucrim, 4 February 2025. https://eucrim.eu/news/abuse-of-legal-business-structures-through-criminal-networks/
- **Directness:** Close proxy. The 86% includes firms that criminals created, so it overstates infiltration of existing companies.

### 10. Italy: 2% of firms, about a tenth of jobs
- **Figure:** "About 2% of Italian firms appear to have links with OCGs": 106,122 firms, 2005-2020. They hold 8-10% of private-sector employment (excluding sole proprietorships) and 10-14% of revenues among firms in the Cerved database. About two-thirds are in the Center-North, though incidence is highest in Calabrian provinces. Waste collection, treatment and disposal has the highest incidence, 11.5%. About 51% were born infiltrated and 49% born clean; born-clean firms hold about 85% of assets. These firms are about 30 times more likely to have been confiscated than others.
- **Measures:** A firm counts if at least one owner or director is on a confidential list of people linked to organised crime. The authors call these firms "potentially" connected. Covers corporations and partnerships.
- **Source:** Arellano-Bover, De Simoni, Guiso, Macchiavello, Marchetti and Prem, "Mafias and Firms", January 2025 (authors at Yale, UIF/Bank of Italy, EIEF, LSE). https://bahamasamlconference.centralbankbahamas.com/documents/2025-02-10-16-58-51-Session-1---Mafia-and-Firms-ECMCA-D.-Marchetti-etal.pdf
- **Directness:** Direct, and the best measure found, for one country with an unusually strong mafia presence and unusually good data. It cannot be scaled to the world.

### 11. Lombardy: 11.9% of firms in a sample
- **Figure:** 1,840 connected firms, 11.9% of the sample, or "more than one business in ten". Connected firms show 15.6% lower return on assets and a 25.5% higher chance of default, and use more bank loans at much lower interest rates.
- **Measures:** Firms in Lombardy, using intelligence-service (AISI) investigation data, 2006-2013. A firm counts if a director or shareholder was under investigation for offences that include false invoices, usury and smuggling. Lombardy produces about 25% of Italian GDP.
- **Source:** "Identikit: shakier than you thought", Bocconi University, 20 July 2021, on Bianchi, Marra, Masciandaro and Pecchiari, The Accounting Review. https://www.unibocconi.it/en/news/identikit-shakier-you-thought-firms-connected-mafia
- **Directness:** Close proxy. The definition is wider than in finding 10, and the sample's selection is unknown, so the two shares should not be compared directly.

### 12. Italy: anti-mafia confiscations
- **Figure:** More than 30,000 assets confiscated over 30 years, in over 400 municipalities, with over 80,000 firms affected. Organised crime owns about 5,000 restaurants and cafés. After enforcement, revenues fall about 4.2%.
- **Measures:** Orbis data on 834,016 Italian firms, 1995-2015. "Affected" means located where enforcement took place, not infiltrated.
- **Source:** Slutzky and Zeume, "Organised Crime and Firms: Evidence from Italy", ABFER conference paper, undated on the page. https://abfer.org/media/abfer-events-2019/annual-conference/corporate-finance/29-D2-Organised-Crime-and-Firms---Evidence-from-Italy.pdf
- **Directness:** Weak proxy, except the 5,000 figure, which is direct but narrow.

## Government bodies and officials

### 13. Italy: dissolved local authorities
- **Figure:** 402 dissolutions for mafia infiltration, 2 August 1991 to 30 September 2025, affecting 294 bodies (288 municipalities, 6 provincial health authorities). Calabria, Campania and Sicily account for 89% (360); with Puglia 96% (386). 83 bodies were dissolved two or more times.
- **Measures:** An official act, based on Avviso Pubblico's dossier "Il male in Comune". The article gives no national total of municipalities.
- **Source:** Il Sole 24 Ore, 2 December 2025. https://en.ilsole24ore.com/art/in-the-last-34-years-municipality-month-has-been-dissolved-by-the-mafia-AIuG3p
- **Directness:** Direct, for one country and only where the state acted.

### 14. Italy: the count depends on government discretion
- **Figure:** As of July 2019, 328 measures on 249 municipalities since 1991, with 26 dissolutions overturned by administrative judges. Calabria 115, Campania 108, Sicily 79, Puglia 15. The report finds a high degree of arbitrariness, shown by differences between governments, and a historian quoted argues the state is harsher in the South and gentler in the Centre-North. The headline says "18 years", which does not fit the 1991 start in the text.
- **Source:** Il Fatto Quotidiano, 4 July 2019, on "Lo scioglimento dei Comuni per mafia. Analisi e proposte" (Avviso Pubblico). https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2019/07/04/comuni-sciolti-per-mafia-lo-studio-sono-249-in-18-anni-il-record-in-calabria-ma-troppo-arbitrio-al-governo/5299244/
- **Directness:** Direct, and it cuts both ways: some dissolutions may be wrong, and the North may be undercounted.

### 15. Colombia: Congress
- **Figure:** The Supreme Court "has convicted 45 congressmen" for paramilitary ties since 2006. An earlier count: 38 former congressmen convicted and 140 lawmakers under criminal investigation (July 2012). A former leader of the AUC paramilitaries claimed more than 35% of Congress were friends of the paramilitaries; this is an allegation, not a finding.
- **Source:** Colombia Reports, date not shown. https://colombiareports.com/colombia-congress-members-convicted-parapolitics/amp
- **Directness:** Direct as a count. No size of Congress was read, so no share of it can be stated. The paramilitaries are also armed political actors, so this may belong partly under "political and armed movements".

### 16. Colombia: presidents of Congress
- **Figure:** 11 of 13 presidents of Congress, 1999-2000 to 2011-2012: 4 convicted, 7 under Supreme Court investigation. One was absolved and one never charged.
- **Source:** Colombia Reports, 25 July 2012, citing the Electoral Observation Mission (MOE). https://colombiareports.com/11-of-13-former-congress-presidents-investigated-or-convicted-over-ties-to-paramilitaries/
- **Directness:** Direct, for a very small group. The convicted share is 4 of 13; 7 were only under investigation.

### 17. Colombia: everyone named
- **Figure:** 11,179 people: 943 politicians, 870 military members, 330 public servants, 9,036 civilians, including some business owners.
- **Measures:** Compiled by Verdad Abierta from the Prosecutor General's Office: people paramilitaries mentioned in trials or investigations, early 2005 to April 2012. These are mentions and requests for investigation, not charges.
- **Source:** Colombia Reports, 7 June 2012. https://colombiareports.com/11179-colombian-politicians-officials-businessmen-involved-in-parapolitics-scandal-report/
- **Directness:** Close proxy. It is the only source found that splits one country's named people by institution, but it rests on the word of paramilitaries.

### 18. Mexico: the 71.5% figure and its critique
- **Figure:** Edgardo Buscaglia (ITAM) said 71.5% of municipalities are "under criminal control", with 34% in 2001, 53% in 2006 and 73% in 2010. His team observed crime carried out openly and took this as a sign of local government support. InSight Crime says there is "a great deal of distance" between corruption and a gang's total control of a city, and that the method cannot see hidden activity such as smuggling.
- **Source:** "Do Gangs Control 70% of Mexico?", InSight Crime, 3 January 2012. https://insightcrime.org/news/analysis/do-gangs-control-70-of-mexico/
- **Directness:** Weak proxy. It should not be cited as a measured share.

### 19. Mexico: attacks on mayors
- **Figure:** 163 assassination attempts in 138 municipalities, 2002-2021: 85 successful and 69 failed by the paper's breakdown (the other figures were not explained in what I read). 2,471 municipalities in total, so about 5.6% had an attempt (a calculation from those figures, not stated in the paper). Mayors are at least 9 times more likely to be murdered than the general population. Attempts are tied to criminal-group presence and competition, not to homicide rates.
- **Source:** Seung-hun Lee, "Organized Crime, Local Politicians, and State Capacity", 24 February 2026. https://seunghunlee918.github.io/research/Mexico_Mayors_260224.pdf
- **Directness:** Weak proxy. A killing shows pressure on mayors, and may show a failure of infiltration, not its presence.

### 20. Brazil: Rio militias
- **Figure:** Militias control 57.5% of the territory of Rio city (686.75 km²) and dominate 25.5% of neighbourhoods. About 2.2 million people, given as 12.7% of the population, live in militia areas. Comando Vermelho 11.4%, Terceiro Comando 3.7%, ADA 0.3% (whether of area or neighbourhoods is unclear). About a quarter of the territory was contested. The 2.2 million and 12.7% imply a base of about 17 million, which looks too large for a city, so that share is doubtful.
- **Measures:** Mapa dos Grupos Armados do Rio de Janeiro, 2019 data, from UFF, Fogo Cruzado, USP's NEV, Pista News and Disque-Denúncia. The article does not say police or officials are in the militias.
- **Source:** Correio Braziliense, 19 October 2020. https://www.correiobraziliense.com.br/brasil/2020/10/4883182-milicias-controlam-57--do-territorio-do-rio-de-janeiro-informa-estudo.html
- **Directness:** Weak proxy. Territory is not institutions, and most of the area is not where most people live.

### 21. Honduras: police purge
- **Figure:** "More than 5,000" police employees had left the force through the purge by October 2018, and about 2,000 more were under investigation. Some dismissed officers stayed on active duty, one kept receiving pay, police investigated themselves, and the Attorney General's Office secured few convictions.
- **Measures:** Purge from mid-2016. Grounds for the whole purge and the force's size were not read. Alleged drug-trafficking links are cited in individual cases.
- **Source:** "Arrests in Honduras Police Reveal Setbacks in Purge", InSight Crime, 15 October 2018. https://insightcrime.org/news/arrests-honduras-police-reveal-setbacks-purge/
- **Directness:** Close proxy. It is the largest count of people found in this part, but not all removals are for crime ties.

### 22. Africa and the Sahel: statements without figures
- **Finding:** The Africa Organised Crime Index 2025, as reported, finds state-embedded actors "the most prevalent criminal actors"; the page cut off after the introduction, so no country scores were read. UNODC's Sahel report, as reported, cites "widespread corruption, including by members of political elites" and calls corruption and money laundering "major enablers". No count of officials appears.
- **Sources:** The East African, 19 November 2025: https://www.theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/news/east-africa/how-embedded-state-actors-promote-organised-crime-in-africa-5269372 . North Africa Post, 8 May 2024: https://northafricapost.com/?p=77155
- **Directness:** Weak proxy. Both are second-hand and give no scale. I read no UNODC report directly.

## Reading

1. The best direct measure is Italian: about 2% of firms, holding 8-10% of private-sector jobs, potentially tied to organised crime, and two-thirds of them outside the South. It comes from one country with an unusually strong mafia presence and unusually good data.
2. Counted by bodies, companies far outnumber government bodies in Italy: 106,122 firms against 294 local bodies. The firm list is loose and the dissolution bar is high, so the gap overstates the difference.
3. Counted by people inside the state, police look largest: Honduras has more than 5,000 departures, Colombia names 870 military members, and police rank as the most suspected institution in Afrobarometer and the Global Corruption Barometer. Colombia also names 943 politicians, so elected office is not far behind where that record exists.
4. Direct evidence that crime reaches top national office is rare, but Colombia's Congress shows it can happen: 4 of 13 congressional presidents were convicted and 7 were investigated.
5. The widest global measure cuts against a simple picture of outsiders getting in: GI-TOC scores state-embedded actors (6.04) well above mafia-style groups (3.95), and much of that crime may start inside the state with no outside party.
6. My guess, within organised crime's own infiltration, counting entities and people: companies about half, police, customs, prisons and military about a quarter, local office and parties about 15%, with judges and prosecutors, ministers, heads of government, central banks and international bodies sharing the rest.
7. My guess for organised crime's share of all infiltration worldwide is 20-35%, large in low-level numbers and small in control of national decisions. This is judgement, not arithmetic: nothing I read compares it with other outside parties.
8. Confidence is low on both guesses, and enforcement bias runs through everything: Italy and Colombia look large because they investigate and publish, Italian dissolutions are discretionary, and Colombian names come from paramilitaries.
9. Survey figures mostly measure corruption or bribery, so they set an outer limit and are not a share tied to crime groups. Afrobarometer's 29% is the nearest citizen measure, and I found no survey that asks directly about working with crime groups.
10. Missing: any share of officials tied to crime in most countries, denominators for Colombia and Honduras, numbers for West Africa and the Balkans beyond perception, GI-TOC's regional state-embedded scores, Transcrime's figures, firm-level data outside Italy, and anything on India, the Middle East or Central Asia.
