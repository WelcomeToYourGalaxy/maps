## Candidate sentences, by kind

Each is a grep hit, not a finding. Verify every one against the document named before using it, and read the whole paragraph it sits in — selective quotation is the fault that has decided more drops on this map than any other.

**Where this extraction is known to be thin, from round 31's writers.** Four of ten cases in one batch turned on a passage no candidate sentence surfaced, and the misses ran to a pattern: text inside footnotes, compensation-status tables dated after the closing date, counts sitting in a table's row label rather than in a sentence, and the borrower's own annex to the completion report, which sometimes contradicts the Bank's main text. Patterns for the first three were added after that round; the borrower's annex is flagged but not parsed. **Read the completion report's footnotes and its borrower annex yourself.**

## What to decide

Apply the forward rule in the handoff, item 9 of "What is actually open": a case needs either (a) a document counting people or households actually **relocated**, or (b) a specific documented harm to identifiable people — deaths with a stated cause, injuries, arrests, land taken, compensation unpaid or below replacement cost, livelihoods not restored. Neither limb is a drop, and a card whose entire content is a count of people "affected" is a drop. Where a document records housing actually demolished, in a table with a separate enterprise column, limb (b) is met without a household count.
