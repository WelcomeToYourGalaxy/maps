# Fact-check of r26f_people_totals_2.json

Checked 11 November 2026. All 30 non-empty entries were checked against the document each cites; 15 of the 125 empty entries were spot-checked. Output: `r26f_people_totals_2_checked.json` (155 keys, 28 non-empty).

## What the World Bank projects API actually counts

The API answers agents: `https://search.worldbank.org/api/v2/projects?format=json&rows=0` returns `{"total":"22729"}` unfiltered, matching the writer's figure. All ten dated queries were re-run and all ten returned exactly the number in the entry (355, 1,220, 1,056, 656, 1,848, 1,502, 576, 858, 363, 839).

**The date filter is on board approval date.** Checked by a method of my own rather than by comparing range sizes:
- A one-day window (`strdate=enddate=1993-04-27`) returns 4 rows and every row's `boardapprovaldate` is exactly `1993-04-27T00:00:00Z`; rows in a wider window come back ordered by that field and none falls outside the window.
- Cross-checked against a different field: `approvalfy=1993` returns 149, and the fiscal-year window 1992-07-01 to 1993-06-30 returns 150. A filter on closing, signing or last-update date could not line up with approval fiscal year like that.

**What a row is.** Facets on `prodlinetext` show the count is not IBRD/IDA only. For Kim's window the 1,848 breaks down as 1,527 IBRD/IDA, 232 recipient-executed (trust-fund) activities, 35 guarantees, 25 GEF, 14 GEF medium-sized, 6 carbon offset, 6 special financing, 2 Montreal Protocol, 1 debt reduction facility. Older windows are almost pure IBRD/IDA (Black and Clausen 100 per cent; Conable 1,054 of 1,056); recent windows are mostly so (Malpass 1,469 of 1,502; Banga 571 of 576); the trust-fund share is largest under Kim (13 per cent) and Georgieva (6 per cent). IFC and MIGA are not in this database at all.

**Dropped and pipeline projects are excluded by the date filter.** The status facet over all 22,729 rows gives 14,602 Closed, 2,666 Active, 1,909 Dropped, 700 Pipeline; inside Kim's window there are no Dropped and no Pipeline rows (Closed 1,477, Active 369), because a project that never reached the board has no board approval date. So a date-filtered row is a board-approved operation. One row is one operation — additional financings carry their own project ID and count separately, which is correct for "approvals" but means the count is of operations, not of distinct physical projects.

Only Kim's sentence needed rewriting for this; the others are within a point or two of being pure IBRD/IDA lending, and every note now carries the breakdown.

## Weakest figures let stand
1. **warren-anderson** - 98,000 payroll and about 700 sites, both from secondary compilations because the 1984 annual report is not online; the 700 figure is dated 1985.
2. **keshub-mahindra / vijay-gokhale** - 14 plants, from an uncited management case study, and the source attributes them to the parent rather than to the Indian company the two men ran.
3. **ian-lundin / alex-schneiter** - the sevens are this map's counts of lists in a trade news item, and the lists describe the assets a buyer would acquire, not an audited inventory.
4. **christian-herrault** - the 2012 Lafarge figures are not matched to his term, because no source opened gives his years in the post.
5. **brian-anderson-shell** - 1997-1999 figures attached to a 1995 post, and they describe a joint venture in which SPDC held 30 per cent.
6. **augusto-jimenez-mejia** - 2019 figures for a man whose years in the post the map's own sources put as far apart as 1989-2019 and 1990-2012.
7. **miguel-facusse** - no quantity at all; it describes a company rather than measuring it.
8. **fabio-schvartsman** - figures right, but read from a third-party mirror of the SEC filing rather than from SEC's own page.
