## aswan-high-dam-nubian-displacement — CORRECTED, entry survives

Opened: the full translated text of the agreement at
humanities-collections.exeter.ac.uk/dame/files/original/a88c0b5a5fe98fdf358b2f4f46d831b9ad1248d6.pdf
(Russian State Archive of the Economy f.7590 op.17 d.249, with the 9 January 1959 covering letter
to the Soviet State Bank). Articles 1-12 read in full.

Confirmed in the agreement's own words:
- Article 5: the credit is "a loan in the amount of up to 400 million rubles" and covers only
  Soviet organisations' design, survey and research work, machinery, equipment and materials
  "determined on the basis of FOB prices to Soviet ports", and the travel of Soviet specialists.
  Article 1 adds that all Soviet expenses "will be made within the limits of the amount of the
  credit". `tied` holds.
- Article 3: "The U.A.R. Government shall establish a special organization for the administrative
  and technical management and financing of construction." `admin` holds.
- Article 5: any excess over 400 million roubles "must be paid by the Government of the U.A.R. by
  supplying U.A.R. goods to the U.S.S.R." `fincov` holds. This sits close to failure mode 6, but it
  is an obligation in the binding agreement with a specified mechanism, not a financing-plan line
  in an appraisal.
- Article 7: repayments and interest are credited in Egyptian pounds "to a separate account to be
  opened by the Central Bank of the U.A.R. of the Egyptian Region in the name of the State Bank of
  the U.S.S.R.", and the balance is spent on Egyptian goods or converted to sterling. `repay` holds.

Cut:
- The interest rate of 2.5 percent a year and the twelve equal annual instalments beginning a year
  after the first stage was finished and the reservoir filled. All of it is in Article 6 and all of
  it is true, but it is pricing and tenor, not a requirement (failure mode 7).
- "Articles 7 and 9" merged two different accounts. Article 9's payments for keeping and moving
  Soviet specialists go to the "general account", not to the separate repayment account of
  Article 7. The entry now names both correctly.
- "400 million rouble credit" corrected to "up to 400 million roubles"; "Egyptian goods" corrected
  to "U.A.R. goods", which is the text.

No date problem: the agreement is of 27 December 1958 and the flooding of Nubia followed.

## thilawa-sez-resettlement — BLANKED

Opened: JICA's ex-ante evaluation for private sector investment finance 7150,
www2.jica.go.jp/en/evaluation/pdf/2014_7150_1_f.pdf, read in full as text.

What the document actually says, in section 7(i)f: "The Project involves involuntary relocations of
81 households ... but an agreement was reached with all households in December 2013 regarding the
details of compensation and support relating to the relocations. The relocations will be carried
out in accordance with the Resettlement Work Plan prepared based on the JICA environmental
guidelines." Section 7(i)g: "Based on an environmental monitoring plan and the monitoring form
accepted at the time of screening, monitoring of air quality, water quality, noise, vibration, etc.
will be performed by a contractor during construction and by MJTD once each lot is ready to use."

Grounds for blanking:
- **Failure mode 1.** The guidelines named are JICA's general Guidelines for Environmental and
  Social Considerations of April 2010, which section 7(i)b cites as the reason for the Category A
  rating. A plan "prepared based on" them is a plan built to a lender policy; nothing in the
  document says the investment depended on it. The writer's second flagged point was right.
- **Failure mode 3.** "must monitor following the monitoring plan accepted at screening" is the
  writer's obligation, not the document's. The document says who will do the monitoring, in the
  future tense, and imposes nothing. The words required, must and condition do not appear anywhere
  in the document; "A request will be made to the Myanmar government" is as close as it comes, and
  that is about infrastructure delivery.
- **Failure mode 5.** Compensation and support were agreed with all 81 households in December 2013
  and the first moves were made in 2013; the agreement is dated 23 April 2014 and the project
  schedule is "To be started in 2015". So even a real term of the 2014 agreement could not bind the
  2013 displacement that the case records. The writer's own note tried to save this by limiting the
  claim to relocation "carried out from 2014 on" — but the case's harm is the 2013 eviction and the
  cash-for-farmland compensation settled in December 2013.

The resettlement was carried out by the Yangon Region Government (failure mode 2 territory as
well). No JICA investment agreement for the Class A Area is published.

## hambantota-port-zone-clashes — CORRECTED, reduced entry survives

Opened: the SAIS-CARI briefing paper (sais-cari.org/s/Briefing-Paper-Sri-Lanka-Debt-V5-ewjg.pdf,
302 to static1.squarespace.com) and the special appendix
(sais-cari.org/s/Special-Appendix-Documents-Accessed-V3.pdf). Both read as full text.

Kept, with its words checked:
- Loan-agreement nuance 1: "All five loans were signed by the Government of Sri Lanka (GoSL) as the
  borrower (as represented by the Ministry of Finance and Planning, with the SLPA as the
  end-user)." This is a term of the agreements and is repayment security: `repay` holds.
- Nuance 2: "All five loan agreements refer to contracts signed between SLPA and a Chinese supplier
  or contractors responsible for constructing the port, signed months ahead of the loan agreement."
  Kept as fact. Not typed `tied`: the paper does not say the loan required a Chinese contractor,
  and it hedges the reason ("possibly due to the use of unsolicited bids").
- The negative finding: "Of the loan agreements signed between ChEXIM and GoSL for the port, none
  had a clause specifying the port as collateral to the loans provided in case of an inability to
  service the loans. Event of default clauses in the four Hambantota port loan agreements do not
  refer to the port as collateral ... There is no reference to a lien or mortgage on the port."

On the writer's third flagged point, ruled in two parts:
- **The 85 and 93 percent figures are cut.** The paper's note 3 says the 2007 Phase 1 and 2009
  bunkering loans "covered only 85 percent of total project costs specified in project contracts
  with Chinese contractors. The remaining 15 percent was to be financed by Sri Lanka", and that the
  two Phase 2 loans "only covered about 93 percent total project cost". That is a share of project
  cost with the borrower paying the rest: a financing plan (failure mode 6) and an ordinary
  export-credit cover ratio (failure mode 7). The entry's inference "so the money could only be
  drawn against those contracts" is nowhere in the paper. Three further errors in the figures: the
  85 percent is of total project costs, not of the CHEC-SCL contract value; it covers the 2009
  bunkering loan as well as the 2007 one; and the paper never links 93 percent to the US$808.1
  million CHEC contract, which appears only in Table 2 against a 31 December 2010 date. `fincov` is
  removed.
- **The second-hand collateral statement is kept.** The appendix records that the five agreements
  were obtained by right-to-information request to the External Resources Department in December
  2019 and lists them one by one (Documents 4a/4b Phase 1 buyer's credit October 2007; 5 bunkering
  facility; 6 Phase II concessional loan; 7 Phase II preferential buyer's credit; 8 Phase I
  ancillary works). The paper says "Going through the five Hambantota port loan agreements we
  obtained from the Ministry of Finance". This is reputable research reporting a negative finding
  from documents its authors held, and the entry attributes it to them rather than asserting it.
  Not typed `none`: the paper says there was no collateral clause, not that no condition was
  attached.

Also cut: the Treasury sentence. It is verbatim ("the Treasury (Ministry of Finance) is required to
provide the funds to the SLPA for repayments on the Hambantota Port loans"), but Box 2 places it
after the 2017 lease and adds that the lease "did not result in changes to loan
terms/commitments, loan repayments, or payment rescheduling". It is Sri Lanka's own repayment
arrangement, dated ten years after the first loan: failure mode 2 and failure mode 5. The `repay`
type now rests on the sovereign-borrower clause instead.

## ramu-nickel-basamuk — BLANK CONFIRMED

Opened the document the writer could not: the Highlands Pacific corporate profile "Ramu Nickel
Frieda Copper: World Class Assets - Here Now", September 2009, at
announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20090921/pdf/31ktrcxgpxsjd2.pdf. The path the writer recorded
(without /pdf/<code>.pdf) returns a client error; the full path fetches.

The "Appendix - Ramu Finance Structure" slide reads in full: "Fully funded non recourse to
Highlands / Capex: US$1.4B. (Including financing costs during construction). / Structure: 75% debt
& 25% equity. Equity funding free carry. / Repayment: By 2017 from cash flow. / Tax: JV 10 year tax
holiday / Security : Debt funding arranged and secured by MCC. / China Syndication - US$560M China
Exim Bank, US$470M Shareholder loan from the Chinese Syndicate / Non recourse - Non Chinese JV
partners have debt portion of capex capped at approx US$950M."

**The blank was right.** "Debt funding arranged and secured by MCC" is one line with no object: it
does not say what secured the facility, whether MCC gave a guarantee, or what China Eximbank asked
for, and it reads as naturally as MCC having obtained the funding. Writing it up as security would
be the writer supplying the term, not the source carrying it. The rest of the slide is ordinary
commercial structure — a 75:25 debt-to-equity split, a non-recourse cap, repayment from cash flow —
which failure mode 7 excludes. The slide shows no conditions precedent, covenant or drawdown
requirement. The `searched` note was amended to record the working URL and this ruling.
