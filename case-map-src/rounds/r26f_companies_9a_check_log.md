# Independent check of r26f_companies_9a

50 input names, all kept as keys. Every non-empty description was checked against at least one page opened in this session; sources the writer cited but that I did not open have been dropped, and several were replaced with better ones. 2 blanks were left as the writer had them (Wuxi Huaguang Electric Power Engineering, Vale Coal Colombia / CNR III) and no new entry had to be blanked.

## Changed entries

- **Richards Bay Minerals** — "on the coast of northern KwaZulu-Natal" and "titanium slag" overstated the source, which says "northern region of KwaZulu-Natal province" and lists the product as "Slag"; ownership and the 2019 figures stand. Settled by the Rio Tinto RBM document (minedocs copy).
- **Rössing Uranium Ltd** — "Erongo region" and "open-pit" are not on the company's own page; both are stated by World Nuclear News, which was added as a source and also records that the stake sold was Rio Tinto's 68.62%.
- **SEPHOS SA (Tervalis group)** — added the Dakar address, which the order gives, and the exact royalty (161,574,890 FCFA). The USGS yearbook was dropped (not opened). The order names no owner, so the Tervalis blank stands.
- **PT Vale Indonesia (formerly PT Inco)** — the flagged attribution was accurate: the Vale page does carry "On October 7, 2020 … MIND ID has 20% of our shares". But the page is stale. Indonesia Miner records the divestment completing on 28 June 2024, making MIND ID the largest shareholder on about 34%, Vale Canada about 33.9% and Sumitomo about 11.5%; the description now gives those and the stale page's figures are recorded in the discrepancy file. "Across Sulawesi" removed — the page gives only the hectares.
- **Vale S.A.** — unchanged in substance; verified against the Form 20-F on EDGAR rather than the minedocs copy, which replaced the source.
- **Vestas Wind Systems** — the company announcement nowhere calls Vestas Danish and does not describe its activities; replaced "Danish" with the stated Aarhus, Denmark office and took the activities from a State of Green profile that states them.
- **Sindh Engro Coal Mining Company (SECMC)** — the ownership, location and capacities came from a sponsored "featured company" page at mining-technology.com, which was dropped. A PACRA rating report of July 2019 gives Government of Sindh ~54.7%, Engro Energy and Thal ~11.9% each, HBL 9.5%, HUBCO 8%, CMEC Thar Mining Investments 4%, and Huolinhe as holder of the preference shares (not a "strategic investor"), plus 3.8 mtpa commissioned July 2019 ramping to 7.6. "The country's first open-pit lignite mine" was unsupported and removed. 222 staff confirmed in the annual report.
- **Urbaser** — "US buyout firm" removed; the release says only "global buyout firm". Completion date 21 October 2021 added.
- **Sime Darby Plantation** — the Sime Darby Oils refining claim is in neither source and was removed. The annual report gives 13 countries; the four named countries and the PNB/EPF percentages come from the Free Malaysia Today report.
- **PT Sumber Segara Primadaya (S2P)** — "owns the Cilacap power station" is not in the ESDM release, which says the expansion is S2P's participation; and the release does not call PT Pembangkit Jawa-Bali a state generating company. Both corrected; the 51/49 split and the US$1.389 billion investment confirmed.
- **Ternium (Las Encinas)** — "manufacturers and builders" replaced with the customer industries the 20-F names; shipments (15,622 kt) and net sales (US$17,649m) confirmed in the 6-K; Las Encinas' Aquila operation added.
- **Procesadora Metalúrgica (Promel)** — Emol calls it only "la empresa chilena Promel": "small", "metals-processing" and the Procesadora Metalúrgica name are not supported and were removed, Boliden is Swedish rather than "European", and the imports ran five years from 1984.
- **Shell CAPSA** — petrol, aviation fuel, LPG, solvents and polyols are not in the opened Petrotecnia article; the product list was cut to asphalt and emulsions, greases, lubricant base oils and low-sulphur diesel. Added the legal name and the 300-in-1953 figure the article gives.
- **Scatec Solar (Scatec ASA)** — neither opened Scatec page names solar, wind or hydropower as its technologies; the list was removed in favour of "renewable energy plants". "Norwegian" replaced with the stated Oslo head office, and the 4.8 GW dated to the 2024 report (the current about-us page gives 8 GW plus 2.1 GWh storage).
- **Salini Impregilo** — sequence wrong: the release is dated the day of the shareholders' meeting and calls the completion of the Astaldi acquisition a step still to come, so "renamed Webuild on 4 May 2020 after taking over Astaldi" was corrected to the announcement of the change during the merger.
- **Rhodia (Rhône-Poulenc, now Solvay)** — the Solvay annual report does not state Rhodia's nationality; "French" was replaced with the Euronext Paris listing stated in the AMF notice of 31 August 2011, which was added as a source. Product lines, 16 September 2011 closing, EUR 6,171m pro forma net sales and 14,255 FTEs confirmed.
- **Van Oord** — unchanged in substance (the press release states "Dutch family-owned", Rotterdam, fifth generation, EUR 2,866m, 5,766 FTEs); the annual-report web page was dropped as a source because it was not opened.
- **Suape Industrial Port Complex** — "Pernambuco state-owned" softened: the article calls it an "estatal portuária" and records the Pernambuco government co-funding dredging, but does not state state ownership. The sixth-largest ranking is now attributed to the port director, as in the article. The 47th anniversary (7 November 2025), 90 companies and 30,000 jobs confirmed.
- **RP Global** — two figures wrong: the pipeline is "exceeding 14 GW(p)", not about 16 GW, and the EUR 480 million is equity and convertible bonds, not mezzanine bonds. The "3 MW to 500 MW" range is in neither opened source and was removed; Vienna and Madrid and the technologies confirmed by the Mirova release, which replaced the law-firm and cluster pages.
- **Sempra Energy** — the about-us page does not say Sempra or Sempra Infrastructure Partners runs Energía Costa Azul, nor give a stake in it; "runs" replaced with the page's own description of the terminal.
- **Sumitomo Corporation** — business-group names brought into line with the page's own labels; all figures confirmed.
- **Salazar Resources** — the Newsfile copy the writer cited returned no text; a syndicated copy of the same release was opened and substituted, confirming the tickers and adding that Curipamba is carried to production and Pijili and Santiago to a construction decision.
- **Vale Coal Colombia / CNR III** — blank kept; the note was rewritten to point to the discrepancy file, where the verified owner history now sits.
- **Samtan** — unchanged in substance; the Petromindo article is dated 6 June 2011 and gives the 49% and the marketing agent service, and the Jakarta Post the 45% purchase (by Indika with PT Muji Inti Utama) and the residual 9%.
- **PowerChina** — the Belt and Road Portal page gives neither the headquarters nor state ownership; Beijing, rank 104, revenues of US$101.9bn, 179,815 employees and "Government owned 50% or more" all come from the Fortune Global 500 profile, which was opened. "State-owned engineering and construction group" replaced with Fortune's wording, and no year is attached to the rank because the page states none.
- **Shell (Shell Brasil)** — the article speaks only of "Shell" in Brazil and never gives the legal name Shell Brasil Petroleo Ltda, so the name was removed; and 420,000-450,000 b/d is a 2025 target, not recorded output, which is now stated. The Shell plc Gato do Mato release was dropped (not opened).
- **Red Back Mining** — "Canadian" is not in the Kinross filing and was removed; the TSX listing, the delisting on 21 September 2010 and the 1.778-shares-plus-0.11-warrant ratio are all confirmed there.
- **Radio Corporation of America (RCA)** — the most rewritten entry. Neither the IEEE's ETHW page nor the Science Museum Group record says GE founded RCA (GE and AT&T are named as part owners), dates the Princeton laboratories to 1941, says the 1950s colour system was adopted as the US standard in 1953, mentions computers until 1971, or mentions car rental, frozen food or publishing. All of those were removed; the 1919 origin in the government takeover of the American arm of Britain's Marconi company, NBC in 1926, Victor in 1929 and the 1986 GE purchase and break-up stand.
- **Thomson Consumer Electronics / Technicolor** — "the world's largest maker of disc media" is not in Britannica and was removed; the 1972 Thomson-Brandt renaming, which Britannica gives, was added.
- **PT Makmur Elok Graha** — "property development company" narrowed to "development company", the description the article supports, and the lease terms attributed to BP Batam, as the article does.
- **Sino-Metals Leach Zambia** — China Daily does not call China Nonferrous Metal Mining state-owned, so that was removed; the 2004 establishment, Kalulushi, the three shareholdings, the capacities and the 426 staff are confirmed. The China Global South project record was dropped (not opened).
- **Shell plc** — the writer's paraphrase of the businesses replaced with the filing's own segment names, and the US$16.5 billion labelled as income for the period including non-controlling interests (income attributable to shareholders was US$16.1 billion).
- **TVI Pacific Inc.** — date wrong: the annual information form puts the loss of control in a 2014 transaction with PRHI, not December 2013, and describes the 30.66% as a jointly controlled venture requiring unanimous consent. "Publicly traded" removed (neither opened page states TVI's own listing); Alberta incorporation in 1987 added; the Agata end is a final DSO shipment in August 2023.
- **Plantations du Haut Penja (PHP)** — FreshPlaza never calls PHP the country's largest exporter, only that it still leads the market; "largest" changed to "led". The Compagnie Fruitière de Marseille relationship and all figures confirmed.
- **SOCO International plc** — the name change was approved on 16 October 2019 and expected to take effect later that day on a new certificate of incorporation, so the flat "changed its registered name on 16 October 2019" was made precise; the working-interest percentages the release gives were added.
- **Tampieri Financial Group** — stands on Al Jazeera, as flagged, with the 15 million euro figure now attributed to the sales manager's 2013 statement of a plan. Nothing from the Tampieri S.p.A. of Faenza register entry is used, and the two are not conflated.
- **Senhuile SA** — "switched to sunflower seed for export to Italy" softened: the Oakland Institute says the plans changed several times and names peanut seed and maize too, with Tampieri's interest being sunflower for its edible oil business. The Oakland report supplied the 14 July 2010 incorporation as Senéthanol, the sweet-potato bioethanol plan and the lease's March 2012 date and 50-year term.
- **Wildlife Alliance** — the history page lists a Phnom Penh address but does not call it the headquarters, so that was softened; the programme list was replaced with the activities and countries the page names (forest protection, wildlife rescue, community agriculture, ecotourism and "Southern" Cardamom Mountains are not on it). The IRS figures are confirmed by ProPublica.

## Weakest entries I let stand, and why

- **SEPHOS SA (Tervalis group)** — everything rests on one 2016 ministerial royalty order. It is a government document and names the company, the mineral, the site and the tonnages, but it says nothing about what the firm is beyond holding a small phosphate mine, and nothing about ownership, so the key's "Tervalis group" is still unaddressed.
- **Tampieri Financial Group** — a single Al Jazeera description, as flagged. "Italian" and the edible oil factory are stated there and nowhere else I could open; if that sentence is wrong, the whole entry is.
- **Procesadora Metalúrgica (Promel)** — the only source is a 2019 newspaper retrospective, and it does not confirm the full name in the key. I kept the entry because the firm, the town, the Boliden residues and the bankruptcy are all stated, but the identity of "Procesadora Metalúrgica" with "Promel" is not sourced.
- **Suape Industrial Port Complex** — one local newspaper article, with the "sixth-largest public port" claim coming from the port's own director rather than an independent count.
- **PowerChina** — the figures rest on a Fortune Global 500 company profile that carries no year for the rank, and the page also labels the company type "Private" while stating "Government owned 50% or more". I used Fortune's wording rather than resolve that for them.
- **PT Makmur Elok Graha** — one Indonesian newspaper report, whose substance is the Rempang lease rather than the company; little is known about the firm itself beyond its group.
- **Stora Enso** — a trade-press summary of the results release, with no head office, nationality or listing stated. The writer's honest gap was kept rather than filled from memory.
- **Shell CAPSA** — a centenary history published by an industry institute. Good on dates and the refinery, but the product range had to be cut back to what that article states.

Two records were added: a second source for the Vale Coal Colombia chain (Coal Age on Murray Energy's August 2015 purchase) and one for PT Vale Indonesia, whose own page gives a shareholding superseded in June 2024. 15 records in all.
