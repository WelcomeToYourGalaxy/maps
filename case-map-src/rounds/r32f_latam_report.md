## My own two weakest entries

**1. `pelotas-street-vendors-shopping-popular` (P094199).** The weakness is the `displaced` figure and the framing. The completion report's own vendor numbers do not agree: its results annex says "383 peddlers and 217 walking vendors benefited" and "100 of the 600 persons targeted were not removed to the Shopping Popular", while an indicator note says the results refer to "the 500 beneficiaries of the Street vendors' facility". I put 500 in `displaced` because it is the report's own count of the people in the facility, and I did no arithmetic on the others. A checker could reasonably cut the field and leave the figures in prose. The second weakness: every word in the Bank's documents frames these people as beneficiaries, and the one source outside the lender is a preliminary conference paper by two UFPel researchers that reports protests and accusations of coercion but gives no figures at all. If that paper is judged too thin, the confidence drops from `documented` to `contested` and the case rests entirely on the Bank reading its own project as a success.

**2. `honduras-road-rehabilitation-ii-land-taken` (P109058).** The whole case is one sentence of one document — the completion report — and that sentence says compensation was "effected in full" in every one of the 201 cases. The harm is land taken with full payment, plus "administrative delays in the payment of compensation to affected parties" that the report itself calls minor, with no count of who was delayed and no length of delay. Three households moved is a real limb (a) count, but it is three. No evaluation review of this report was harvested and nothing outside the Bank was found. This is the thinnest keep in the batch and it is defensible only on the strict reading that land taken is a listed harm.

Next thinnest, so the checker knows where I hesitated: `colombia-water-easements-unsettled-at-closing` counts parcels and easements, not people, and the two families in Neiva are described as "in progress" rather than moved; and `skeldon-cattle-farmers-grazing-loss`, where the money the Bank brought is a carbon purchase and not finance for the works, and the land was cleared for a modernization project the appraisal document says carried no Bank or IFC financing.

## Whether the briefs helped or misled

**Helped, in three specific ways.** The document inventory with paths was worth more than everything else combined: twenty-one rows were read without a single fetch of a Bank document. The plan-reprint section earned its place on exactly one row, P057481, where it flagged the figure 11 as sitting in both the evaluation review and the appraisal document — that flag is the whole drop. And the "no completion report is reachable" headers on five rows were accurate; checking parents and siblings found nothing more for any of them.

**Misled, or close to it, in two ways.**

The candidate sentences were worse than useless on this batch and I stopped reading them after four rows. Of the sentence kinds offered, the ones I saw were: a loan agreement's interest-on-late-payment clause filed under "eviction, or people never traced"; a table of contents filed three times under "borrower's own account"; a lessons-learned paragraph about project duration filed under "PLAN VERB — the reprint trap"; a passage about greenhouse gas emissions "could be arrested" filed under "arrests, police or force". Two whole rows had their only candidate sentences drawn from acronym lists. On the ten rows I kept, **not one** turned on a sentence the extraction surfaced. Every keep came from my own grep or from reading a section end to end.

**Sentence kinds the extraction should have surfaced and did not:**
1. **Spanish and Portuguese plan text, at all.** The task note was right: the extraction is blind here. Every figure in the Dominican Republic case, the Argentine eviction, the Belize income loss and the Honduras house came out of Spanish or Portuguese documents and none appeared as a candidate. The single most valuable pattern would have been `antes de` within twenty words of `indemniza` or `pag` — "ocurrieron antes de otorgarles las indemnizaciones" and "No se pagaron los Predios ocupados" are the two sentences that made the Dominican Republic a case, and both are formulaic enough to pattern-match.
2. **Tables whose rows are people.** Belize's income loss is in a table whose row labels are names and whose columns are length of relocation and valuation of loss; the Dominican plans' expropriation and payment dates are in tables where the finding is that one date precedes the other. A pattern over paired date columns in a compensation table would have caught both. Round 31's writers flagged counts in row labels; dates in row labels are the same problem one step on.
3. **Footnotes in plans, not only in completion reports.** The Argentine eviction — judicial notice in 2002, a judge's order in 2009, the move to Saucelito — exists only in footnote 2 of a four-page remedial action plan. The brief tells the writer to read the completion report's footnotes. The plans' footnotes are where the people are.
4. **The sentence that begins by denying harm and then concedes it.** Three of my ten keeps turn on one: "While there is no involuntary resettlement of people in this project, as part of the SSMP expansion, the Cattle Farmers Association … had to be relocated"; "These investments were not expected to cause physical displacement of people. However, OP/BP for Involuntary Resettlement was triggered as a precaution. As it happened, however, the works … led to the resettlement of 114 families"; "the Involuntary Resettlement policy was not applicable since the rehabilitation works … were expected to be carried out within existing right of way. During implementation …". A pattern for a negation followed within two sentences by `however` or `as it happened` plus a relocation word would have found all three. It is also the inverse of the withdrawn screen, which is presumably why nobody built it.

**One thing in the briefs that could cause a wrong drop.** Every brief carries the same "What to decide" paragraph ending "Where a document records housing actually demolished, in a table with a separate enterprise column, limb (b) is met without a household count." On a batch where nothing is demolished, that sentence reads as the only worked example of limb (b), and a writer following it would have dropped Belize, St Lucia, Peru and Colombia. The worked examples that matter here are a payment dated after a taking, and land occupied and not paid for.
