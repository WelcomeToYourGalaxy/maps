## The four things to settle

**1. The Zhamushui phase gap — settled, and the gap is narrower than the writer thought.**
The July 2006 amendment and the 4 January 2007 Guangrun Project Agreement are not published. But a
third document is: *Official Documents - Amendments to the Loan Agreement and Project Agreement for
Loan 4666-CN*, a letter of 11 January 2012 from the Country Director to the Ministry of Finance,
Hubei province and the company, found through the documents API and fetched. It restates the Loan
Agreement's defined terms as amended: "Project Companies" includes the Guangrun company, Part E of
the Project is the Zhamushui and Hongwawu works, and "Resettlement Action Plans" means the four
original plans and "the Guodian Guangrun RAP", defined as "the resettlement action plan, acceptable
to the Bank, dated December 7, 2005 ... for the compensation, resettlement and rehabilitation of
Affected Persons in respect of Part E of the Project". It also records that the Project Agreement
among the Bank, Jianshi Guangrun Power Development Company Limited and Hubei province is "dated
January 4, 2007, as amended to date", that the successor company assumed "All of the legal
obligations of Guangrun Company under the Project Agreement", and that "All other terms and
conditions of the Loan Agreement and the Project Agreement, except as specifically amended hereby,
shall remain in full force and effect". So the Guangrun resettlement plan was an instrument of this
loan and the works were a part of this project, and the entry may stand with the phase boundary
stated. What still cannot be said is what the January 2007 Project Agreement required of the
Guangrun company, so the 2002 covenants stay scoped in the prose to the four original companies and
counties. One further fact from the 2011 restructuring paper, now in the entry's `why`: the fifth
component "comprises of Bank financed equipment and counterpart funded civil works", so the dam
itself was built with counterpart money.

**2. Wuhan compliance cross-check — settled, and the covenant is now on a better document.**
The conformed Loan Agreement and Project Agreement for Loan 4727, both dated 12 May 2004, are
published and were fetched. The covenant is confirmed, in stronger wording than the appraisal
document's: Project Agreement Schedule 2, paragraph 5(b), "carry out the EMPs, RAPs and the RPF, in
a manner satisfactory to the Bank", and paragraph 6, which requires a new resettlement action plan
satisfactory to the Bank, prepared under the Resettlement Policy Framework and furnished for
approval, before work starts on any activity not in the RAPs that needs land. Failure by Wuhan to
perform any obligation under that agreement is an additional event of suspension under Loan
Agreement Section 5.01(a), so these are obligations with a remedy attached.
On compliance: a search of the documents API for the completion report returned none — the API
lists for P069852 only the agreements, the appraisal document, safeguard documents and the
Independent Evaluation Group's review. The review prints no covenant-compliance table. It does
relay from the unpublished completion report that the project was in compliance with the Bank's
environmental and social safeguard policies and that the resettlement cost nearly twice the
appraisal estimate, paid entirely by the borrower. The entry says plainly that no compliance record
was reachable.

**3. P114107's "Significant, non-standard conditions, if any: None" — settled.**
Page 6 of the document (PDF page 8) was rasterised and read as an image. "None" sits on the same
line as the printed question, in the same type, inside the same table cell; the four stage lines
("Negotiations and Board presentation: None", "Effectiveness: None", "Disbursement: (i)...(iv)",
"Implementation: ...") follow as separate paragraphs in that cell. So "None" is the answer to the
template's question about significant, non-standard conditions, not a heading over the lines below
it. That reading does not empty the entry, for two reasons: the answer is about significance and
non-standardness, not about existence; and the body of the document decides the point anyway —
section G, paragraph 105 says "In addition to the standard covenants ... the following specific
conditions are included in the loan agreements" and then prints the same four disbursement
conditions. The entry now quotes both sentences and lets the reader see the tension, rather than
quoting neither.

**4. The debt service reserve account — stays.**
CONDR's `fincov` names "a required escrow or minimum balance" in terms, and this is one: before
commissioning its first generating unit a project company had to open the account, pay all net
revenues into it until it held the next six months' principal and interest, and keep that balance.
It is not on the brief's list of ordinary commercial terms, which covers pricing, fees, tenor,
maturity, grace, front-end fees, debt-to-equity limits, debt-service ratios, dividend restrictions,
share pledges and completion guarantees — the other money terms in the same section of that
document, all of which stay excluded. The line is kept, scoped in the prose to the four original
project companies, and `fincov` stays on the entry on that line alone.

## The two weakest entries after correction

**1. `zhamushui-guangrun-resettlement-postponed`.** Still the weakest, and it will stay that way
unless the 4 January 2007 Project Agreement surfaces. The amendment letter settles that the
Guangrun works were inside this loan's instruments and that a resettlement plan of 7 December 2005
was one of the loan's defined plans; it does not say what obligation attached to that plan for the
Guangrun company, and the civil works were counterpart-funded. The entry is therefore long on what
the loan required of four companies that are not the one in this case. A lead who wants it shorter
could cut everything from "Hubei had to maintain a project management office" to the debt service
reserve account and lose nothing that bears on Zhamushui.

**2. `wushan-hospital-landslide-resettlement`.** The entry's force is in an absence — no
resettlement covenant among the six — and that absence rests entirely on the 2012 data-sheet
covenant table being the complete list of the loan's legal covenants. The underlying agreements for
Loan 8171 were not looked for in this round; if they are published, the table should be checked
against the agreement's own schedule before the absence is relied on. The same caution applies, a
little less strongly, to xuancheng, where the completion report's "All the legal covenants were
complied with on time" at least confirms the table is the operative list.
