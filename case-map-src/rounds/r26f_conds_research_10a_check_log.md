## 1. kalangala-oil-palm — CONFIRMED document, entry REWRITTEN

**The document is real and the entry quoted it accurately.** EB 2010/99/R.19 opened in full on
members.ifad.org (webapps.ifad.org redirects there). Everything the entry asserted about it checks
out: the document number, the title "Proposed loan to the Republic of Uganda for the Vegetable Oil
Development Project – Phase 2", the amount of SDR 33.5 million / about US$52 million, the
ninety-ninth session of the Executive Board in Rome on 21–22 April 2010, the three conditions
precedent to withdrawal in section E.1 (project manager and financial controller "both acceptable
to the Fund"; "MAAIF shall have constituted a Contracts Committee"; "Draft guidelines for oilseeds
and oil palm development and a Project operations and financial management manual" submitted to
the Fund), the Schedule 3 covenant "No new oil palm development shall be undertaken on the
outlying islands or Buvuma Island" until environmental impact assessments are done and the
environment agency "has issued its compliance certificate", and the Schedule 2(b) rule that such
expenditures "shall only be eligible subject to prior approval by the Fund". The document also
holds further covenants the entry did not use: prior IFAD approval for changes to smallholder loan
terms, for island offices and land development on the outlying islands, and for the oilseed
guarantee fund's operating modalities, plus a formal commitment from OPUL before funds flow to
Buvuma smallholders.

**But it is the wrong phase for most of the harm.** The earlier finding was right. IFAD's interim
evaluation (EC 2010/63/W.P.4, members.ifad.org) gives the first phase as a US$20 million IFAD loan
approved by the Executive Board in **April 1997**, effective **July 1998**, closing date originally
December 2005 and extended to **December 2011**. That is the money that paid for Bugala Island: the
nucleus estate raised from 1,000 ha to 6,500 ha, 7,700 ha made available to OPUL of which 5,624 ha
planted, the 3,500 ha smallholder and outgrower scheme, the Government–BIDCO agreement of April
2003, implementation from July 2003 and smallholder planting from 2006.

**Which phase the card's harm falls in:** the card runs 2002–2015. The land acquisition and the
clearing on Bugala Island — the harm itself — fall in **phase one**, the 1997 loan, which ran to
December 2011. Phase two, approved April 2010, overlaps only the last five years of the card and
extends the project to Buvuma Island and the four outlying islands (Bukasa, Funve, Bubembe,
Bunyama) as well as continuing on Bugala. The quoted environmental covenant restrains new planting
on the outlying islands and Buvuma — not on Bugala, where the dispossession happened.

The evaluation names no covenant of the phase-one loan; it says only that the government's
compliance with loan covenants was rated satisfactory. It also records that the 24 risk-mitigation
conditions on the oil palm development were set by Uganda's environment agency in approving the
2003 environmental impact statement — a government permit condition, not a lender term.

The entry was rewritten to say which phase is which, to date the documented terms to the 2010 loan,
and to record that the first phase's covenants are not published. Types kept: admin, safeguard,
fincov. The interim evaluation was added as a second source.

## 2. libya-coast-guard-eu-italy-detention — CONFIRMED as written

The PDF opened in full on data.privacyinternational.org. The header carries
"Ref. Ares(2019)7307599 - 27/11/2019 Annex 3.6: DG NEAR AD EUTF Libya". It is the action fiche for
"Support to Integrated border and migration management in Libya – First phase"
(T05-EUTF-NOA-LY-04), with EUR 42,223,927.28 from the Trust Fund of an estimated total cost of
EUR 46,300,000 (the balance being EUR 2,231,256 from Italy and EUR 1,844,816.72 from the EU
Internal Security Fund), implemented by the **Italian Ministry of Interior, Public Security
Department**, under indirect management.

Every quoted passage was re-read word for word and each is verbatim:

- "The financing of border control facilities will be also conditional to a progressive access of humanitarian actors" (3.2, Activity 4)
- "The Libyan authorities have to commit not to move the personnel, once they are trained, to a different office/body;" (3.4)
- "a report every four months on the progress of the action, including conflict sensitivity analysis" (4.5)
- "These regular assessments will constitute a basis for a possible decision of suspension of activities" (4.5)

**Genuineness and completeness:** genuine — it carries the Commission's own Ares registration
number and reads as an action fiche throughout. Eighteen numbered pages, running to section 4.6
(communication and visibility); the annexed logframe is not included, so it is not the complete
file, but every section the entry draws on is present and intact.

**Is this the money the case turns on?** Yes. The card's EU money for Libyan border and migration
management was routed through the Italian government, and this is the action under which that
happened: the implementing partner is Italy's Interior Ministry and the activities are support to
Libyan border and coast guard authorities. The amount corrected only to add the total-cost context.

## Does the Accountability Console S3 bucket serve AfDB documents?

**Untested — and the failure was not the host.** The Console complaint page
(`accountabilityconsole.com/complaints/construction-of-the-marrakech-agadir-motorway/`) opened and
does link the closure report at
`https://aconsole-static.s3.amazonaws.com/media/public/cases/eng_closure_report-_marackech_agadir_motorwary_11_december_2016.pdf`,
including a `#page=10` anchor from its commitments table. Two attempts to fetch it were refused
with `PROVENANCE_REQUIRED`: the URL had appeared only inside a fetch result, not inside a search
result, so it needed a user permission grant, and the session's permission channel had already gone
unanswered. A targeted search on the exact filename did not surface the URL, so search provenance
could not be manufactured. Amazon S3 never answered either way.

The pattern is worth recording for the next round, because it is the real constraint: **a URL that
appears in a WebSearch result fetches without a prompt; a URL that appears only in a WebFetch
result needs a permission grant, and once one grant has timed out, every later one in the session
is refused without being sent.** That is why afdb.org served for a search-surfaced document URL in
the same session in which a fetch-surfaced afdb.org URL was refused. The S3 bucket is very likely
reachable — the Console serves its pages from it and the link is public — but that has not been
demonstrated here. The next round should surface the S3 URL through a WebSearch, or fetch it first
in a fresh session before any permission prompt has lapsed.

**It would not change the Marrakech–Agadir ruling.** The Console page already sets out what the
closure report contains: complaint RQ 2010/1, filed 19 July 2010, closed 6 October 2016, monitoring
from 1 January 2012 to 6 October 2016, status "Closed With Outputs", dispute resolution closed
because the actors involved refused to take part, no compliance review undertaken, no compliance
report, no finding of non-compliance. The five items — clearing stones from crossing points,
materials to repair blasting cracks in homes, reconstruction of valley banks, road drainage, the
destroyed water point — the page presents as commitments from the closure report, not as loan
conditions, and on that record they are undertakings agreed years after the complaint. Under the
brief's third failure that is not a condition. The case stays blank either way; what the PDF might
add is the loan's own terms, which the Console page does not give (it lists only "Investments: AfDB"
and "Support: Loans", with no amount and a blank client field).
