# Round 40, batch C — report

Four cases: two India procurement/compensation cards, one India forestry card, one Lesotho roads card.
**Three moved from lender-only to partly outside-sourced; one found nothing. No card reaches
`documented`.** 5 WebSearch calls used of the 20 allowed; everything else came from the two
judgment indexes, the Comptroller and Auditor General's site and two PDFs read from disk.

Document cache: `/tmp/r40c/` (Indian Kanoon search pages and judgment texts, LesothoLII API JSON)
and `/tmp/r40c/pdfs/` (the two Bank completion reports, the Karnataka and Bihar audit chapters, the
CESS working paper).

## What moved

### 1. `patna-ganga-riverfront-resettlement` — the two Beur deaths are now outside the lender

The Caravan, 15 September 2021, reporting from Patna: two labourers died in a sewer at the Namami
Gange works in Beur on 31 May 2021 — **Saddam Hussien, 20, and Mohammed Iqbal Hussain, 18**, both
from Bhagwangola in Murshidabad district, West Bengal; BUIDCo held the works, Larsen and Toubro the
contract, and a Murshidabad subcontractor, Nayan Raza, the men. Maktoob Media, 1 July 2021, carries
the DASAM / Loktantrik Jan Pahal fact-finding report, which held the company and contractor
responsible and recorded that the police registered only an **unnatural-death FIR, 15/21**.

This is the card's own harm — same place, same date, same count as the Bank's report, which puts
"two in Beur in May" 2021 — and the Bank's investment table lists `Patna Beur: Sewerage works` as a
project contract, so these are the credit's works. **The correction is the compensation.** The
completion report says "the next-of-kin were duly compensated" and, in its risk section, that the
payments "were fully paid". Three and a half months after the deaths the families had received
nothing, and there was no culpable homicide case. The report gives no date for the payments, so it
may be describing something later; it does not mention the gap or the FIR.

Second find, on the card's procurement paragraph: **Patna High Court, M/s EMS Infracon Pvt. Ltd. v.
State of Bihar, CWJC No. 9597 of 2021, 19 July 2021**. The bid was for the Munger 30 MLD plant and
167.3 km sewerage network under this US$1 billion credit — the same package for which the Bank
reports a leak of confidential information. The court set aside BUIDCo's blacklisting of the
bidder, found it had "acted irresponsibly in passing the impugned order in casual and cavalier
manner", recorded that three blacklisting orders against bidders on the same invitation by the same
Chief Engineer had been quashed, and ordered a fresh evaluation chaired by the Development
Commissioner with the Mission's Executive Director (Technical) on the committee. It does not
mention a leak: it found different defects in the same evaluation.

**The trap was there and it is empty.** `M C Mehta v. Union of India` was opened twice — the NGT
order of 18 December 2019, which tabulates Bihar's sewerage works including Beur STP, the Beur
network and the Digha and Kankarbagh letters of award, and the order of 29 January 2026, where the
Mission annexes the World Bank's *Second* National Ganga River Basin Project document as technical
support for intercepting drains. Neither records a single fact about the riverfront resettlement or
about any death on these works, and the 2019 order does not mention the World Bank at all.

The riverfront harm itself — 249 families, the two-year stall in the land allotment, the extra cash
instead of land, the sites handed to the contractor before people were resettled — **is still the
lender's alone**, and so is the Begusarai death of March 2021. Hence `contested`.

### 2. `karnataka-state-highways-ii-compensation` — a judgment and a state audit

**High Court of Karnataka, Dharwad, 24 November 2021, MFA No. 102520/2017 and 28 connected
appeals.** Land at Karagudari village, Hangal taluk, Haveri district, taken under the final
notification of 10 January 2011 for the link road between State Highway 1 and State Highway 69,
Hangal to Tadas, **Link No. M7D** — which the Bank's completion report lists as package WEP-2B of
this project, 43.5 km, works 6 July 2013 to 31 January 2017. General award 30 April 2011; reference
court at Hangal fixed Rs 44,000 per gunta on 2 November 2016; the project appealed and the High
Court cut it to Rs 35,000. Ten and a half years from award to final figure, and the state's appeal
lowered what the reference court had allowed. The card had the courts running one way only — owners
suing to raise amounts, or money deposited for want of title. This is the project suing to lower
them, and the Bank's report does not record it. Entered as a correction and as a replacement
`suppression` field.

**Comptroller and Auditor General of India, Karnataka public sector undertakings, year ended 31
March 2019, Chapter V**: of the six concession roads under this loan, audit found "delay in
initiation of land acquisition in respect of six projects" — roads identified and the IBRD
agreements concluded in May 2011, acquisition begun only between September 2014 and October 2017,
final possession notifications August 2016 to February 2019, Resettlement Action Plan approved only
in 2015, because the land acquisition cell was formed in 2012 and staffed in 2014.

**The Karnataka trap, and a second one I nearly walked into.** The KMC-VDB and Gammon India
candidates are contractor and arbitration matters and were left unopened on that ground. But the
CAG chapter carries a paragraph that reads exactly like this card's harm — awards delayed 13 to 23
months and **Rs 180 crore of compensation unpaid as of June 2019** — and it is about the Yelahanka
to Andhra Pradesh border road, which the same chapter treats as the corporation's own project
outside the World Bank co-financing. It is not used. A checker should confirm that reading from
Appendix-20 of the full report, which I did not download.

### 3. `andhra-pradesh-community-forest` — no judgment, but an independent Indian review

Indian Kanoon has nothing on the evictions. What does carry part of the harm is **CESS Working Paper
No. 62, October 2004** (Reddy, Reddy, Saravanan, Bandhii, Springate-Baginski), Centre for Economic
and Social Studies, Hyderabad: the Bank's project funded 2,910 of the state's forest protection
committees; about **24,000 hectares** of land under podu cultivation was returned to the Forest
Department in committee areas, mostly Visakhapatnam and Adilabad, which the review says means "land
has been taken out of use for livelihood support of tribals"; tribal organisations in Paderu
refused the second phase for fear of losing more podu land; and of an estimated **46,725 families**
who may have been cultivating forestland assigned to committees, the second-phase Resettlement
Action Plan covered an estimated **11,680** — about a quarter, against the 28 per cent the Bank
expected to affect.

The two judgments that do exist are worth recording so nobody re-finds them hopefully. *Divisional
Forest Officer, Kothagudem v. Madhusudhan Rao* (Supreme Court, 8 February 2008) names the project
only as the source of funds a forester was charged with embezzling, Rs 4,865 of labourers' wages
among them. *Sreeramanjaneyapuram Village Society v. Divisional Forest Officer* (AP High Court, 18
April 2003) dismissed a claim by 400 Banjara, Sugali and Dalit families at Venkatayapalem whose
possession was formalised through a committee account later cancelled, and recorded the
government's case that they "have already been evicted" — same scheme, same state, inside the
project years, but Bellamkonda in Guntur is not a tribal forest area on this card and the judgment
names neither project nor lender. Not used as the card's harm.

## What did not move

### 4. `lesotho-integrated-transport-farmland-and-resettlement` — nothing

Sixteen phrases through the LesothoLII API, including both of the project's own road names. No
judgment about these works. `compensation for crops` returns only section 54 of the Land Act 2010;
`resettlement` returns five documents, the nearest a Labour Court case against the Lesotho
Highlands Development Authority. The batch's leading candidate, *Ministry of Public Works and
Transport v. Lesotho Consolidated Civil Contractors*, C of A (CIV) 9 of 2014, is about the
termination of a M212,771,071.27 contract of 18 November 2008 for the **Likalaneng to Thaba-Tseka**
road — a different road, no lender, no land, no crops, no households. Two other ministry cases that
the party-name search turns up are a dismissal after a forensic audit and a pedestrian's fall into
a pit. The card stands exactly as it was.

## Routes

- **Indian Kanoon**: served every search and every judgment over plain `curl` with a declared
  user-agent. Results sit in `class="result_title"`; `/doc/<id>/` returns the full text for the same
  id as `/docfragment/<id>/`. "No matching results" is printed in the page body, so a true empty is
  distinguishable from a failure. Its tokeniser drops short alphanumeric codes: `"M7D"` returns ten
  irrelevant documents and not the Karnataka judgment that contains it. Village names worked.
- **LesothoLII**: the `/search/api/documents/?search=%22...%22` path serves JSON to `curl`, as the
  brief says. **The judgment pages themselves do not**: `/akn/.../eng@DATE`, its `/source`, and the
  `.xml`, `.html`, `.txt` and `?format=xml` variants all answer `curl` with a Cloudflare
  interstitial and HTTP 403. That is a route failure of `curl` against those pages, not a refusal by
  the host — the same URLs opened through a fetch tool, which gets the PDF behind the page. There is
  no document-detail API: `/api/v1/documents/?expression_frbr_uri=...` is a 404. **Worth carrying
  into round 41 for the other ten peachjam sites.**
- **cag.gov.in**: chapter PDFs download over `curl`. One file on the `www.` host died with
  connection reset (curl 35) while the same path on the bare host served others.
- **buidco.in**: `curl` refused it — the server's TLS certificate has expired (curl 60).
  Verification was not disabled. The file, BUIDCo's own environmental and social due diligence
  report of April 2018 for the Digha zone, opened through a fetch tool: it names the World
  Bank-financed project, records no formal acquisition because the land is the municipal
  corporation's, and records encroachers and a few families growing seasonal vegetables on the site
  with livelihood loss to be assessed. It is the executing agency's own document, it gives no
  number, and only about a third of it was read, so it is **not** entered as a source. A later round
  with a working certificate should read it from disk against the card's three people at Digha.

## Weak points in my own work, named

**My weakest call is treating the Beur deaths as deaths on this credit's works.** No outside
document read says the World Bank financed the Beur sewer. The Caravan traces the money to a Rs 398
crore sanction by the Ministry of Jal Shakti of 6 April 2017, and the Comptroller and Auditor
General's Bihar audit treats the whole Bihar programme as centrally funded Namami Gange and does not
mention the Bank once. The link is the Bank's own investment table, which lists `Patna Beur:
Sewerage works` as a project contract with 13.9 per cent disbursed and ongoing at closing, plus the
report's own statement that the fatal incidents were at project sites. If a checker decides that a
sewer worked on under both a central sanction and a partly disbursed Bank contract is not shown to
be the Bank's sewer, then the press reports document deaths on Namami Gange works in Beur and
nothing more, and the card's three deaths go back to resting on the lender alone. I have said so on
the card's `confidence_note` rather than hiding it.

Three more, in order of how much they would cost if wrong:

1. **The two Patna outlets give different names for the dead.** The Caravan: Saddam Hussien, 20, and
   Mohammed Iqbal Hussain, 18. Maktoob Media: Mohamad Idrish and Mohamad Iqbal. Only "Iqbal" is
   common to both. I have put both on the card and resolved neither. Newsclick carries a third,
   undated Patna sewer incident on Namami Gange work — Ranjan Ravidas, 24, and Munna Razak, 23, at
   Ward 56 near Zakariyapur — which I did **not** add to any count, because it has no date.
2. **The Karnataka acquisition predates the loan.** The final notification is 10 January 2011 and the
   general award 30 April 2011; the loan was approved 24 March 2011. The judgment never says which
   phase of the highways project it belongs to. My identification rests on the link code M7D and the
   acquiring authority, with the Bank's own table giving M7D Hangal-Tadasa as this project's WEP-2B.
   Early acquisition for a road under preparation is ordinary — the report's odd effectiveness date
   of 17 July 2008 is already flagged on the card — but if M7D was acquired for the first Karnataka
   highways project and only rebuilt under the second, the judgment belongs to a different card.
   Checking it needs the first project's road list, which I did not open.
3. **The CESS figures are second-hand.** 46,725 and 11,680 families are the review citing
   Madhusudhan (2003); 24,000 hectares is the review citing Rangachari and Mukherji (2000). I did
   not open either underlying source, and the 24,000 hectares sits beside the card's 15,000 without
   a document that says what either counts. I wrote that as a correction that cannot yet be made
   rather than as a figure change.

Two things I did not try at all, and a later round should. For Lesotho: the government gazette,
which is indexed on the same host — nine documents answer to `"Nyenye"` and I opened none of them,
because the pages would not serve to `curl` and I had spent the fetch-tool calls elsewhere — plus
the Auditor-General's reports and a Lesotho newspaper archive. For Patna: the 2013 environmental
impact assessment for the river front development of 20 ghats, by Voyants Solutions, which exists
only on Scribd and SlideShare in what I could find, and which is the one document likely to carry a
census of the riverbank households.
