## The promotion I cut, and why

**bukoba-airport-resettlement-arrears.** The writer named this as its weakest call and the call was
weak. Both new records are real and I read both of them myself.

The audit holds. I downloaded AGR/DP/2016/2017 and read the Swahili: significant finding (a),
*Kutolipwa Fidia kwa Watu Waliothiriwa na Miradi*, three projects, TZS 11,870,767,751; schedule 1(i),
*Programu ya Kusaidia Sekta ya Usafirishaji (TSSP)*, 36,826,419; and the funder tables listing TSSP
against *Benki ya Dunia* with seven audits that year. The reading is exact to the digit.

The Kamugisha material holds too, and I did not take the writer's word for any of it. Thirty-odd
paired-phrase queries of my own returned, off TanzLII's index: the 11/10/2010 institution date, the
9 and 10 May 2018 sitting, "PW1 Regina Peter Kamugisha (59) a widow and reside of Kashai", the Airport
Manager as first defendant, Kagera Co-operative Union (1990) Ltd as third party, "her plot No. 142
Block", "measured at 4,768M2, Whereby the 3rd party was paid as ground total of Tshs. 132,330,000",
"Copy of the entire compensation schedule was admitted as exhibit D2 ... Kashai area Bukoba
Municipality and covered plot numbers 109-145", the TZS 10,000,000 disturbance claim, and "she
complained in writing to them in 2009". All of it is in the index. None of it is in a document anyone
has opened.

Three things together sink the promotion.

1. **The audit carries the harm but not the place.** Its figure is project-wide, covering 909 km of
   road and four airports. "Bukoba" appears in that report only in unrelated tables of districts and
   health projects. The step from the figure to this card's place is taken through the completion
   report's statement that no resettlement was needed anywhere else — which is the lender's own
   account. On a card whose place is in its title, that leaves the harm's location inside the lender.
2. **The judgment carries the place but not the credit, and probably belongs to works the credit did
   not pay for.** I found the project's own March 2009 resettlement plan for Bukoba Airport (RP917 v2)
   and it names "Kagera Cooperative Union (KCU) warehouses" among the properties the runway extension
   would affect, which is a real tie between the judgment's third party and the lender's works. But I
   also found the Airports Authority's 2016 plan update (SFG2612), which says that "by the time the
   World Bank got involved in the phase II of the project in 2010, TAA had already finished
   compensation of those identified in valuation report of 2008", that four plot owners from that
   valuation "were fully compensated then", and that the phase II valuation was commissioned only in
   2012. The payment the widow complained about in 2009 sits on the wrong side of that line. This is
   the financing boundary CHECK40 §3 names, and it cuts the one site-exact outside record.
3. **Nothing has been read.** Index metadata, a case note written by peachjam and highlighted
   snippets are not a judgment. For a card that exists because a harm is documented without a count of
   people, promoting it on unread metadata would put more weight on the index than it can carry.

**What would earn `documented` here.** Any one of: a readable copy of [2018] TZHC 2657, from a
registry or a reporter, showing the parcel, the award and whose acquisition it was; a document naming
one of the five cases the completion report counted, with a case number; or a state audit, gazette
notice or Tanzanian press report that places unpaid compensation at Bukoba Airport by name in or
after 2017. The CAG's site-level detail is the most likely of the three: he itemises by project, so a
sector or entity audit of the Tanzania Airports Authority for 2016/17 or 2017/18 may break the
36,826,419 down.

**A by-product the owner should see, and which must not be read onto this card.** The next year's
audit, AGR/DP/2020/21, finding (ix), records that 1,125 households affected by the expansion of
Julius Nyerere International Airport — Kipawa, Kigilagila and Kipunguni — were still unpaid 23 years
after the 1997 valuation, their claim of TZS 7,413,357,576 revalued in May 2020 to TZS 29,768,029,951,
a rise of TZS 22,354,671,575, the recalculation never approved by the Chief Government Valuer, and the
auditor writing that the households' livelihoods had gone on deteriorating. Different airport,
different project, same authority. That is a case, not a sentence for this one.

## Corrections the round produced that I kept and strengthened

The writer's pre-credit correction on Bukoba was right and rested on snippets. The 2016 plan update
puts it on a document, and I have rewritten the correction to say so. The same document also
contradicts the card's statement that the Bank's documents give no count of people: it gives 14 under
the 2008 valuation, 15 in the 2009 plan, 41 plus Tumaini Primary School by phase II, 33 interviewed,
30 entitled to interest for late payment of whom 25 were paid, and the five pending payments broken
down as three court cases, one person who could not be traced and one grievance complainant who meant
to appeal. It also records that a resettlement audit the Bank required in 2015 found the 2009 plan's
implementation did not fully comply with OP 4.12 or Tanzanian law. I added it as a source.

One claim I cut: the round said the Kamugisha claim "succeeded against the airport manager, the
ministry and the Attorney General". TanzLII's case note apportions nothing among the defendants and
gives no sum.

**mafinga-igawa-road-deaths-displacement.** I verified all five audit figures against the PDF —
44,346,567,676.75; 101,462,486,446.77; 17,329,184.29; 129,968.90; 511,718,392.00 — and that the audit
names SATTFP, TANROADS and the World Bank loan ended 31 December 2020. I also confirmed that

not read as figures about this road. TanzLII holds exactly the two judgments the round describes and
both are as described; neither touches the card's harm. `killed` stays at 2: no document ties any
death to the project, the company or anyone acting for them.

**lower-nzoia-irrigation-unpaid-compensation.** The Capital FM page opened for me in full and every
detail taken from it is on it. Both Ojwang decisions exist and name the scheme. On the Tribunal
instruction: I paged the whole 34-document result for "Lower Nzoia" and there is exactly one Land
Acquisition Tribunal document in it, Okongo [2026] KELAT 31, which is Lower Kuja, as the round says.
The Tribunal was searched, the trap was seen, and the absence is real.

What the round left on the floor and I have put on the card: the National Land Commission's own
gazette notices. Notice No. 15518, Kenya Gazette of 22 November 2024, sets out compulsory acquisition
for part of the Lower Nzoia Irrigation and Flood Mitigation project for the Ministry of Water,
Sanitation and Irrigation and calls an inquiry to hear compensation claims in Busia County; Notices
14030 and 14031 of 11 November 2022 cite a run of earlier notices from 2020 to 2022, and another
notice sits in the gazette of 1 August 2025, after closing. That is Kenya's own record that land was
still being taken and claims still being heard around and after the credit's close. **Writing that up
is writer-work done by a checker and nobody checks it after me** — flagged as CHECK40 requires. It is
index metadata only: the gazette pages do not open.

**kiidp-kampala-roads-compensation.** The absence stands and is now better founded. ULII's sixteen
"Resettlement Action Plan" documents are Tilenga oil instruments, electricity cases and parliamentary
rules. Pairing the implementing agency's two names with the words that would matter gives: +
"resettlement", one document, a presidential election petition; + "project affected", two gazettes of
2024 and 2025; + "World Bank", three, of which two are a contractor's claim and a 2026 procurement
challenge over a later Bank-financed Kampala contract. At the Auditor General I checked four listings
the round had not — value for money, engineering audits, report extracts, thematic reports — and none
shows a year before 2017 or names this project. Eight phrases added to phrases_tried.

**kenya-1991-university-fees-esac.** Both corrections verified word for word. The State Department
page reads "On June 30, during a riot over student allowances, one Moi University student, Shadrack
Opiyo, was shot dead by the police. No investigation followed the incident." Okuro carries both
claims attributed to him, including "the government for the first time introduced the cost-sharing or
user charges scheme". The entry does not treat the judgment index's silence as a finding about 1991 —
it says so in terms in two places — and it leaves `killed` at 0 with the reasoning in killed_note
rather than moving a count on a date coincidence. That is the right treatment of two records that
share a date and nothing else.

## A route finding that corrects the round's own method note

The round told the next reader that on peachjam "only counts of 0 are absences". **That is wrong, and
following it would produce false absences.** Peachjam ANDs several quoted phrases **within an indexed
page portion**, not within a document. Pairing "Regina Peter Kamugisha" with "132,330,000", "4,768",
"exhibit D2", "109", "145" or "square metres" all return COUNT 0 — and every one of those phrases is
in that judgment, as the unpaired query "132,330,000" and the pair "Kashai" + "compensation schedule"
show at once. I nearly wrote the figures off as unsupported on the strength of those zeroes. A zero on
a paired query means the two phrases are not on one page together. To test whether a document holds a
phrase, query the phrase alone, or pair it with something from the same page.

## Routes that failed, by route

- TanzLII `/akn/` document paths, plus `/source`, `/source.pdf`, the `www` host without language or
  date segments, and the older `/tz/judgment/high-court-tanzania/` form: HTTP 403, Cloudflare

- `https://www.oag.go.ug/reports`: HTTP 500. `https://www.oag.go.ug/valueformoney`: HTTP 404; the

- Search APIs on all three peachjam hosts, `nao.go.tz` PDFs, `documents.worldbank.org`,
  `capitalfm.co.ke`, `ecoi.net` and `journals.eanso.org` served normally over curl.

## Weak points in my own work

My own weakest call is the other direction from the writer's. I cut a promotion partly on the ground
that the Kamugisha acquisition sits before the credit, and my evidence for that is the Airports
Authority's 2016 plan update, which says compensation under the 2008 valuation was finished before the
Bank's phase II involvement in 2010. It does not say that *these* plots were in the 2008 valuation.
The 2009 plan, prepared for phase II, lists the Kagera Cooperative Union warehouses as property the
extension would affect, which points the other way. If the plots at Kashai turn out to be phase II
land, the financing-boundary objection falls and the card rests on the two remaining objections — the
missing site in the audit and the unread judgment — which I would still hold are enough to keep it
contested, but less comfortably.

Second: I did not count how many of the 16 ULII "Resettlement Action Plan" documents I read past the
title and first snippet. I read six titles and seven snippets, which is what the API returned on one
page, and I did not page the rest.

Third: the Kenyan gazette source I added is a heading and one sentence of boilerplate about an
inquiry. It carries no claimant, no parcel and no sum, and I have written it onto the card as index
metadata. Somebody reading the card quickly could take it for more than that.
