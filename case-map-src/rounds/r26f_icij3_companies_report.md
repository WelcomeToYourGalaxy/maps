## Where the facts came from, by entry

**AASANA** — Supreme Decree 8019 of 21 June 1967 (Bolivian legislation database): a state organism depending administratively on the Ministry of Public Works and Communications through the Sub-Secretaría de Transportes, charged with planning, directing and administering public airports and organising national airspace and air traffic control, taking over Lloyd Aéreo Boliviano's aeronautical assets. Supreme Decree 4630 of 30 November 2021 (Lexivox) orders its cessation, suppression and liquidation.

**ABC** — Law 3507 of 27 October 2006 (Lexivox): an autonomous public-law entity with its own legal personality and assets, under the tuición of the Ministry of Public Works, Services and Housing, responsible for the Red Vial Fundamental and for tolls, weighbridges and vehicle-dimension control, with 30 per cent of that revenue going to the departmental prefectures. The law does not say it replaced the Servicio Nacional de Caminos, so that is not claimed.

**NAABOL** — Supreme Decree 4630 of 30 November 2021 gives the full name, the Ministry of Public Works, Services and Housing as the parent, and the three functions. Nothing further was claimed; no staffing, budget or airport count was found in a source that was opened.

**CIPTA** — A 2022 publication of Bolivia's Órgano Electoral Plurinacional gives the 1993 founding, the TCO Tacana I in Abel Iturralde province, La Paz department, its 20 communities in Ixiamas and San Buenaventura, and the joint role with CIMTA. A 2016 WCS/CIPTA history gives the directorio's structure. The pending 16,000 hectares of compensation land was left out as case-specific rather than descriptive.

**CONDER** — JUCEB, Bahia's commercial registry, lists it among the state's empresas públicas. A state decree reported by Bahia Notícias makes it the executor of works and extensions on state public buildings above per-secretariat cost limits; Bahia Law 13.204 of 2014 permits the state administration secretariat to delegate the building of public facilities to it. Its own site (conder.ba.gov.br, both root and /institucional) was blocked and could not be opened.

**SEDUR** — Bahia Law 13.204 of 11 December 2014 states its finalidade (state urban development, housing and municipal technical-assistance policy) and creates the Superintendência de Mobilidade and the revised Superintendência de Planejamento e Gestão Territorial inside it. Note: the same law moved EMBASA and AGERSA out of SEDUR to SIHS and extinguished SUCAB; it does not list CONDER as linked to SEDUR, so no parent-subsidiary link between the two was claimed.

**Committee for Roads** — A 2016 ministerial order names it the Roads Committee of the Ministry of Investments and Development and refers to an authorised state body for highways handling diagnostics and certification; the official gov.kz page heads itself as the Committee of Highways of the Ministry of Transport while its metadata still says Ministry of Industry and Infrastructure Development; an AIIB project document says it is "responsible for planning and setting strategic direction" and approves the national operator's annual investment plan. ## Case-data findings (reported, not changed)

Five items are in `companies_out_disc.json`. In short:

1. **PGCB** was renamed Power Grid Bangladesh PLC by a resolution of 10 February 2024. The card's name was right for the 2020 completion report it cites.
2. **MOAD** is described in the present tense as the body running the Donsin works. A decree from the Council of Ministers of 18 June 2025 gives its functions to the new Société des Aéroports du Faso (SAFA), a state company. The Sidwaya report names it Société des Aéroports du Faso, not "du Burkina Faso" as one news article had it.
3. **Committee for Roads / KazAvtoZhol.** The KazAvtoZhol card says the company was set up in 2013 under the Committee for Roads and did not exist when the 2010 and 2011 Inspection Panel cases were filed. An AIIB project document says the Roads Committee was itself "established in 2013 under the Ministry of Transport, together with QAJ". If that is right, the Committee for Roads card's description of it as the project implementing agency for the 2010-2011 complaints needs checking. Against that, a 2016 ministerial order names a Roads Committee under a different ministry, and the body's official site now heads itself under a third, so the committee has been reattached repeatedly and may predate 2013 in another form. Flagged, not corrected.
4. **AASANA.** The card's dissolution date of 1 December 2021 matches publication; the decree itself, DS 4630, is dated 30 November 2021 and takes effect on publication.
5. **NTPC appears under two keys** in the data — "NTPC Limited" (siddhirganj, barkagaon) and "National Thermal Power Corporation (NTPC)" (singrauli, ramagundam, farakka). These are the same company; its own site uses NTPC Limited. The two keys should carry the same description.

Three items in the job list do not match where the names actually sit in the data, which is worth passing back so nothing is looked for in the wrong place:

- **DORP** is on `siddhirganj-pipeline-transmission-land`, not `ceip-polder-land-payments-bangladesh`. The CEIP polder case has an empty `companies` array.
- **Intercontinental Consultants and Technocrats Pvt. Ltd.** is on the Nepal road case, not an Indian road case. Its role there is the Satbanjh-Jhulaghat survey, design and Resettlement Action Plan.
- **NTPC Limited** is on the Bangladesh Siddhirganj case (operation and maintenance contract with EGCB) as well as on barkagaon-ntpc-firing-2016.

## Two weakest entries

**CONDER.** The thinnest of the 21. Its own site was blocked twice, so the entry rests on a registry listing that gives only its legal class (empresa pública of Bahia) and on two sources about public-building works: a news report of a state decree making CONDER the executor of state building works above set cost limits, and a clause in Bahia Law 13.204 allowing the administration secretariat to delegate public-facility construction to it. "Urban development" is in the body's name and nowhere in a source that was opened, so it is not in the description — which leaves the entry describing a state building contractor, which may understate what the company is.

**Committee for Roads of the Republic of Kazakhstan.** The parent ministry could not be pinned down: the official site's heading and its own metadata name two different ministries, and a 2016 order names a third. The entry therefore lists the ministries it has sat under instead of stating one, which is honest but vaguer than the brief would like. Its functions come largely from an AIIB project document rather than from a statute or the committee's own charter, which was not found in English or Russian in what was opened.
