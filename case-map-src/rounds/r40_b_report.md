# Round 40, batch B — outside-sourcing report

Four cases: NHIIP (India), KISIP (Kenya), the Kwabenya stone crackers on the Aboadze–Volta 330 kV line
(Ghana), and the Assam State Roads Project package (India). One moved to `documented`, two were
strengthened and corrected while staying `contested`, one found nothing.

## What moved

**`assam-roads-uncompensated-acquisition` → documented.** The Bank says, of itself, that on one package
"the land and properties were acquired without paying compensation to the owners" and names no package,
corridor, district or owner. The Gauhati High Court names them. Three orders deal with acquisition for the
double-laning of State Highway 2 from Langla to Barpeta Town "under the Assam State Road Project", which
the completion report's own economic annex lists as SH-02 Chapaguri–Manas–Barpeta, 60.52 km, one of the six
upgrading stretches:

- WP(C) 5103/2016, *Amzad Hussain and 2 others*, 29 April 2022. Three owners of patta land at Bartari,
  Mouza Titapani, Barpeta were served notices to collect compensation, attended, got nothing. The Circle
  Officer's affidavit conceded the land was theirs. The court found "no reasons have been furnished as to
  why compensation due has not been paid" and gave four months to release it — six years after their
  representation of 7 April 2016 and two and a half years after the loan closed. The Project Director,
  Assam State Roads Project, is respondent 3.
- WP(C) 2114/2021, *Hakim Mullah*, 23 March 2022. 4 kathas 7 lechas at Jahorupam acquired in LA Case
  1/2011-12; award made, pattadars left off the award statement in favour of occupiers, Rs 9,84,936 about
  to go to the occupiers, nothing disbursed to anyone. Sent to the District Judge under section 30.
- WP(C) 5699/2024, *Saidul Islam*, 8 November 2024. Heirs of a pattadar at Kandulia left off the
  beneficiary list of 5 September 2020 in LA Case 9/2011-2012; the whole compensation for their plot paid
  to relatives. Sent to a civil court.

A fourth, WP(C) 6047/2015 (*Super Infratech*, 20 September 2019), is a contractor's matter but names the
respondent as "Assam State Road Projects … World Bank Aided Projects, P.W.R.D.", which is how the project
appears on the court record. Taken together these contradict the project team's email of 10 June 2020 to
the Independent Evaluation Group that all compensation was fully paid and all land acquisition issues
amicably settled.

**`nhiip-india-corridor-land-acquisition` → contested-but-strengthened, with four corrections.** The card
said no court record about land acquisition under this project was found, and that Bihar highway land
disputes name no section financed by this loan. Both are wrong. NH-106 Birpur–Bihpur is named as a project
road on three independent records: the Central Administrative Tribunal's judgment of 10 September 2026
(OA 3193/2025) quotes the ministry's project title — rehabilitation of the "Birpur-Bihpur Section
(km 0+000 to km 106+000) of NH-106 … under Phase-I of National Highway Interconnectivity Improvement
Projects (NHIIP)", contractor IL&FS, agreement 27 September 2016; the Patna High Court's suo motu order of
18 April 2025 says the same; and the Bank itself published an environmental impact assessment for
Birpur–Udakishanganj (km 0 to 106) of NH-106 under P121185 in February 2014, which states "The Project is
being financed by World Bank". On that road:

- CWJC 15126/2016, *Rajeev Kumar Mishra*, 15 September 2025: 5 katha at Tola Kamalpur, Supaul acquired and
  NH-106 "constructed without payment of any compensation to him"; the writ had sat nine years, and the
  court sent him to a civil court instead of deciding it.
- CWJC 13438/2024, *Smt. Nutan*, 10 September 2024: an arbitration award for NH-106 land (Koshi Circle,
  Arbitration Case 31/2020) reached execution, which ordered Rs 3,66,61,312 paid within fifteen days on
  8 February 2024; still unpaid. The Nodal Officer, NHIIP, is a respondent.
- CWJC 10131/2026, *Vijay Kumar*, 14 July 2026: balance compensation for 0.1990 acre at Madanpur,
  Madhepura, paid as residential rather than commercial land.
- CWJC 320/2019 (suo motu), 18 April 2025: works due by 2 October 2019 were at "only 95.31%", with no link
  roads, so even the 103 km built was unusable.

It stays `contested` because the money is not traced: the completion report names only the eight sections
finished under the loan and never names NH-106 or the five Bihar/West Bengal roads transferred in 2020 to
the Green National Highway Corridors Project, and no court record gives a loan number. The project-wide
figures (775 acres, 11,138 PAPs, 6,390 structures) remain lender-only.

**`kisip-informal-settlements-fatalities` → contested-but-strengthened, one real correction.** The card said
a search for independent reporting or litigation on harms under this project found none. Kenya Law's index
lists 44 documents naming KISIP, including two rulings in a Kisumu constitutional petition with the project
as first respondent (*Michael Miyawa Obul & 4 others*, [2018] KEHC 4492, 30 August 2018, and [2019] KEHC
1895, 15 November 2019, whose petitioners the index describes as residents of Kopere Market), *Paul
Odhiambo Jaramba & 2 others v KISIP* ([2021] KEELC 855, 19 November 2021), and — at the card's own place —
*Mutai & 4 others (Representing 31 families/squatters at the Riparian Section in Majengo/Talai Settlement
Scheme) v Kericho County Government & 4 others*, ELC Constitutional Petition E001 of 2025, ruling of
11 December 2025, which the snippets show turning on "lists of squatters in Majengo/Talai Settlement Scheme
prepared by KISIP". Majengo Talai is one of the three settlements where the Bank said 82 households had to
be moved and had not been; the court was still at it six years after closing. Separately, Kenya's
Auditor-General audits this project year by year: the FY2020 report (the closing year) is unqualified and
confirms the three financing agreements and their dates — IDA 4873-KE of 23 May 2011, AFD CKE 1055 01 J of
17 December 2015, Sida TF-018327 of 7 July 2015 — and the FY2022 report, signed 13 April 2023, is qualified
only over Kshs 15,203,347 of goods and services (including Kshs 1,519,600 of subsistence allowance for
unidentified officers). Neither says a word about deaths, resettlement or compensation arrears. The six
deaths therefore still rest on one paragraph of one World Bank document.

**`ctb-kwabenya-stone-crackers` → no-outside-source-found.** GhaLII has nothing. "stone crackers": 0.
"Aboadze-Volta": 0. "transmission line corridor": 0. "330 kV transmission line": one ECOWAS supplementary
act. The three Volta River Authority judgments the batch flagged were checked at index-and-summary level
and all three are off-subject: *Osei* (IL/0033/2022, 5 June 2023) is an Accra matter with an industrial
court number, *Ettibuah* (E3/3/24, 24 July 2026) is an employment case about medical retirement on a
Medical Board finding, *Ampomah* ([1989] GHACA 25) is a 1989 dispute over rent cards. The "Kwabenya" hits
are tenancy, contempt and land-title matters at a place of that name; the gazette hits are marriage
licences, electoral areas and site plans.

## Routes

- **Kenya Law and GhaLII `/search/api/documents/` served normally over plain curl.** Quoted phrases
  behaved as the brief says.
- **Both sites' judgment pages refused plain requests.** Kenya Law `/akn/…` returns HTTP 202 with an AWS
  WAF JavaScript challenge (`x-amzn-waf-action: challenge`) and an empty or script-only body, including on
  `/source` and `/source.docx`; the fetch tool refuses the same URLs as robots-disallowed; the legacy
  `/caselaw/cases/view/…` path is 403. GhaLII `/akn/…` returns HTTP 403 with a Cloudflare interstitial.
  These are route failures, not refusals by the hosts — the APIs on the same hosts answered throughout.
  I did not attempt to solve either challenge.
- Indian Kanoon served every judgment over curl with a declared UA. World Bank documents and the Kenyan
  Parliament library's PDFs (55 MB and 59 MB) downloaded and read with `pdftotext -layout`.
- Web search used 4 times out of the 20 allowed; three of the four found nothing, the fourth found the
  Auditor-General reports.

## Weak points in my own work, named

**My weakest call is treating the Gauhati High Court cases as the Assam card's harm and setting that card to
`documented`.** The Bank names no package, no district and no owner, so the match runs through the works
(ASRP, State Highway 2 to Barpeta, which the ICR's annex lists as a project stretch) and not through the
parties. These three claimants may well be a different grievance on the same corridor from the one the Bank
suspended, and in two of the three the failure is misdirected payment — the award money went to occupiers
or relatives rather than to the patta holders — which is an apportionment failure as much as a
non-payment. Only *Amzad Hussain* is an outright "nothing paid to the owners" case. A checker who thinks
the standard requires same-people-same-parcel identity with the Bank's package should demote this to
`contested-but-strengthened`; I judged that the Bank's silence makes that identity unachievable by anyone
and that the harm type, project, corridor and dates all match.

Second weak point: **the NH-106 financing chain.** I have the ministry and the court calling it Phase-I of
NHIIP and the Bank publishing its environmental assessment under P121185, but I never found a document
tying the civil works payments to loan IBRD-83010 rather than to the Green National Highway Corridors
Project after the 2020 transfer. I searched the Bank's document API for "Birpur" and "Udakishanganj" under
both project numbers and found only the one 2014 assessment (whose own file name carries P121100, while
the proid filter returns it under P121185 — worth a checker's eye). If NH-106 was in fact dropped at
appraisal and built with other money, the Indian court material survives as corridor harm but weakens as
this loan's harm.

Third: **everything I say about Kenya's three judgments is index metadata and search snippets, not the
judgments.** I have titles, courts, case numbers, dates and fragments of highlighted text, and nothing
about what was pleaded or decided. I have not claimed otherwise anywhere in the JSON, but a checker with a
browser that can pass the WAF should read [2018] KEHC 4492, [2019] KEHC 1895, [2021] KEELC 855 and
[2025] KEELC 8641 — the last of these, the 31 riparian families at Majengo/Talai, is the likeliest to carry
the card's unfinished-resettlement harm, and the Kopere Market petition is the likeliest to carry an
eviction or works-related harm.

Fourth: **on Ghana I stopped at the index.** GhaLII's judgment text was unreachable, so my rejection of
the three VRA cases rests on their titles, case numbers and GhaLII's own one-line summaries. Those are
clear enough to reject (employment, tenancy), but if a checker can read them and one turns out to mention
the line's corridor, that is on me. I did not search Ghanaian press archives, the Lands Commission, the
Public Utilities Regulatory Commission or Parliament's Hansard, any of which could hold the Kwabenya
quarry operators' side of this.

Fifth: **I did not look for Kenyan parliamentary committee reports** (Public Investments Committee on the
Auditor-General's KISIP reports), which is where a death on a World Bank-funded works contract would most
plausibly surface in a Kenyan state document, and I read only the FY2020 and FY2022 audits of the four in
the Parliament library — the FY2021 and FY2024 reports are unread, and the FY2021 one covers the months
when the Bank said compensation for the fatalities was due (deadline end of August 2020).
