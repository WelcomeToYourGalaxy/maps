outcome documents rather than from plans. Two of the nineteen reasons changed substantially on
re-reading, and three figures found in the outcome documents are recorded below so the owner can
reverse those rows if the rule is read differently.

## The two least certain calls

**P082328, Betim.** The completion report says the move was needed "In order to execute drainage
works, interceptors, and the rehabilitation of rivers banks and green areas" - works-driven, not
purely a move out of danger - and it says cash compensation below R$10,000 was "too small in
value ... to allow families to purchase a new house". Read alone, those two lines make a case.
Read whole, the same sentence gives the remedy: families in that position were housed in the
Conjunto instead. The pressure the report records - the social team "had to work hard in order to
convince these families" - carries no count, and the only field-based document found the opposite,
with families contacted on site saying the conditions were adequate and five saying their new
houses were far superior. What is left unanswered is where the 557 cash-compensated families
ended up: the report says they were monitored "for a short time" and most moved to other cities,
and no survey was ever done. If the owner reads an unmonitored cash settlement capped at R$20,000
as compensation below replacement cost, this row becomes a case on 887 families.

**P121263, GEF City Cluster Eco-Transport.** The money question and the figure question pull in
opposite directions. The grant paid for consultants, and the terminals were built with the Hunan
Department of Transport's own US$27.27 million - which is the Burkina Faso rule and the ground for
the drop. But the Bank triggered OP 4.12 on the Western Changsha hub and required a resettlement
action plan for it, so the Bank itself treated the hub as the project's business, which is the
opposite of the Burkina Faso finding that the airport platform was not an associated facility. If
the owner judges the hub an associated facility, there is a relocation to write about, and the
figures would have to come from the January 2011 plan RP1093 - 64 households, 240 persons, 46 of
them already living in sheds from an earlier displacement - because the completion report's
"65 urban and 54 rural households" is the plan's scope restated, not an outcome.

## Where documents disagree

- **P100968.** The completion report's Annex 10, paragraph 8, says one 14 mu gas storage site was
  not formally acquired as of March 2018 and that compensation would follow approval. The
  Evaluation Group's review of 14 December 2018 omits it and says "all compensations following the
  RAP were paid to the affected people, and the process and outcome of resettlement were found to
  be satisfactory for them."
- **P100619.** Completion report paragraph 39 and the Evaluation Group's review of 9 June 2017 say
  no land acquisition was needed. The borrower's comments in the same completion report say
  Executive Instruments for compulsory acquisition of the depot and terminal were issued and
  compensation for the depot was paid.
- **P129563.** The completion report gives no family count and no temporary-land figure; the
  Evaluation Group's review gives 546 families with 1,914 people, and 1.26 ha taken temporarily
  from 39 households with 130 people "that caused seasonal economic losses".
- **P082328.** The completion report's own ratings table gives Borrower Performance
  "Satisfactory". The Project Performance Assessment Report's principal-ratings table prints the
  completion report's borrower rating as "Highly Satisfactory". Outcome: Satisfactory in the
  completion report, Moderately Satisfactory in both the review and the assessment report.
- **P093882.** The completion report says the three dropped plants went because other financing
  was found; the Evaluation Group's review frames two of them as needing no land because the
  units sat inside existing plants. Immaterial to the verdict.

## Three figures recorded for a possible reversal

1. **P112626, Liuzhou II.** Five named business premises demolished at Baisha - a materials
   recycling company, a pig-raising equipment company, an equipment lease warehouse, a car park,
   and prefabricated houses used as a construction team's quarters - 5,631.01 m2, 5 households,
   31 persons, on 19.13 mu taken beyond the plan for urban landscaping and road construction. If
   business premises demolished count as trading places taken, this row is a case.
2. **P129563, Anhui.** 39 households, 130 people, seasonal economic losses from 1.26 ha taken
   temporarily, land later returned, compensation paid. If a compensated seasonal loss counts, this
   row is a case.
3. **P100968, Shanxi.** 14 mu occupied by civil works without formal acquisition and without
   compensation as of March 2018. Against it: the project's own supplement plan says the storage
   stations take only unused land, so no crop, dwelling or trading place sits on it, and no person
   is counted.

## Routes

Every document came through the wds API and the documents1 `/txt/` route, which worked for all
nineteen P-numbers. Three notes on routes, not hosts:

- **`wds?projectid=` returns only what is filed under the exact number, and `harvest.py`'s
  document-type filter does not ask for implementation status reports.** For P087291 and P100619
  those reports are the only outcome documents or the only year-by-year record, and the default
  harvest returns none of them. They were fetched by a second pass over the same cached wds
  response. Anyone re-running a project with no completion report should do the same.
- **The `qterm` title search for a Jincheng completion report returned the same eight items the
  projectid query gave, plus one unrelated board report for P100968.** That is a genuine absence,
  not a route failure.
- **One route failed, once:** the Loan Agreement for L7531-CN under P093882 came back as a PDF
  with no text layer (`pdf-no-text`), so it was not read. It is a conformed loan agreement and
  bears on nothing here.
