# Weakest entries I let stand, and why

1. **shell-ogbodo-isiokpo-spill.** The only document is a Human Rights Watch
   chapter about SPDC's Nigerian operations as a whole. It names neither Ogbodo
   nor Isiokpo, and the headline production figure comes from a document SPDC
   wrote for its own workshop. It stands because the venture is the operator of
   this project in this period, every figure is on the page, and the paragraph now
   says in its first sentence that no field-level figure was found. A reader could
   still reasonably say this is company context rather than project outcome.

2. **penarroya-cobrac-santo-amaro-lead.** After the false workforce disagreement
   came out, what is left is: dates, the ore source, an unsourced ~2,500 workers,
   and a 1974 capacity request known only at third hand (a 2024 student research
   project quoting a 2007 paper quoting a 2003 UNDP report, neither of which is in
   its reference list). No output, revenue or tax figure exists in any of the three
   documents. It is thin but it is honestly thin, and each remove is attributed.

3. **heinda-tin-mine-myaung-pyo.** The only statements about what the mine
   delivered to anyone are the company's, published under a mediation settlement in
   which the newspaper agreed to print them. The thesis supplies area and reserve
   estimates, not output. The EITI report supplies nothing mine-specific, and I
   could not read enough of it to confirm the writer's negative claim, so that
   claim is gone.

4. **shell-vila-carioca-contamination.** Both documents are real and opened, but
   one is a council minute of unknown year and the other a 2005 magazine report;
   between them they establish two dates, a quote and an agreement, and nothing
   about what the terminal handled.

5. **tayan-nuur-altain-khuder.** Everything about output passes through SOMO from a
   company slide deck, and SOMO itself carries two unreconciled 33% figures, one
   of which the writer had used as fact. The corrected paragraph now uses only
   SOMO's sourced numbers and labels the market share as the company's claim.
