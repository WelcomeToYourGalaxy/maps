# Round 41 — nhiip-india-corridor-land-acquisition

**Verdict: `documented`.**

One case, one question: does the link from the harm to the loan hold well enough to promote. It does,
and the ground is firmer than round 40's checker proposed. But the document the checker recommended
promoting on — the execution order of 8 February 2024 — was set aside by the Patna High Court on
17 November 2025, and neither the round 40 writer nor the checker found that order. A card promoted
on the checker's reading alone would have carried a vacated order as settled fact.

## (a) The procurement plan, re-found

I did not take the checker's word for it. I found the plan myself through the Bank's documents API
(`proid=P121185`, 93 documents) and downloaded it from `documents1.worldbank.org`. Works package 12:
rehabilitation and upgrading of the Birpur–Bihpur section of NH 106, Rs 435.89 crore, ICB,
prequalification yes, review by Bank **Prior**, expected bid opening 07.03.2014. Dated September 24,
2013. The third table carries a consultant or NGO to implement the resettlement action plan for the
same section (item 10, QCBS, **Post** review — the checker said "prior review" of the works, which is
right, but the RAP agency itself is post-review). Fifteen works packages, five item-rate and ten EPC;
NH-106 is the twelfth, in the EPC group. All confirmed.

Two things the checker did not record. The plan's own heading reads "Procurement Plan for Phase I of
NHIIP **[P121885]**" — a third project-number artefact alongside the P121100 in the environmental
assessment's file name. And every World Bank URL in the round 40 entry points at
`documents.worldbank.org`, which does not serve; the same paths serve on `documents1`.

## (b) What the Patna High Court records as fact

The checker's central round 40 correction was sound and I applied the same test to the document it
relied on. **CWJC 13438 of 2024 does not record the non-payment as a finding.** Paragraphs 3 and 4 —
the award, the Rs 3,66,61,312, the fifteen days, "till date, the petitioner has not been accorded the
award amount" — all sit inside the narrative introduced by "Learned Senior Advocate for the petitioner
contended that". What the court does in its own voice is in paragraphs 6 and 7: it asks the execution
court to bring Execution Case 06 of 2023 to its logical end within eight weeks, and directs the
ministry's and the Highways Authority's counsel to co-operate in complying with the order of
8 February 2024 — qualified by "if the award holds the field till date". That is a court acting on a
payment not made, which is more than a pleading and less than a finding.

So I went looking for the rest of the file, and found three more orders in the same dispute:

- **CWJC 1694 of 2023, 28 March 2023.** The court recites the Koshi Division Commissioner's order of
  16.7.2021 (memo 785 of 17.7.2021) in Arbitration Case 31 of 2020, which directed that the Collector
  of Madhepura take possession of her land only after the compensation had been calculated. The court
  then orders the authorities to "maintain status quo with respect to the land of the petitioner". The
  Rs 3,66,61,312 figure appears here as counsel's account of a letter of 4 April 2022.
- **CWJC 1694 of 2023, 9 May 2023.** Full respondent list: the Project Director, N.H.-106 at Purnea;
  the Executive Engineer, National Highway No. 106, Madhepura; the Land Acquisition Officer cum
  Competent Authority, N.H.-106, Madhepura. The writ is disposed of for the section 36 remedy.
- **C.Misc. 919 of 2024, 17 November 2025.** The Executive Engineer of the Madhepura National Highway
  Division applies to quash the execution order. The court's own opening paragraph — its recital of
  what is under challenge, not a party's submission — sets down the award dated 16.07.2021 in
  Arbitration Case 31 of 2020 and the direction to pay Rs 3,66,61,312.00. It then **sets the execution
  order aside** for want of jurisdiction (an Additional District Judge is not the Commercial Court for
  an award of that value), sends the parties to the District Judge of Madhepura, and records that the
  state's own Miscellaneous Appeal No. 1 of 2022 against the award is pending there.

That is where the award and the amount are court-recorded fact. It is also where the promotion the
checker proposed would have gone wrong: the compensation is still unpaid, but the amount is back in
dispute and the enforcement order is gone.

One further court finding, from the High Court's own suo motu proceeding, that neither round 40 pass
had: on **24 January 2023** the Chief Justice recorded the MoRTH–IL&FS agreement of 29 July 2016 (not
27 September 2016, the date the tribunal quotes from a ministry letter), commencement 3 October 2016,
completion due 2 October 2019, contract value **Rs 675 crore**, the work split into three sections over
km 0 to 106 — and that beyond the hindrances the contractor had named there were 507 electric poles to
move and "at least 60 structures (house, building, shops and religious structures)" still to be
removed before the site could be handed over. Sixty households, shops and places of worship standing
on the alignment of a loan-financed package, six years after the works began, in the court's own
voice. I re-read the 18 April 2025 order and the round 40 quotations from it check out.

## (c) P121100/P121185, and whether another operation paid

**Project number.** Settled independently: `qterm="Birpur"` on the documents API returns exactly one
document, the NH-106 environmental assessment, `projectid P121185`. P121100 in the file name and
P121885 in the procurement plan's heading are typographical.

**Post-2020.** This is where the round goes beyond the checker, and the answer is that the map's
Burkina/São Paulo precedent does not bite.

- The project's **own procurement plan of 9 June 2020** carries the works as package NHIIP-BR-106-7,
  "Birpur - Bihpur Section (km0 to km 106.0) of NH-106 ... under Phase-I of National Highways
  Inter-connectivity Improvement Projects", against **loan IBRD / 83010** — the loan number on this
  card — under the Road Improvement and Maintenance Component, review type **Prior**, actual amount
  **US$101,671,942.50**, status Under Implementation. An Authority Engineer consultancy for the same
  section sits on the same plan against the same loan. The court's Rs 675 crore and the plan's
  US$101.67 million correspond at about Rs 66 to the dollar, the rate of the year of award; that is my
  arithmetic, not a document's, and the card says so.
- The **transfer happened on 6 July 2022, not in 2020.** The Green Corridors completion report of
  11 September 2026 records that its first restructuring incorporated "the balance NHIIP civil works
  contracts for 155 km" and reallocated US$95.41 million to new Components D, E and F to finance them.
  The NHIIP restructuring paper of 30 September 2020 names no road.
- **No Green Corridors procurement plan names NH-106.** I read six — 3 Dec 2020, 3 Nov 2021, 12 Apr
  2022, 21 Nov 2023, 9 Oct 2024, 20 Mar 2025 — and grepped each for Birpur, Bihpur and NH-106. Zero
  hits. The only trace is a technical audit consultancy for "stretches under GNHCP & NHIIP".

So the works that took the land were procured under this loan, under the Bank's prior review, with a
contract amount recorded against the loan number, and they stayed there until the loan closed.

## The chainage "inconsistency" dissolved — and the parcels found

The decisive document neither round 40 pass opened is the ministry's **Social Impact Assessment and
Resettlement Action Plan for NH-106**, disclosed by the Bank as RP1245 v11 in February 2014. It says
the Birpur–Bihpur section runs km 0 to km 136 and is split into **Package I, Birpur to Udakishanganj,
km 0 to 106**, and **Package II, Udakishanganj to Bihpur, km 106 to 136, the Kosi missing link**. The
loan financed Package I. Every chainage in the record is therefore correct for what it describes, and
the checker's recorded "inconsistency" was a misreading.

The plan also carries a village-by-chainage table, which puts both claimants' villages inside the
financed package: **Manikpur, Madhepura, at km 75+400 to 76+000** (where Smt. Nutan lives; the plan's
land schedule values 39,083 sq m there at Rs 1,95,41,500, and its CPR list has structures at Manikpur
at km 75.610), and **Kamalpur, Supaul, at km 43+000 to 44+400** (Rajeev Kumar Mishra's Tola Kamalpur,
whose plot numbers appear in the plan's list of affected holdings). Package figures: 1,997
project-affected people, 449 households (140 land, 309 structures), 17 CPRs, private land 9.2491 ha in
Supaul and 7.3245 ha in Madhepura, R&R budget Rs 76.49 crore. The plan also says OP 4.10 was **not**
triggered on this road.

## Two corrections to the card beyond the harm

- The card said there was **no Inspection Panel complaint**. The Panel received a Request for
  Inspection on 18 December 2015 and 28 more after it, naming this project, from over eighty people in
  East Godavari alleging displacement by the widening of NH-216, and issued a Notice of
  Non-Registration on 2 February 2016 on the ground that neither project financed NH-216. A complaint
  naming this project exists and was refused; no registered complaint about this project's harm.
- The Green Corridors completion report says NHIIP **"closed in September 2022"**. This project's own
  documents say 30 September 2020. The Bank's record contradicts itself on when its loan closed.

## Two near misses, named so they are not used later

1. The resettlement plan's census records a **Nutan Devi** as a residential squatter at km 9+450,
   Bhagwanpur, Supaul. That is not Smt. Nutan, wife of Arun Kumar Gupta, of Manikpur, Madhepura.
2. A **Lok Sabha speech of 17 March 2020** by the member for Madhepura says land for NH-106 was
   acquired from farmers and the ministry has no money to pay compensation — this card's harm exactly,
   from a state record, in the words of an elected representative. It is about **km 124 to 136**, the
   Kosi missing link in Package II, under the Prime Minister's package, which this loan did not
   finance. It is on the card as what it is and is not treated as support. It is the single most
   seductive wrong source in this file.

The Indian state audit route produced nothing: the Principal Director of Audit's report and certificate
on loan 8301-IN for 2018-19 (6 March 2020) is unqualified, badly scanned, and silent on land.

## My weakest call, named

**Promoting on one landowner.** The outside record of this card's harm is, at the level of a person
unpaid, one woman in one village. Her village is inside the financed package by the loan's own
resettlement plan and the acquiring authority is the NH-106 Competent Authority, but **no document
places her holding on a chainage, and she is not in the plan's 2014 list of affected owners for
Manikpur**, which names other people. Her award is a 2021 re-valuation, so the gap is explicable, but
it is not closed. A checker who thinks `documented` requires the harmed person to be identifiable in
the lender's own census should demote this, and the card now contains everything needed to make that
argument. My reason for promoting anyway: the court's own January 2023 finding of sixty unremoved
houses, shops and religious structures on the alignment is harm on this package recorded by a court
with no identification problem at all, and it does not depend on Smt. Nutan.

**Second weakest.** The Rs 675 crore / US$101.67 million correspondence is my arithmetic. If it is
wrong, the IL&FS contract the Patna High Court supervises and the package in the Bank's procurement
plan are tied only by road, section, chainage and dates — which is still a tie, but one document fewer.

**What I did not do.** I did not find a disbursement record naming the NH-106 package, which is the
one thing that would make the financing chain unarguable; the checker named this gap in round 40 and
it is still open. I did not read the FY2014-15 through FY2016-17 audit reports or the 2022 audited
financial statement, any of which might carry a statement of expenditure by package. I did not pursue
the Bihar press, which on a nine-year court file about an unbuilt national highway almost certainly has
reporting on the sixty structures and the protests the April 2025 order mentions. I did not look for
the District Judge of Madhepura's disposal of the award, which the November 2025 order required within
six months — i.e. by about May 2026, and today is 17 November 2026, so it may exist.

Used no WebSearch calls of the twenty allowed; every document came from the documents API, Indian
Kanoon or a direct PDF. Did not touch base.html, did not build, did not commit.
