## My own two weakest entries

**1. `muradiye-hepp-urgent-expropriation-van` (Turkey, P112578).** The lender-link is not nailed down. The project is a financial-intermediary loan: the Bank lent to TSKB and TKB, which on-lent to private sponsors, and the completion report names no sub-project at all. The Muradiye reporting form is filed as volume 13 of the project's resettlement plan series RP762 — the series number of the project's own resettlement policy framework — and the form says the sponsor reports to TSKB, but the form calls itself a reporting form for **portfolio projects** and never says which loan paid for the plants. A checker could reasonably cut the Muradiye figures on round 31's fault 3 and leave only the project-wide admissions, which have no place to sit on a map. I kept it because the project-wide statement ("Resettlement in some sub-projects took place without a Resettlement Action Plan") is a real harm of limb (b), and because dropping a case for lack of a named site is how round 31's withdrawn screen destroyed real cases. The uncertainty is stated in `confidence_note`, not buried.

**2. `kurgan-tyube-istaravshan-utility-works-assets-removed` (Tajikistan, P079027).** Every loss here is small and every document says it was paid. There is no unpaid balance, no relocation, no complaint and no count of people moved — a fence, six trees, trees of four families, a cotton field occupied for a while, and a house foundation inside a reservoir site where the owner was given another plot. It survives limb (b) on "land taken" and assets destroyed with named claimants, and nothing more. One figure in the borrower's annex is also impossible as printed: six small farms with "a total area of 3,936 ha" at Istaravshan, against 0.92 ha for nine farms at Vakhdat and individual farms of 0.096 to 1.9 ha in the Istaravshan plan. The report never corrects it, so the card gives it as printed and says so.

## Figures where two documents disagree, with both sides

1. **Bajkaj landfill, how many owners were owed.** Borrower's land acquisition completion report, October 2014: 21 plots, **20 land owners**, 13 paid in full, **7** outstanding. Bank's completion report, 27 April 2016, safeguards annex para 7: 3.2 hectares "from **21** project-affected people", 13 full payments, **8** pending. Neither explains the difference; the plan/owner distinction (21 plots, 20 owners) is the likely cause but no document says so.
2. **Bajkaj, why seven were unpaid — inside one document.** The executive summary and section 3.3 say the seven "are either deceased or living abroad, mostly in Greece". Section 3.2 of the same report says seven "have not applied for compensation", with migration the reason for three and the death of the proprietor for four. 3+4=7, so the two accounts are compatible, but they are not the same statement.
3. **Shkodra orchard, date and size.** Bank completion report, Annex 10: "in **June 2008**, the Municipality of Shkodra ordered demolition of a **5,000 M2** orchard". Site plan of 15 April 2010: "In **May 2008** … demolished the existing improvements", enclosure of about **4,000 m²**, 3,600 m² grapes and 400 m² cherries. Neither document reconciles them.
4. **Shkodra compensation deadline.** The amended financing agreement set **30 April 2010**; the completion report says the ministry "had complied with this covenant by **May 2010**".
5. **Balakhani waste pickers, what happened to them.** Completion report, 28 February 2019: 144 project-affected people in a 2011 survey; "Most continued employment either safely at the landfill, or with Tamiz Shahar in the sorting facility with benefits, including pension." Independent Evaluation Group field assessment, 13 September 2021: 100 to 110 waste pickers, of whom only 14 accepted the offer and **11** were working at the facility at the time of the mission. Both are in the card.
6. **Jale, when the Panel investigation opened — inside one document.** The Bank's completion report says on page 13 and again in footnote 11 that "On November 1, 2007, the Executive Directors of the World Bank authorized an Inspection Panel (IP) investigation"; paragraph 24 of the same report says "on January 9, 2009, the IP opened an investigation that resulted in a 17-month suspension". The 9 January 2009 date is the suspension of disbursements elsewhere in the same report. The extend uses the 1 November 2007 authorisation and the 17-month suspension lifted in June 2010.
7. **Ukraine, affected-people counts at two dates.** 106 in the original 2012 plan; **124** as of 30 November 2017 (113.8947 ha, UAH 70,245,919); **122** as of 31 October 2018. These are updates, not a conflict: the 2018 addendum explains the movement (three plots avoided on the Poltava bypass, one plot's three heirs paid in equal shares, two more affected people added at Krasnohorivka).
8. **Tajikistan, Istaravshan farm area.** 3,936 ha as printed in the borrower's annex, against 0.92 ha for nine farms at Vakhdat and 0.096–1.9 ha per farm in the Istaravshan plan. Unreconciled in the documents.

## Did the pre-extraction help or mislead?

**It helped, materially, on four rows and it was the reason three rows got read properly at all.** The flag "filed as a plan but reads as an outcome document" on `RP_2014-10-01` (Albania) and `RP_2014-10-30`/`RP_2016-06-23` (Turkey gas) pointed straight at the three documents that carry the actual outcomes in this batch. The plan-reprint table did its job on Bulgaria: the 0.99 ha and 13 plots the Evaluation Group prints as an outcome are the 2014 plan's figures reprinted, which is exactly what the check is for, and checking the verb then led to the completion report sentence that settled the drop.

**What it did not surface, and should have:**

1. **The strongest finding in the batch was not in any candidate list.** The four households and 17 people at Tuz Gölü (P093765) appear in paragraph 95 of the Bank's main text, which no pattern caught. The brief's only hit on "17" came through the plan-reprint check, pairing the borrower annex's "17 persons" against the *page number* "Page 17" in a 2005 land acquisition plan — a false pair that happened to point at the right paragraph for the wrong reason. A pattern for "notified about physical displacement", "vacate the land" and "informal residents" would have found it directly.
2. **The Bank's own admissions of non-compliance went unflagged.** P112578's "Resettlement in some sub-projects took place without a Resettlement Action Plan" and P078933's "The project was non-compliant with the Involuntary Resettlement Safeguard Policy … at several points" are both limb (b) harms in one sentence, and neither appeared in its brief. The P078933 brief had a "resettlement non-compliance" bucket available — it fired on Bulgaria's *forward-looking risk* paragraph instead, and on nothing in Albania. Pattern worth adding: "non-compliant with", "noncompliance", "without a Resettlement Action Plan", "took place without".
3. **Safeguards annexes are where the detail is, and they are not parsed.** P078933's whole case sits in Annex 10 and P086807's compensation outcome in Annex 4. Both are the Bank's own annexes, not the borrower's, so the existing borrower-annex flag does not cover them.
4. **The counter-clause that saved a drop was in a candidate list and is worth keeping there.** The Turkey P112578 brief's "eviction, or people never traced" bucket surfaced the old woman's burnt house at Çamoluk — and the same sentence says the fire "was not caused by the company", that the house "was not located in the project area", and that the company repaired it anyway. That is a candidate sentence doing the opposite of over-matching, and it stopped a false card.
5. **The over-matching the brief warned about was real but harmless.** "Police" fired on the arrest of nine officials in a 2007 procurement scandal (P107833) and on road-safety text; "replacement cost" fired on policy recitals in five of twelve rows. Both were obvious on sight.
