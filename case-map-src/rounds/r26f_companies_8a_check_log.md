# Independent check of r26f_companies_8a

Every entry with a non-empty desc was checked by opening at least one source directly
(not relying on the writer's citation). 60 keys in, 60 out. 52 descs checked; 24 changed,
0 blanked outright (the 7 blanks in the output are the writer's own 7, all confirmed as
honest blanks). Blocked hosts are named where they cost a fact.

## Entries changed

- **PT Wilmar Sambas Plantation and PT Agronusa Investama (PT ANI)** — "Singapore-listed Wilmar International" was invented: the RSPO ASA-1.3 report gives only a Singapore office address ("56 Neil Road Singapore") and says nothing about a listing. Removed; added the earlier name PT Bantanan Eka Jaya and the exact certified area 8,391.61 ha (assessed 9–13 Aug 2022), both from the same report.
- **Rio Algom** — correct as written; added the April 1975 renaming and "revenue from continuing operations", both from the Form 40-F (sec.gov/Archives).
- **Proactiva Medio Ambiente** — all claims stood; added "created in 1999" from the FCC release.
- **SN Power** — "wholly owned by Norfund" is more than the page says. The Scatec release says Scatec will "acquire 100% of the shares in SN Power AS from Norfund" but never states Norfund's pre-deal holding. Rewritten as the share sale, with the agreement dated 16 October 2020 (the release's dateline) and the net capacity 0.5 GW added.
- **Rio Tinto (SIMFER)** and **RTZ (Rio Tinto)** — headcount wrong-footed: the 2022 Form 20-F gives 52,000 employees for managed operations at 31 December 2022 and 54,000 in its business overview. Both entries now use the dated 52,000 figure and the note records the conflict. SimFer's 85/15 and 53/47 splits confirmed word for word in the Engineering News page.
- **Plantar S.A.** — two overclaims. The CDM project page does not say the pig iron mill is Plantar's own ("its own pig iron mill" → "a pig iron mill") and refers to "planted forests", not eucalyptus. The eucalyptus, the February 1967 founding by Gualter de Moura Alves and the 35 million seedlings a year all come from the Diário do Comércio article, which was opened and confirms them.
- **Zoomlion Ghana Ltd** — "household and bulk waste collection" is not on the company page; replaced with its own service list (solid waste collection, waste transfer stations, landfill site management, plastic recycling, waste recovery). The Fourth Estate article settles the payment year: "In 2023, Zoomlion was again paid GHC288.8 million to evacuate 250 refuse dumpsites in 78 districts" (covering services provided in 2022). "45 companies" confirmed exactly, not "about 45".
- **Shwe Taung Cement Co. Ltd** — "selling nationwide" turned out to be supported ("distributes its cement all over Myanmar"); added the plant location, Thazi District, Mandalay region, from the same Summary of Investment Information.
- **Salini Impregilo (now Webuild)** — "Italian construction group" replaced with the release's own "headquartered in Italy", and the project list widened to the one the FY2024 release gives (railways, metros, roads, bridges, tunnels, dams, hydropower, water treatment).
- **Salini Costruttori (later Salini Impregilo, now Webuild)** — the classic parent-for-subsidiary fault, in reverse. The Webuild release footer says only "a Company subject to direction and coordination of Salini Costruttori S.p.A."; it does not call Salini Costruttori a parent, state its sector or give any size. "Italian construction company" and "is the parent" removed; the desc now describes Webuild and says plainly what the filing says about Salini Costruttori.
- **Tanzania Electric Supply Company (TANESCO)** — "It holds all of the country's electricity transmission activity" was not found in the EWURA report; removed. The 91% of installed capacity and 99.50% of customers as of June 2024 are quoted there verbatim.
- **Pizzorno Environnement** — "for French towns and rural areas" invented; removed. Family ownership, Euronext Paris compartment C, ticker GPE, ISIN FR0010214064, €264.6m 2023 revenue and "près de 2 500 personnes" all confirmed in the Actusnews release.
- **Titan Cement Company** — the cited Athens Exchange listing document (athens.euronext.com, and its athexgroup.gr and titan-cement.com/titanmaterials.com equivalents) refused automated fetches, so "fly ash and mortars" and "cement plants in nine countries as of 2019" had no opened source and were removed. Re-sourced to the International Directory of Company Histories entry, which confirms the 1902 founding at Elefsis by the brothers Nicholaos and Angelos Canellopoulos, the first cement plant in Greece, the product range, and the listing on the Athens Stock Exchange "since 1912".
- **Raiffeisen Bank International** — the "eight regional Raiffeisen banks" claim checked out: the page reads "61.17% of Raiffeisen Bank International shares is owned by 8 regional Raiffeisen banks". Dating corrected: the page dates only the €226bn total assets to 30.09.2026 and draws customers and staff from its Q3 2026 fact sheet; the free float 38.83% added.
- **Seli** — the TunnelTalk article gives no sale date or price ("financial terms were not disclosed"), so "In 2015 it restructured and sold" became a restructuring "reported in May 2015", and the arm is stated as now owned by Grandi Lavori Fincosit rather than as sold in 2015.
- **Sunbird Bioenergy** — the release names the seller only as "AOG", never "Addax & Oryx Group"; corrected, and AOG identified as the release does (an investment group founded in 1987 by Jean Claude Gandur).
- **SEMOS** — two date faults. The Engineering News report refers to a "transaction agreement entered into in December 2019", not an announcement in December 2019; and it gives the completion statement as "issued on December 31" with no year (the report itself is dated 4 January 2021). It also gives no post-sale percentages, only the government's further 2% (1% from each miner), so the 20% is now stated that way.
- **SONACOS** — "the country's main peanut processor" is not in the JETRO profile; removed. The five sites, 900,000 t crushing capacity, ~150,000 t of crude oil and 3,000+ workers are all there, with the data collected in financial year 2008-09.
- **Zambia Consolidated Copper Mines (ZCCM)** — figure attached to the wrong story. The SciELO journal paper gives no date for an Anglo American purchase and instead describes "the disinvestment of Anglo from the Copperbelt", so "the main packages going to Anglo American by the end of March 2000" was removed. Neither opened page says ZCCM-IH holds the state's remaining minority interests; replaced with the documented facts — ZCCM-IH incorporated in 2000 and the August 2015 transfer of the government's 60.3% to the Industrial Development Corporation.
- **Westinghouse Electric** — the only source was Grace's Guide, a user-edited wiki. Britannica (opened) confirms the 1995 CBS purchase for US$5.4bn and that in 1997 Westinghouse "dropped its own corporate name and assumed the name CBS Corporation". The BNFL-1998 and Toshiba-2006 nuclear chain was removed: wiki-only, the date is doubtful, and the SEC filings under CIK 106413 (which CBS Corporation kept using) do not mention it. The 1989 ABB and 1996 Northrop Grumman disposals are kept but rest on that wiki alone.
- **Roan Antelope Mining Corporation of Zambia (RAMCOZ)** — the source calls the company "Roan Antelope Copper Mines Limited (RAMCOZ)", so that name is now in the desc rather than only the note; "took on 6,200 employees" corrected to "took over 6,200 employees" and the mines described as passing to "new owners, Luanshya Copper Mines" as the article has it.
- **Unión Fenosa** — the company history gives no year for the 24,772 employees, so that is now said explicitly; "generation and distribution" simplified to "electricity company" because the history describes diversified operations. The April 2009 close and the Gas Natural Fenosa name are confirmed in El Independiente.
- **Águas de Portugal** — "13 bodies managing multimunicipal water and wastewater systems" corrected to the source's "13 entidades gestoras de sistemas multimunicipais e parcerias" (systems *and partnerships*).
- **Tanahu Hydropower Limited** — "state-owned Nepal Electricity Authority" not stated on the pages read; the ADB project administration manual says only that THL "is a subsidiary of NEA". The state-ownership wording was dropped; the 140 MW and the Companies Act incorporation as an SPV are quoted there.
- **Sojitz Corporation** — "Japanese trading company listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange (ticker 2768)" rested on a market-data aggregator. The JPX corporate governance report (opened) gives "As a general trading company, Sojitz Group is engaged in a wide range of business on a global scale", securities code 2768 and the Prime Market segment, but never names the exchange; reworded accordingly and the JPX document added as a source.
- **TotalEnergies EP Mozambique Area 1** — "Mozambican oil and gas company" overclaims: the Engineering News page states no country of incorporation for Total E&P Mozambique Area 1 Limitada. Removed.
- **Siemens, Voith Hydro, ABB, Sulzer Escher-Wyss, General Electric Canada, Agra** — ABB's listings corrected from "Nasdaq OMX Stockholm" to the Financial Report's "Nasdaq Stockholm" (the report also notes ABB deregistered from SEC reporting in 2024 and its ADSs trade over the counter); Siemens' segments given as the Annual Report names them (including Siemens Healthineers); AGRA described as the Lexpert article does ("Canadian-based AGRA Inc., one of North America's largest engineering, construction and technology service firms") with the merger agreement dated 16 February 2000. Voith's "more than 155 years" and the quarter-of-world-hydropower line confirmed; Sulzer's 53% in 1966 and full takeover in 1969 confirmed. General Electric Canada left undescribed, as the writer had it.
- **SK Engineering & Construction** — "project development, financing and operation and maintenance" is not in the GlobalData profile; removed, and the technology sector added. The profile never uses the name SK Engineering & Construction in its text, so the rename is implied by the filing name only and the desc now says the profile describes the company under the name SK Ecoplant.
- **S. Alam Group** — attribution fault: the "nearly two dozen" company count is the newspaper's, not the group's website; only the ~Tk1.5 lakh crore asset figure is attributed to the website. Corrected, with the per-sector figures the article gives.
- **SEPCO III Electric Power Construction (PowerChina)** — sourced only to Global Energy Monitor's gem.wiki. The 1985 founding and "wholly-owned subsidiary of Power Construction Corporation of China" were confirmed instead from China Daily, which was opened; the plant-type list (coal, nuclear, gas, hydro, wind, biomass, solar, desalination) and the 66,700 MW installed-capacity figure were removed — wiki-only, and the capacity carried no year.

## Weakest entries I let stand, and why

1. **The Navigator Company** — the single source is StockAnalysis.com, a market-data
   aggregator, not a Navigator filing or the Lisbon exchange. Everything in the desc is on
   that page (1953 founding, Mitrena/Setúbal, NVG, 3,893 employees, the Portucel, S.A.
   former name and the February 2016 change), and the facts are unremarkable, but nothing
   primary was opened. Worth one filing check before it goes on a card.
2. **Sojitz Corporation** — the business line and the listing now come from a JPX
   disclosure, but the ¥2.76 trillion revenue and 26,789 employees still rest on
   StockAnalysis.com. The fiscal year end (31 March 2026) is the aggregator's, not a filing's.
3. **Westinghouse Electric** — the 1989 ABB and 1996 Northrop Grumman disposals are
   supported only by Grace's Guide, a user-edited wiki. I kept them because Britannica
   corroborates the surrounding history (1995 CBS purchase, 1997 renaming) and no source
   contradicts them, but they are one rung below the rest of the batch.
4. **Tokyo Electric Power Services Co.** — the Devex organisation profile is
   organisation-supplied text on a development-sector directory. It is consistent and
   specific (December 1960, TEPCO affiliate, the donor list) but is not a filing or the
   company's own site.
5. **Titan Cement Company** — now rests on the International Directory of Company
   Histories (via Reference for Business), a published reference work but a secondary one,
   because every Titan and Athens Exchange page tried refused automated fetches. Nothing
   current is claimed, which is the safe side of the line.
6. **S. Alam Group** — single source, one newspaper, and the asset figures in it are
   contested within the article itself. Kept because the desc now says who says what.
7. **SK Engineering & Construction** — the identity link between the key's name and
   SK Ecoplant comes from GlobalData's filing name, not from the page's text. Kept, with the
   uncertainty stated in the desc and note, because the profile is unambiguously about the
   Seoul contractor in the case.
