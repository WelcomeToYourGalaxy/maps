# Round 40, batch A — check log

Four cases checked. Nothing cut. All four verdicts are `stands-with-corrections`. I opened sixteen
judgments, two PDFs from disk and one state department page, and ran nine Indian Kanoon searches and
two web searches of my own. Seventeen corrections went in. The one promotion in the batch survives,
but only after its confidence note was rewritten to say what it rests on, and after four factual
errors in it were fixed.

## The three weak calls the writer named

### (a) lucknow-muzaffarpur-highway-land — the promotion holds, with the link stated

The test CHECK40 set was: does any document name the loan and the parcels together? No. I ran the
quoted pair `"World Bank" "National Highway No. 28"` on Indian Kanoon and it returns nothing at all.
`"Lucknow-Muzaffarpur"` returns four copies of one income-tax appeal and two NHAI–Hindustan
Construction arbitration matters. `"Lucknow Muzaffarpur National Highway Project"` returns only the
income-tax appeal. None of the three judgments the writer relies on contains the words World Bank; I
grepped each full text.

So the promotion cannot stand as written. CHECK40 item 2 allows one of two outcomes: downgrade, or
say on the card in terms what the link rests on. I took the second, because the link does not in fact
run through geography alone, and I was able to document more of it than the writer did.

What the chain is, each link from a page I opened:

1. The completion report, downloaded and read from disk. The restructuring cut the corridor from
   483 km to 328 km, "from Lucknow up to the UP-Bihar border", after four poorly performing contracts
   were removed. The Lucknow-to-border length is 358 km "including the 328 km comprising
   bank-financed stretches". The 30 km difference is the Gorakhpur bypass, which NHAI financed itself
   because it was greenfield — and which is in Gorakhpur district, not Barabanki or Basti.
2. Annex 7 of that report, the borrower's own submission, states that the project passes through
   Barabanki, Ayodhya, Basti, Gorakhpur, Kushinagar and Gopalganj, and that of the twelve contracts
   "8 Packages fall in the State of Uttar Pradesh and 4 Packages in the State of Bihar". The four
   removed were the Bihar ones; all eight surviving Bank-financed packages are in UP.
3. Annex 11 lists those eight by length and contract cost. Package II: 47.0 km, Rs 212.34 crore.
4. **The link the writer did not have.** The Allahabad High Court, in NHAI v. Hindustan Construction
   Co. Ltd of 16 October 2025, records that "The project related to the four-laning of National
   Highway No. 28 from Km 45.00 to Km 92.00 of the Lucknow - Ayodhya Section", under a contract with
   HCC executed on 05.10.2005 for Rs 212,33,44,969, and elsewhere calls it Package-2 of the
   Muzaffarpur Road Project. Length and price match Annex 11's Package II. That puts Barabanki inside
   a Bank-financed package on a figure that is not the Bank's.
5. The Sampata Devi appeals place the acquisition under one section 3A notification for kilometre 9
   to 360.57, Lucknow to the UP–Bihar border — the same corridor.

That is works, statute, implementing authority, dates and contract-package chainage. It is still an
inference as to any individual parcel, and the card now says so in those words. The writer's own
doubt about whether these parcels might belong to a dropped stretch I can narrow but not close: the
report gives no location for the up-to-1,800 metres taken out of scope over "complex ownership
issues", but those were ownership disputes, whereas all three judgments are valuation arbitrations
on land where possession had been taken and basic compensation already paid. Different kind of
dispute, so overlap is improbable rather than excluded. I could not find a documented chainage for
any package but Package II, so Basti rests on steps 1 to 3 only.

Four factual errors in the promotion, all found by reading the judgments against the entry:

- **The notifications were conflated.** The confidence note had both Barabanki awards flowing from
  the single notification of 26 May 2004. The Ram Awadh judgment records a *separate* notification for
  the Malinpur parcels, gazetted 1 June 2005, declared under section 3D on 18 October 2005, award
  31 August 2006. The 26 May 2004 notification is the one in Sampata Devi, whose award was
  7 December 2005. Split in the note and in the community text.
- **Eight appeals, not eight — twenty-one.** The source title said "Nos. 53 to 60 of 2023", the report
  said "seven connected appeals", the community text said "Nine Barabanki landholders". The judgment
  of 31 October 2023 decides twenty-one connected appeals, Nos. 53 to 73 of 2023, each with a lead
  respondent and two or more others. This error understates the card.
- **The High Court did not examine the valuation.** It dismissed NHAI's appeals on limitation alone.
  The non-agricultural finding is the arbitrator's, affirmed by the Additional District Judge. The
  card now attributes it there.
- **"Valued it as agricultural" was on the wrong sentence.** The judgment attaches that to the award
  of 7 December 2005; the Ram Awadh judgment does not use the word. Moved.

One more thing worth recording: Sampata Devi names no village. The only locality in it is the
Sub-Registrar's office at Nawabganj, Barabanki. The card does not claim a village for those
landholders and must not acquire one later.

### (b) The Mirzapur section — resolves in the writer's favour, and on a document

The writer placed Mirzapur on Kanpur–Mughalsarai from the CAG stretch name and the geography. There
is a document, and it is one the writer had already opened without noticing the line. In *Ajay Kumar
and Another v. Union of India*, 11 August 2022, counsel for the Railways states that "the total
estimated cost of DFCC project from Pt. Deen Dayal Upadhyaya Junction to Prayagraj Junction is more
than Rs.3,000 crores", and that the petitioners' house at kilometre 155+030 in village Pirallipur,
Tehsil Chunar, District Mirzapur obstructs a 181 km section otherwise almost complete. Prayagraj sits
between Kanpur and Mughalsarai, so the Mughalsarai-to-Prayagraj work is a sub-stretch of
Ludhiana-Khurja-Kanpur-Mughalsarai, and Mirzapur is inside the stretch the Bank financed.

So *Thakur Prasad* and *Ajay Kumar* do not weaken. They strengthen. I put this into the card's first
correction, with its document.

### (c) The CAG statement, and whether the hold is right

The CAG sentence is verbatim correct. From the chapter, read from disk: the Ministry of Railways
approached the Bank in 2008 and, after field visits, meetings and appraisals, the Bank "agreed
(May 2011) to provide fund (US $ 2725 million equivalent to 13,625 crore) only for a stretch of
Ludhiana-Khurja-Kanpur-Mughalsarai (1183 kms) section of Eastern Corridor", first loan agreement
October 2011 for US$975 million, second unsigned as at March 2014. The chapter also names
Sonnagar-Mughalsarai and Durgwati-Karwandia as separate sections in its delay findings, which is what
puts Kaimur outside. The writer's financing-boundary correction holds.

Two qualifications:

- The chapter is not silent on land. It carries no land-acquisition or resettlement finding about
  affected people, but it does find an increase of Rs 4,442 crore in land cost attributed to
  escalation, against a background of alignment changes. Narrowed on the card.
- The same chapter calls one civil and systems contract section "Ludhiana-Khurja Dadri Section", so
  the CAG by itself does not cleanly place the Khurja–Dadri spur inside or outside the 1,183 km. The
  exclusion of Khurja–Dadri rests on the Bank's own documents, which the card already cites. I
  recorded this in the check rather than on the card.

**Is the hold right? Yes, but one of the writer's two reasons falls away and the hold had to be
re-grounded.** Mirzapur is now inside the Bank's stretch on a document, so "the clearest judgments are
on the Dadri spur and the Bihar section" is no longer the whole picture. What replaces it is something
the writer did not weigh: *Thakur Prasad* records a threatened wrong, not a completed one. The court's
finding is that the land had neither been acquired nor sold; the demolition without payment is the
owners' pleading; and the court went on to say the land "is needed for the public purpose" and told
the petitioners to appear before the authorities and settle a price "so that the project may not be
put to halt". That is not the card's harm recorded as done. Add the card's quantities still being the
lender's alone, and the Gautam Budh Nagar and Kaimur findings sitting outside the 1,183 km, and
`contested` is right. If the checker's standard were the harm and not the arithmetic, as the writer
put it, this judgment still would not flip it, because inside the Bank's stretch no judgment I opened
finds the harm done.

Corrections beyond the three weak calls:

- **Jaiveer Singh, "filed objections in time".** The judgment finds that objections were taken and
  never adjudicated. It makes no finding on whether they were within the thirty days section 20D

- **Jaiveer Singh, the inquiry.** The court required *the respondents* to hold an inquiry into the

- **The Madras petition has seventy-four petitioners**, numbered 1 to 74 in the cause title, not

- **Two source titles.** Indian Kanoon serves the Patna bunch under "Binod Kumar Singh v. The Union of

  petitioner 1 in CWJC 6406 of 2014 and Muthusami is petitioner 1 in W.P. 29133 of 2012, so both the
  writer's citations are sound, but the Patna title now carries the index's name so the record can be
  found again.
- **The ITAT contradicts itself on the Dehlon bypass** and the card was passing it on as coherent. The
  order says "km 18+750 to km 20+800 of 2.98 km length". That chainage spans 2.05 km. Flagged in the
  two places the card used it.
- **Three batch candidates the writer neither opened nor named**, which I opened and reject: a Delhi

## The route failure, named

The writer's weak point 4 was right and I can now name the route rather than the host. `curl` to
`prbdb.punjab.gov.in` fails for me too, twice, with "Recv failure: Connection reset by peer" (error
35), on HTTP/2 and on forced HTTP/1.1 with TLS 1.2. The agent proxy's own status endpoint records all
five attempts, mine and the writer's, as `ws_closed_mid_exchange`: "tunnel closed (code 1006) after
16s; 517 B sent, 39 B received, client reading". **The failing route is the agent proxy's tunnel to
that host.** It is not a 403, not a policy denial and not a refusal by the host, which serves the same
URL through a fetch tool — I confirmed the page's thirteen-item link list that way and it matches the
writer's description exactly, including that no link text is dated and none mentions the World Bank.

Because a fetch-tool summary is not a document opened, I moved the two board items out of
`documents_opened` on the Dehlon card and left them only in `routes_that_failed`. The writer put no
figure, name or quote from them into the card, which was the right call. Until someone can reach that
host with a downloader, the award announcement's date and the unclaimed schedule's plots and
co-owners stay out of reach, and with them the one route by which that card could become documented.

## What a promotion to `documented` would still need, per contested card

No promotion was cut this round, so this is not a list of what I took away. It is what each of the
three cards still at `contested` is missing, stated so the next round does not have to rediscover it.

### eastern-dedicated-freight-corridor

The closest card to promotion in the batch, and it needs one of two things.

1. **A finding, inside the Bank's 1,183 km, that the harm happened rather than was threatened.**
   Everything I opened inside Ludhiana–Khurja–Kanpur–Mughalsarai stops short of that. *Thakur Prasad*
   (Mirzapur) finds the land unacquired and entry unlawful *if* it happens, then tells the owners to
   settle a price. *Vikas Chandra Kanaujia* and *Panchu Ram Yadav* (Chandauli) find money and
   Second Schedule benefits not yet released under an award of 1 June 2023 — strong, but a delay
   recorded while an enquiry runs, with the state saying it will pay. *Bhola Nath Singh* finds a jobs
   representation undecided, not refused. What would do it: a later order in any of these matters
   recording non-payment as final or the enquiry as concluded against the landholders; an order on the
   Chandauli jobs claims recording that the Railway Board policy was applied and the job refused; or a
   demolition or dispossession found as fact in Chandauli or Mirzapur. The Chandauli awards are recent
   enough that follow-on orders are likely to exist by now — *Panchu Ram Yadav* is April 2025 — so
   searching those case numbers forward is the cheapest next move.
2. **Any outside count.** Nothing outside the lender touches the 85,000 project-affected people, the
   4,336 hectares, the 265 households, or the number paid at pre-2013 rates. A state revenue return, a
   Railway Board answer in Parliament, or a CAG state report with a district-level acquisition table
   would do what CAG Report No. 48 of 2015 does not — that chapter's only land content is cost.

Also still open: the Cheeto village sit-in of 25 February 2016 and the hunger strike of 11 July 2016
rest on one wire report of a political party's claims. A Hindi-language Chandauli press search is the
obvious untried route, and neither the writer nor I ran one.

### nagapattinam-canal-demolition-tsunami-rebuild

This card needs the demolition itself, from anywhere outside the Bank. The harm is stated plainly in
paragraph 53 of the lender's own report and nowhere else that either of us could find. What would
earn the promotion, in rough order of likelihood:

- A Tamil Nadu state audit or Accountant General report on the Emergency Tsunami Reconstruction
  Project with a Nagapattinam waterworks or R&R finding.
- A Tamil-language press report of the demolition. This is the single biggest untried route on the
  card and it is untried by both of us.
- A government order or district collector's proceeding on the retrofit cash grants to the six
  families, which would name the village and the stretch the lender never names.
- The PWD (Water Resources Organisation) papers for the ten de-silting and bank-strengthening works
  over 122.6 km in Nagapattinam district, which the completion report records as completed in 2009
  and which are the likeliest home for the canal work.

Note what is *not* needed: the canal's name is not the obstacle. The geographic oddity — a Buckingham
canal in Nagapattinam, when the Buckingham canal runs north of there — is the lender's own, and the
card already says so. Searching the canal name has now failed twice and should not be repeated as the
primary route.

### dehlon-bypass-uncompensated-landowner

This card could become `documented` in one sitting by anyone who can download from
`prbdb.punjab.gov.in`. The board publishes the acquisition file for this very bypass under the 2013
land law: an announcement of award under section 23, a notice regarding compensation, a second
unclaimed-compensation notice, and approved and draft rehabilitation plans for the Dehlon–Rurka
stretch. If the award announcement carries a date and the unclaimed schedule names the plot and its
co-owners, that is the harm on the card — a co-owner unpaid — recorded by the implementing body itself
rather than by the Bank's task team leader speaking to evaluators.

What stands in the way is the route, not the documents: the agent proxy's tunnel to that host closes
mid-exchange, so the PDFs cannot be put through `pdftotext`, and a fetch-tool summary is not the
document. Whoever takes this on needs a working downloader for that host.

Failing that, the High Court case the Independent Evaluation Group describes — filed May 2017, heard
31 January 2018 — would do it. It is not in Indian Kanoon under the bypass name, the project name or
the board's name. The Punjab and Haryana High Court's own case-status service, searched by the board
as respondent for 2017, is the untried route.
