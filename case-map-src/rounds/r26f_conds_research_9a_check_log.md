## The ICICI group ruling (seven entries, all blanked)

SPI 25492 is real and was re-read: a 15-year subordinated Upper Tier II loan of US$150 million, approved
21 December 2006, signed 22 December 2006, Category FI. The requirement is there but the entries quoted it
without its hedge: the page says "Commit to formalize, **if required**, the Social and Environmental Management
System (SEMS)", with supplemental actions "prior to disbursement that is satisfactory to IFC". Two things sink
the entries anyway. First, use of proceeds: the money supports "the Bank's capital requirements to finance
growth, especially in key areas such as rural banking and microfinance" - regulatory capital, not an earmarked
facility that reaches projects. Second, the link: Inclusive Development International's own report, fetched this
round, describes ICICI's lending to these companies as general corporate purposes or refinancing, does not
mention NTPC, Adani, Essar, NHPC or Zuari/Nuziveedu at all in the Africa volume, and states its basis plainly -
the banks were IFC clients and IFC does not disclose end use. It never claims IFC money reached any of these
loans. That is failure 4: a sound requirement from a different loan.

## The Nedbank ruling (two entries, one survives)

Same facility, two different outcomes, which is the point. For `siguiri-area-one` the lender's own accountability
mechanism registered a complaint under project 26014 about the 2015 Area One resettlement and called the mine
operator IFC's sub-client; that is a documented step from intermediary to project, so the entry stands.
For `trident-kalumbila-resettlement` there is no such case, IDI records Nedbank's May 2014 US$142 million share of
a US$3 billion revolving credit facility as being "for general corporate purposes" and the May 2016 loan as a
refinancing, neither tied to Trident or Kalumbila, and both postdate the 2011 start of the resettlement.

**On the ZAR 1 billion match the writer offered as evidence: it is a coincidence of nothing - it is the same
single IFC facility quoted in both cases.** CAO's page and the SPI both give project 26014 as "up to ZAR1billion
($139 million equivalent)", and IDI gives the same facility as US$140-141 million. The match confirms which IFC
project number is meant. It says nothing about whether the money reached either mine, and it cannot, because
there is only one loan in it.

## Ruling: `sendou-bargny-coal`

Resting the entry on the Corrective Action Plan alone would be failure 3 in substance, with one qualification.
The plan was drafted on 24 April 2014 after the residents' complaints and a joint site visit, and the FMO panel
report also records that the second disbursement "did not materialise in 2014" and was eventually made in
October 2015 with "None of these outstanding issues ... solved before the second disbursement was made in 2015".
So the plan did not in fact gate the money. The qualification is that the report does say, in terms, "This was
sufficient for the AfDB to approve the 2nd disbursement" - so it is not pure recommendation, and it is kept in
the entry worded exactly that way, as something the report states about an approval decision rather than as a
covenant. The entry's weight has been moved to a genuine term of the financing, which the same report supplies:
the Common Terms Agreement dated 19 December 2012, signed by FMO, the other lenders including the AfDB, the
project company and the sponsor, "required compliance with all IFC PS and completion of the items addressed in
the ESAP". The report is dated 12 October 2017, not 2020; the source line was corrected.

The "contains no tariff condition" claim cannot stand on what is readable. Two fetches of the project paper both

## Finding: `kalangala-oil-palm` (case data not changed)

The `wb` lender key is wrong. A World Bank projects API query for Uganda matching Kalangala, oil palm or
vegetable oil returns `"total":"0"`. IFAD's own independent evaluation of the Vegetable Oil Development Project,
fetched this round, gives the financing as an IFAD loan of US$20 million approved in April 1997, US$33.1 million
of private sector cofinancing, US$3.8 million from the Government of Uganda and US$3.1 million from
beneficiaries, later rising to about US$156 million as the private investor and government increased their
shares. No World Bank or IDA financing is named. The World Bank appears as the **cooperating institution** -
supervision, not finance - from the start until August 2004, when UNOPS took over, and it withdrew because it
"feared that the expanded oil palm project would not comply" with its forestry safeguards policies. The lender of
record is IFAD, with the private investor and the Government. The entry's `searched` line was extended to record
this; the case's lender keys were left alone as instructed.

6. `amaravati-land-pooling` — the live loan is from November 2024, at the very end of the case's span.

8. `green-resources-uganda-evictions` and `agrokasa-ica-aquifer` — right loan, wrong place: the required items concern a Tanzanian sawmill and the Barranca/Supe assets, not the Ugandan evictions or the Ica aquifer. Both entries say so.
