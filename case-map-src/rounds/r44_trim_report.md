## The three residuals

**1. `banks-that-bank-the-looters`: "None of them was prosecuted."** Removed, and replaced with what
the entry's own source says. I opened the WealthBriefing report of 9 March 2001 myself. The FSA's
managing director Phillip Thorpe is quoted there saying potential breaches of the money laundering
regulations "are also being discussed with the appropriate law enforcement authorities", and the
report adds "These include the police and the National Criminal Intelligence Service". That now sits
as the last clause of the sentence that already carries the regulator's other three caveats, so the
card no longer holds an unsourced negative and the paragraph gains nothing it cannot show. No quote
was added: the entry already uses its one WealthBriefing allowance in paraphrase, and the
quote-per-source rule is unchanged across the entry.

**2. `water-handed-to-companies` paragraph 2, the apparent contradiction.** Both halves stay. The
joining sentence now says why they are not in conflict, and it rests on a source already on the card:
ICIJ's account of the Jakarta loan, which states from World Bank loan documents that the Bank was
encouraging Indonesia to privatise its utilities, that it expected the loans to make the waterworks a
more attractive investment, and that it appointed the consultants who advised PAM Jaya on the
privatisation. Against that the Bank's completion report says the particular handover was not
envisaged at appraisal. The new sentence reads: the Bank was encouraging privatisation and expected
this loan to make the waterworks a more attractive investment, so its money and its advisers smoothed
the way for a handover it had not itself designed. No new source is needed.

**3. `aid-cover-for-spies`, the three country notes.** One re-sourced as written, one re-sourced with
a date dropped, one recommended off.

- **Brazil: re-source.** The National Security Archive's briefing book 324 says Dan Mitrione "was
  hired by the U.S. AID Office of Public Safety to train policemen in Brazil and Uruguay" in the mid
  1960s. That carries the note. The second half of the new note is the Congressional Record item
  already in the entry's sources, which is the only thing in it that mentions Brazil.
- **Uruguay: re-source, with "from 1964" dropped.** The same briefing book has Mitrione as "Director
  of the U.S. AID Office of Public Safety (OPS) in Uruguay and the main American advisor to the
  Uruguayan police", killed on 10 August 1970 after ten days held by MLN-Tupamaro insurgents, and has
  the OPS consolidating the Uruguayan police's intelligence directorate under him. It gives no start
  year, so 1964 comes off rather than being carried on a page that does not say it.
- **Guatemala: the note as written should come off.** "Largest Office of Public Safety programme in
  Central America" is a comparative superlative and nothing I could open ranks the country
  programmes. In its place I offer a note from the Guatemalan National Police Historical Archive's own
  report, Del Silencio a la Memoria, volume 1: declassified US documents record the Public Safety
  Program in Guatemala beginning in 1956, OPS advice shaped coordination between the security forces
  in the 1960s, and USAID's own five-year analysis of the programme covers 1967 to 1971. The owner can
  take the replacement or leave the country off; what should not happen is the superlative staying.

Two sources are added for those notes, both opened by me: the National Security Archive briefing book
and the Nizkor copy of the police archive report. One thing to flag for the owner: the police archive
dates Guatemala's Public Safety Program to 1956, before the Office of Public Safety existed, so the
replacement note says Public Safety Program for the 1950s start and OPS for the 1960s advice.

## My weakest call

Replacing the Guatemala superlative instead of simply deleting the country. The instruction was to
re-source or recommend removal, and the honest answer for the claim as written is removal. Offering a
different, sourced note for the same country is one step past that: the new note says less than the
old one and is not what the old one claimed, so if the owner reads `countries` as a fixed set of
claims rather than a per-country line, the right outcome is to drop GTM. I have written the
recommendation so either choice is one edit away.

Second weakest: dropping "Only leaks and occasional inquiries have shown any of it" from the banks
card's paragraph 4. It carries no record, but it was doing a small job, telling a novice how little of
this is visible, and it went for 10 words.
