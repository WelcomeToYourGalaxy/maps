# Round 40, batch B — check log

Four cases. One promotion to `documented` survived with corrections, two `contested` entries stand with
corrections, one absence stands. I opened every document the writer cited — all ten judgments, both
Auditor-General reports, both completion reports and the environmental assessment — and re-ran every
index query. Nothing was cut outright. What I did cut is inside the entries: three claims stated as
settled that the documents only record as pleaded, one audit opinion described as cleaner than it is,
and one over-read snippet.

## (a) assam-roads-uncompensated-acquisition — `documented` stands, with corrections

The writer asked whether `documented` survives a match made through the works. It does, and not for the
reason the writer feared. The link is not geography: two of the three orders name the lender's operation
in words. *Hakim Mullah* (WP(C) 2114/2021, 23 March 2022) puts the petitioner's 4 kathas 7 lechas at
Jahorupam inside the double-laning of State Highway No. 2 "under the Assam State Road Project from
Langla to Barpeta Town" in LA Case 1/2011-12, and records that the award was made and the compensation
"has not yet been disbursed" to either the pattadars or the possessors, for want of no-objection
certificates, a position the Land Acquisition Officer's letter of 27 August 2018 confirms. *Saidul
Islam* (WP(C) 5699/2024) describes the same works in the same terms in LA Case 9/2011-2012. The
completion report's Table 4.1, which I read myself, lists SH-02 Chapaguri–Manas–Barpeta at 60.52 km as
one of six upgrading roads. So a court names the project, the stretch, the acquisition case and the
parcel in one document, and records the owners unpaid. That is CHECK40's test — "whether a document
names the loan and the parcels together" — met at the level of the project rather than the loan number,
which here is the same operation under its own name. Loan IBRD-81360 appears nowhere, and the card now
says so.

Three corrections, all cutting:

1. **Not three orders, two.** *Amzad Hussain* (WP(C) 5103/2016) names no highway number and no
   acquisition case. Paragraph 2 calls the taking "for construction of National Highway"; paragraphs 5
   and 7 call it "for construction of State Highway". What ties it to this project is its third
   respondent, the Project Director, Assam State Roads Project. The writer's confidence note had all
   three dealing with the SH-2 upgrading. That is now split, and the card says which order rests on
   which.
2. **Saidul Islam decided nothing.** The court recorded as admitted that the petitioner and his
   siblings were off the beneficiary list of 5 September 2020 and that the whole compensation for their
   1 katha 5 lessas had gone to the seven children of the pattadar's late brother. It then held that the
   petitioner "must first establish that he is a person interested", noted that he could have objected
   when the 1894 Act notifications were published in 2012 and 2013, and refused to entertain the writ.
   The writer's card asserted the omission and the misdirected payment as established harm. The
   admitted facts stay; the entitlement does not.
3. **Super Infratech carries nothing.** WP(C) 6047/2015 is a Sivasagar district contractor's matter
   disposed of in two sentences on the petitioner's own submission that it had become infructuous. It
   has no connection to State Highway 2. It stays only for the form of the project's name in its cause
   title, and the card now says that is all it is for.

Also corrected: Saidul Islam's plot is at Kandulia in Kalgachia Revenue Circle, Bongaigaon district,
"earlier, District - Barpeta" in the order's own words; the card had Barpeta flat. And the Assam quote
was 17 words; trimmed to "double lining of existing carriageway under the Assam State Road Project".

**The counter-argument, named.** A checker could demote this. The Bank's harm is on *one package* it
suspended and then said it had paid for. None of these three claimants is shown to be on that package,
and the cleanest instance of owners acquired from and paid nothing — *Amzad Hussain* — is the one order
whose project link rests on a respondent's name. I did not demote because the card's harm is land taken
for this project without paying the owners, and *Hakim Mullah* carries exactly that, by name, with the
parcel and the acquisition case, five years after the award. If the standard is identity with the Bank's
package, nobody can ever meet it, because the Bank withheld the package.

## (b) nhiip-india-corridor-land-acquisition — the financing chain, resolved further than the writer got

**P121100 versus P121185: resolved in the writer's favour.** The file name is an artefact. The Bank's
own catalogue record for the assessment carries `projn` "IN-National Highways Interconnectivity
Improvement Project -- P121185", and a query for `proid=P121100` returns a Bank paper on smart subsidies
in rural finance. There is no ambiguity to carry.

**The transfer question, and a document the writer did not find.** The Bank's **Procurement Plan for
Phase I of NHIIP, dated 24 September 2013**, headed as covering projects "being implemented with World
Bank loan assistance", lists at works package 12 the "Rehabilitation and Upgarding of Birpur - Bihpur
Section of NH 106", Rs 435.89 crore, international competitive bidding, prequalification yes, **Bank
prior review**, bid opening expected 7 March 2014 — and, in its third table, a consultant or NGO to
implement the resettlement action plan for the same section. Its fifteen works packages divide into five
item-rate and ten EPC contracts, exactly the split Table 2 of the completion report gives, and NH-106 is
one of the ten. So the road was a procurement package of this loan under the Bank's own prior review,
and the resettlement work on it was procured under the loan too. The writer's "the money is not traced"
was too strong and I narrowed it: what is open is only which operation paid after the 2020 transfer.

I held the card at `contested` anyway. The verdict vocabulary CHECK40 gives me has no upgrade, and
promoting a card on a document I found is writer-work, not checking. **Flagged for the writer: the next
pass should decide whether the procurement plan plus CWJC 13438 of 2024 earns `documented`.** My own
reading is that it probably does — the Patna High Court records as fact, not as pleading, that an
arbitration award for NH-106 land reached execution, that the executing court ordered Rs 3,66,61,312 paid
within fifteen days on 8 February 2024, and that "till date, the petitioner has not been accorded the
award amount", with the Nodal Officer, NHIIP among the respondents and the parcel in Madhepura, which the
Bank's assessment puts at km 70.8 of the 106 km it covers.

**Two claims cut.** The writer wrote that "at least one owner's land was taken and the road built with
no compensation paid to him". In CWJC 15126 of 2016 those words are the relief sought, set out by the
court from the petition; the court decided nothing, granted liberty to go to a civil court, and recorded
that the writ had been pending nine years. Same for CWJC 10131 of 2026, where the
commercial-versus-residential valuation is the petitioner's case. Both now read as pleadings.

**Two corrections to the lender's document.** The completion report cannot be right about the roads left
unfinished: its safeguards paragraph says "the remaining five roads in the state of Bihar and West
Bengal" were transferred, while its economic annex says "In 2020, eight roads were completed, and the
remaining seven roads are projected to complete". And the eight it names are the eight in its economic
analysis, not all its works — its own table counts fifteen contracts over 1,071 km, 916 km done at
closure, 155 km unfinished. The procurement plan names all fifteen, over NH 98, 217, 113, 11B, 200, 60A,
234, 201, 30A, 106 and 104. I also recorded the chainage inconsistency: four documents give km 0 to 106,
the Mishra petition gives km 0.00 to 136.00, and the Bank's assessment calls the same 106 km
Birpur–Udakishanganj rather than Birpur–Bihpur.

The ministry's project title was quoted at about 25 words; trimmed.

## (c) kisip-informal-settlements-fatalities — snippet marking, and an audit finding the writer missed

Every Kenyan claim checks out as snippet-grounded, and the card does say the judgment text was
unreachable. Counts re-run and matched exactly: "KISIP" 44, "Kenya Informal Settlements Improvement
Project" 21, "Kenya Informal Settlements" 33, "Kopere Market" 1, "Majengo Talai" 2, "Majengo/Talai
Settlement Scheme" 1. Each quoted fragment is in the API response — including "lists of squatters in
Majengo/Talai Settlement Scheme prepared by KISIP", which the writer was right about: it is returned for
the KISIP query, not the Majengo query, and the same response carries "which families were recommended
by the KISIP". Nothing is stated as if a judgment had been read.

Three re-markings:

- "A case the index shows turning on …" overstates what a highlighted fragment can show. A snippet
  shows a phrase occurs. The card now says so, and says the ruling's subject and outcome are unknown.
- The 2018 Obul identification. The index title reads "v Kenya Informal Settlements & 4 others". The
  project as first respondent, the Kisumu station and the constitutional-petition form all come from the
  cause-title fragment the snippet carries, and "same dispute as the 2019 ruling" is an inference from
  the shared petitioners. The card now attributes each of those to the snippet.
- "Three relevant judgments" is four.

**The correction that matters.** The FY2020 audit — the closing year — is not simply unqualified. It
carries an **Emphasis of Matter**: the statement of receipts and payments excludes "amounts withdrawn
but unclaimed" of **USD 12,998,243.20, equivalent to Kshs 1,335,530,325**, at 30 June 2020, being money
transferred into the project's bank account whose expenditure returns had not reached the National
Treasury by the year's close. The opinion is not modified on it. The writer described the report as
unqualified and silent, and it is silent on deaths and compensation, but it is not silent on the money.

Every FY2022 figure verified against the document: Kshs 15,203,347 of goods and services, of which Kshs
12,505,486 domestic travel and subsistence, Kshs 478,850 of imprest paid on 28 and 30 June 2022 without
surrenders, Kshs 1,519,600 paid on 24 December 2021 as subsistence allowance for unidentified officers
attending infrastructure inspection meetings. Both reports name IDA 4873-KE, AFD CKE 1055 01 J and SIDA
TF-018327 dated 23 May 2011, 17 December 2015 and 7 July 2015, so the dates the writer attributed to the
FY2020 report are in both. Searched both for "fatality", "death", "deceased": no occurrence in either.

**Writer-work I declined to do, named.** The FY2022 report's management narrative says the projects
"adhered to the provisions of the Occupational Safety and Health Act of 2007 … and have enforced the
same in construction and office environments", and that the environmental and social framework "has
facilitated resettlement of project affected persons", while saying nothing about any death. That is a
state document asserting safety compliance over the years that contained six fatalities. It belongs on
this card or it does not, and that is a writer's call, so it sits in
`what_i_could_not_verify` for the writer to pick up rather than in the card text.

**Routes.** I reproduced the judgment-page failure myself — HTTP 202, content-length 0,
`x-amzn-waf-action: challenge`, via CloudFront — and did not attempt the challenge. I also found and
recorded that there is **no second route**: the alternate Kenya Law hostname answers the same challenge
on both the judgment path and its listing path, and the document-detail API paths return 404 because the
search API serves result pages and not document bodies. That turns the writer's "the route failed" into
"there is no route", which is a different and more useful finding.
