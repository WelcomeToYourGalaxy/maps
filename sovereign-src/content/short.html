The short answer

No route on this map gives a private group its own territory under its own laws today. The highest estimate for any route is under 2%, and most are under 0.1%. These figures are judgments and no study measures them. The list of places can be ordered by this figure.

What the routes and combinations show

  • Empty places: the high seas, the seabed, Antarctica and space are closed to claims by treaty. Land that no state claims is reachable only through states that do.
  • Buying land: a purchase gives ownership, not the right to rule. Only a government can give that up, and none has handed it to a private buyer in modern times.
  • Mercenaries: nearly every mercenary attempt ended in arrests. Bob Denard's years in the Comoros needed outside backing and a recognised president, and they still ended.
  • War: states are barred by treaty from taking land by force, and a private army has no legal claim to what it takes. William Walker ran Nicaragua through a president he chose from October 1855, surrendered in May 1857, and was executed in Honduras in 1860. Rebel groups have run courts and taxes for years, among them the Houthis and al-Shabaab, and each of them grew from people already living there. The figures for a private group are 0.3% for holding rebel territory and 0.1% for conquest.
  • Recognition: it has gone to peoples on land they already held, through secession or war, and not to founders of new places.
  • Ships, digital nations and tolerated micronations: these can last for decades because the law of a real state still applies to them. They give a way of living and not a territory.
  • Combinations: each layer of paperwork still traces back to a state that can withdraw it. Adding an investment treaty can win money after a reversal, but a tribunal cannot give the zone back.

The best realistic path

  1. Find a government willing to pass a law creating a zone with its own commercial rules, courts and tax. The Dubai International Financial Centre has run this way since 2004, with its courts working since 2006.
  2. Offer something the host wants, usually investment and jobs, and ask for less than full government. The zones that have lasted ask for less.
  3. Protect the deal where it can be protected: in the constitution rather than an ordinary law, and with investment treaty rights. Honduras had put its zones in the constitution, and its top court still voided them in 2024, so this lowers the risk without removing it.
  4. Accept what stays with the host: sovereignty, criminal law, immigration in most cases, and the power to end the deal.

That path gives separate business law and courts on a defined piece of land. It does not give the full result this map asks about, and it is the reason the host-granted zone has the highest figure, at 1.5%.

The routes with much better odds of full self-rule all start from an existing people with a real movement: secession at about 10%, a frozen ceasefire zone at about 8%, and the protection of a foreign army at about 6%. A founder cannot create that people. Someone who mainly wants to live among like-minded people and be left alone has easier options, such as a flagged ship or a private community, under another state's law.
