How people are ranked, and what the numbers mean

Five evidence tiers. Each person is placed by what stands behind what they said, not by how believable the claim sounds.

Tier 1 — an official record (budget law, audit report, court ruling, government statement) or authenticated original documents.

Tier 2 — a named insider with first-hand access, speaking under oath or with documents, and partly confirmed by others.

Tier 3 — a named insider speaking from first-hand access, not yet confirmed.

Tier 4 — second-hand accounts: researchers reading public data, journalists citing unnamed sources.

Tier 5 — anonymous claims, claims contradicted by records, or claims with nothing anyone can check.

Within a tier, people are ordered by how much of their account has been confirmed and how direct their access was.

Numbers are never added together. A classified budget (money approved in secret), an audit finding (money the books cannot account for) and an allegation are different things. The map colors each country by its single largest figure among the kinds of evidence you switch on.

Sources for every figure and person are linked inside each country.
