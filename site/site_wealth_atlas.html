About the data ⓘ

Three layers, three sources. Global families — Bloomberg World's Richest Families 2025 (Dec 9 2025). Individuals — Forbes World's Billionaires 2026 (Mar 1 2026), top 50. US families — Forbes America's Richest Families 2024, all 25.

On national depth. The US is the only country with a true deep family ranking. Other big economies (China, India, Germany…) publish deep individual lists, which already appear in the Individuals layer.

Honesty flags. A dynasty can recur across layers with different figures (Walton is $513B in Bloomberg's global aggregate vs $267B in Forbes US) — different methods, both labelled with their source. Four global new-entrants carry no figure (≥$46.4B threshold). Markers sit at principal base/HQ; Forbes "country" is nationality.

​​End Part v: "Globalization."
Before moving on to the next section, I intentionally covered only the two formal financial international organizations for simplicity's sake (the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and World Bank (WB)), but there are a number of other more regional formal financial organizations that play similar roles locally. These have faced similar criticisms. There are also a number of informal global institutions and authorities, like clubs and forums for bankers and regulators below them, the most popular being the Bank of International Settlements, an international bank that holds 10-15% of all central banks' monetary reserves (footnote 6.1) and that's owned by 60 central banks of countries that together account for about 95% of world GDP (gross-domestic product). This is the same organization that you read about on my Invasion of Countries section on my On-Planet Invasion page. It provides banking services to central banks and international financial institutions; monitors currency stability, exchange rate fluctuations, and other risks; and sets standards that tighten and loosen economies’ money creation and supplies. Even though it's referred to as the “central bank of central banks,” it does not itself create money. Like many international organizations, it also doesn't fall under the direct control of any government, keeps its inner workings secret to one extent or another, and enjoys a degree of legal immunity to ensure that it operates without interference from individual countries (footnote 6.1). Below the informal institutions and authorities are a number of private and hybrid actors, including associations, think tanks, academic groups, societies, etc. There would be no point in trying to list all of them though, as The Yearbook of International Organizations (https://uia.org/yearbook ), the authoritative resource on international orgs, lists over 75,000 internationals alone from 300 countries and territories, about 42,000 of which are active and to which 1,200 new ones are added every year. That's larger than the populations of some of the countries that they oversee.

What's the BIS up to these days? As countries worldwide transition more from physical cash to digital currencies, the Bank of International Settlements (BIS) is pushing for global electronic currency that will be owned and operated by them under absolute technological control (footnote 11.2). This is the greatest concentration of central banking power that has ever occurred. Because it will erase any and all transaction privacy, any citizens deemed counter to the government's preferences (which, clearly, are anything but immune to crossing the line themselves) can be easily cut off from their money supply. Some central banks are even in the process of introducing under-the-skin RFID microchips for transactions. If it at all follows in the steps of contactless credit cards, which have made cash essentially obsolete in Sweden already (where only less than 1% of all payments are now made physically), who's to say that they won't eventually make it the default, or worse, a requirement (footnote 11.3)? And, like fingerprinting, eye scanning, location tracking, facial recognition, and others, these technologies naturally come with an increased potential for exploitation, whether it be by criminals or by (a criminal) government. *As long as there remains a push for centralized currency with centralized accessibility like this within a system parasitic to the Body, it should be considered nothing other than a Trojan, an evil between you and your body,* regardless of any claims of increased efficiency, scale, and security that might come with it (footnote 11.4).
