What is on the map

Search for a place and the map displays the government offices recorded for it, from municipal to national level.

Green markers identify offices that may provide a route for public engagement on legislation or policy. Examples include asking a legislator to introduce a bill, submitting evidence to a committee, commenting on a proposed regulation or filing a ballot initiative where that procedure exists. Other government offices are included for context, but may have no direct role in changing legislation. A court, for example, decides legal disputes rather than acting as a channel for lobbying on a particular case. Police enforce the law, while auditors generally examine government activity and compliance.

An office’s inclusion does not mean that it has the power to make the change you want. Its authority depends on its legal mandate and the subject involved.

Office titles are relatively stable; the people holding them change. Wikidata’s records of current officeholders may be out of date, sometimes by years. Treat names shown on the map as leads and verify them against an official government source before making contact.

Coverage is uneven. Some countries have extensive records, while others have limited information, particularly at municipal level. A blank means no office was found in the dataset, not that no such office exists.

Office records come from Wikidata, the same source used by govdirectory.org. Administrative boundaries come from cgaz-boundaries; place and local-building data come from OpenStreetMap.

Effectiveness ratings for the contact methods are carried over from the leverage chart in this repository, along with the datasets used to support them. Where no reliable measurement is available, the map says so rather than assigning an estimated success rate.

Wikidata records the names of national legislatures but often lacks their contact details and procedural documents. For national chambers, the map therefore uses IPU Parline, the Inter-Parliamentary Union’s database of national parliaments. It includes contact information and links to parliamentary rules of procedure, which explain how bills are introduced and considered. Parline information is supplied by the parliaments themselves. The magnifying circles

The circles along the left side of the map magnify the different levels of government covering the place you searched.

Click the ground inside a circle to zoom in one step. Double-click to zoom out, and drag to move the view. Hover over the circle to open a second magnifier showing a closer view of the area beneath your pointer.

Click a marker to open its office record. If several offices share the same location, a list appears so you can choose which one to view.

The scroll wheel is disabled inside the magnifiers, so scrolling continues to zoom the main map behind them. When you move the pointer away, each circle returns to the view of its assigned government level.

The magnifiers appear only when the window is wider than 1,180 pixels and the search result includes at least two levels of government.
