About what you are reading

This map draws every office of government Wikidata records for a place, at every level from the municipality up to the country. A marked office is one an ordinary member of the public has a route to that bears on what the law says: introducing a bill, submitting to a committee, commenting on a proposed rule, filing a ballot measure, petitioning for a rule. The unmarked ones are government too and open in full, with their routes and what stops them, but writing to them does not change a law — a judge cannot be approached about a case, a police force cannot alter the statute it enforces, an auditor checks compliance with rules already written.

What each office can do is read from its title and its Wikidata class. It is a good guess, not a constitutional ruling: powers differ from country to country, and a title that means one thing in one place means another elsewhere. Confirm on the official site before acting on it.

Office titles are stable; the people holding them are not. Wikidata records of who holds an office go out of date, sometimes by years. Treat a name here as a lead to check, never as current fact.

Offices come from Wikidata, which is also what govdirectory.org is built on. Boundaries come from the cgaz-boundaries repository. Success figures are reproduced from the leverage chart in this repository, each with the dataset behind it; where nobody has measured a route, it says so rather than guessing.

Coverage is deep for some countries and thin for others, and thins further at local level. A blank here means Wikidata has no record, not that no office exists.
