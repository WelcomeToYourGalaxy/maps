About this map

Search a place and the map draws every government above it that has a part in making a law, from the municipality up to the country, with the offices of each. Levels that make no rules of their own, such as districts run by appointed officials or statistical regions, are named in the chain but carry no offices. Offices with a green dot are ones a member of the public can contact about what a law says; courts carry none, because a court is filed with rather than written to.

How laws are made is set out for every country from its constitution and parliament: who may introduce a bill, who signs it into law, which levels below the country make laws or local rules, and whether citizens can propose a law themselves, from the International IDEA Direct Democracy Database. Methods that do not exist in a country, such as a citizens’ initiative where there is none, are not offered there.

Office titles are stable; the people in them are not. Wikidata's record of who currently holds an office can be years out of date, so treat any name here as a lead to check. Coverage is uneven: deep for some countries, thin for others, thinnest at municipal level. A blank means there is no record of an office, not that there is no office.

Offices come from Wikidata, the same source govdirectory.org uses. Boundaries come from cgaz-boundaries, places and local buildings from OpenStreetMap, citizens’ initiatives from International IDEA. Which methods work more often than others is carried over from the leverage chart in this repository, with the dataset behind each; where nobody has measured a method, it says so.
