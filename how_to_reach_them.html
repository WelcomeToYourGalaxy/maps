About this map

Search a place and the map draws the offices of government recorded for it, at every level from the municipality up to the country. Offices with a green dot are ones a member of the public can contact about what a law says: asking a legislator to introduce a bill, writing to a committee, commenting on a proposed rule, filing a ballot measure. The rest are government too and are listed in full, but contacting them will not change a law. A judge cannot be approached about a case, a police force cannot alter the statute it enforces, and an auditor only checks rules that are already written.

What an office can do is worked out from its title and its Wikidata class, so it is an informed guess. Powers differ between countries, and the same title can mean two different things in two of them. Check the office's own site before acting on anything here.

Office titles are stable; the people in them are not. Wikidata's record of who currently holds an office can be years out of date, so treat any name here as a lead to check.

Coverage is uneven: deep for some countries, thin for others, thinnest at municipal level. A blank means there is no record of an office, not that there is no office.

Offices come from Wikidata, the same source govdirectory.org uses. Boundaries come from cgaz-boundaries, places and local buildings from OpenStreetMap. How often each method works is carried over from the leverage chart in this repository, with the dataset behind each one; where nobody has measured a method, it says so instead of guessing.
