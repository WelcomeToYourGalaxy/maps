## The vein, and the one thing round 72 proved about it
**The supreme audit institution that publishes a report per audited entity**, read year by year.
Five countries have converted on it — Turkey, Mexico, Czechia, Nicaragua and Serbia — and Serbia is
the clearest shape: its State Audit Institution lists the National Bank as audit subject 1071 with
NINE reports covering financial years 2009 to 2017, on a host that serves plain curl. One host, one
naming convention, a document a year, and the bank is the SUBJECT rather than the auditor.

**Find the institution's own index of reports.** Searching a country name returns commercial banks;
the index does not. Look for a report naming the central bank as the audited entity and read its
findings. **A finding is a record; a clean opinion is a documented blank and a valuable one** — say
which it is.

Two more veins that have produced second records: the bank's OWN annual report, where a governance
or integrity section sometimes discloses internal disciplinary findings; and a parliamentary public
accounts committee, whose minutes state facts the press only paraphrases — **check the parliament's
own site before the press**.

**The inversion test, which has closed eight countries across three rounds.** Read the institution's
own list of audited bodies and show the central bank is absent from it.

**Round 72 established one route is shut and you should not try it:** for currency-union members the
unions' own statutes give immunity from member states' jurisdiction, so national audit institutions
cannot reach them. None of those countries is in your list.
