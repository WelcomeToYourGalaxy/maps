BANK OF KOREA
South Korea
South Korea's money is the won, issued by the Bank of Korea, which scores 26 of 100, where 100 means fully exposed to outside influence. One episode of government pressure is recorded. Choi Kyung-hwan became deputy prime minister for the economy and finance minister in July 2014 and pressed publicly for lower interest rates, launching a stimulus package and warning that the economy stood between a leap forward and a recession; he said in public that he had not mentioned the base rate and that the central bank should get the hint. On 14 August 2014 the bank cut its base rate by a quarter point to 2.25 percent, the first move in fifteen months, and cut again to 2.0 percent on 15 October. Reporting at the time described both cuts as made under government pressure rather than as the start of a policy cycle, and a Reuters survey ahead of the August meeting recorded a sharp reversal in market expectations that raised questions about the bank's independence. Governor Lee Ju-yeol said the decision was his committee's and that its independence was guaranteed. Independence is written into the Bank of Korea Act, but the same reporting noted a long record of governments pressing the bank to cooperate: the May 2013 cut under a previous governor was read the same way, and in October 2010 a vice finance minister attended a rate-setting meeting to deliver the government's view of the economy. Two cases are recorded, both ending in conviction. Between 2022 and 2023 a currency-exchange employee at the bank's Daejeon Sejong-Chungnam headquarters broke the rule that older coins leave the vault first, at a coin collector's request, letting 240,000 one-hundred-won coins from 2018 and 2019 go out ahead of 2017 coins; the collector made about 180 million won selling them and gave the employee about 55 million, and the Daejeon District Court imposed two years and six months in prison, a fine and confiscation. Between 2013 and 2015 an employee handling receipts from the bank's publication sales diverted about 56 million won to a personal account and spent about 12 million won on commemorative banknote sheets with a company card; the Seoul Central District Court gave eight months suspended for two years, the money having been repaid. Two officials came from international lenders: Hwang Geon-il of the World Bank was nominated to the rate-setting Monetary Policy Committee, and Rhee Chang-yong of the International Monetary Fund was nominated governor on 23 March 2022. Three audits cover the bank: the Board of Audit and Inspection once a year, an auditor appointed by the President on the finance minister's recommendation who serves three years and reports to the Monetary Policy Committee, and an outside accounting firm the law requires but does not name. The notes and coins are made by the state-run mint, the Korea Minting and Security Printing Corporation, the country's only maker of banknotes and coins and a close partner of the bank, and the Bank of Korea is one of the 63 central banks that own the Bank for International Settlements, whose capital has been held by central banks alone since private shares were bought back in 2001. Its officials also sit on the Financial Stability Board, which lists the Bank of Korea and the Financial Services Commission as its member institutions for the country and is chaired by Andrew Bailey, governor of the Bank of England. A newspaper report of 12 February 2025 says all 104.4 tonnes of South Korea's gold, bought between 2011 and 2013, is stored at the Bank of England. That is the paper's own wording, not attributed to the Bank of Korea, so it is a claim rather than a confirmed fact.

2 documented cases at the central bank:

2022-2023 · embezzlement · convicted. A former currency-exchange employee at the Bank of Korea's Daejeon Sejong-Chungnam headquarters broke the first-in, first-out rule at a coin collector's request, letting 240,000 100-won coins from 2018-2019 be issued before 2017 coins. The collector earned about 180 million won selling the coins and gave the employee about 55 million won. The Daejeon District Court sentenced the employee to 2 years 6 months in prison, a 1 million won fine and confiscation of about 43.31 million won. "Rare Coins Are Valuable"... Former Bank of Korea Employee Sentenced for Embezzling Coins (Asia Economy)
2013-2015 · embezzlement by central bank staff · convicted. A former Bank of Korea employee, moved in 2013 to the museum operations team and responsible for paying in the proceeds of Bank of Korea publication sales, diverted about 56 million won in 14 transfers to a personal account and used a Bank of Korea corporate card to buy about 12 million won of commemorative uncut banknote sheets. The Seoul Central District Court, Judge Lee Heung-kwon presiding, convicted the employee of occupational breach of trust and embezzlement and imposed eight months in prison suspended for two years, noting the full amount had been repaid. Lim Ji-won (member of the Monetary Policy Committee, took office 17 May 2018, four-year term): from JP Morgan's Seoul branch (1999 to 2018) to member of the Bank of Korea's Monetary Policy Committee. The report states that Lim Ji-won worked at JP Morgan's Seoul branch from 1999 until she took office on the Bank of Korea's Monetary Policy Committee on 17 May 2018. It also reports that she still held 6,486 JP Morgan shares on the day she took office and sold them between July and 7 August 2018, that under the Public Official Ethics Act then in force the limit on share holdings covered domestic stock and not overseas stock, so the holding did not breach the law, and that critics said not selling before taking office was inappropriate given the law's purpose of preventing conflicts of interest. The governor at the time, Lee Ju-yeol, said that if the holding breached the Bank of Korea Act the law should be applied, and that the question was legal rather than ethical. No wrongdoing is implied. 임지원 금통위원 취임시 JP모건 주식 보유 (NewDaily Biz)
