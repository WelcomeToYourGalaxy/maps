CENTRAL BANK OF TIMOR-LESTE
Timor-Leste
Timor-Leste has its own central bank, the Central Bank of Timor-Leste, created in 2001, but not its own currency: it uses the United States dollar. The International Monetary Fund's 2023 classification lists it among the places with no separate legal tender and the dollar as anchor. It scores 49 of 100 for exposure to outside influence, where 100 means fully exposed, more exposed than 94 per cent of the 194 places with a figure; the outside influence part alone scores 41. Its weakest side is corruption and secrecy around it, 57, with a Corruption Perceptions Index score of 44 of 100 for 2025. The independence the law gives the bank is comparatively strong, 0.777 of 1 and rank 50 of 191. No documented case at the central bank was found, which does not prove none happened. The dollar it uses dominates world finance: 16 places use it, and it accounts for 89.08 per cent of world currency trading. Ernst & Young Audit & Associados - SROC, S.A. audited the 2023 accounts.

1 documented case at the central bank:

2019 · unexplained accounts or weak controls · official finding. In its report and opinion on the 2019 General State Account, Timor-Leste's Court of Accounts found that 6.6 million US dollars of European Union direct budget support, held in three bank accounts at the Banco Central de Timor-Leste, was not booked as state revenue, so 2019 state revenue was understated by that amount, and the 2.3 million US dollars spent from those accounts was spent outside the state budget and the General State Account. The report also recorded a closing accounting balance of 4.4 million US dollars against a cash balance at the central bank of 5.2 million US dollars. OWNERSHIP AND OUTSIDE CONTROL

Official dollarization (US dollar as sole legal tender) (dollarization)

The IMF AREAER 2023 classification (end-April 2023) lists Ecuador, El Salvador, Marshall Islands, Micronesia, Palau, Panama and Timor-Leste as having no separate legal tender with the US dollar as anchor.
Ecuador adopted the US dollar in January 2000 at 25,000 sucres per dollar under the Ley para la Transformación Económica (Registro Oficial Suplemento 34, 13 March 2000); Article 94 of the Código Orgánico Monetario y Financiero (2014) makes the dollar the currency of transactions.
In El Salvador, legal reforms made private-sector acceptance of Bitcoin voluntary and tax payments payable only in US dollars, listed as prior actions for the IMF EFF approved on 26 February 2025.
