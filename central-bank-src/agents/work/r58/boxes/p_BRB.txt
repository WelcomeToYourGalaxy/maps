CENTRAL BANK OF BARBADOS
Barbados
The Central Bank of Barbados issues the Barbados dollar and in practice holds it at a fixed rate against the United States dollar. In 2017 Finance Minister Chris Sinckler dismissed Governor Delisle Worrell. The government's stated reason was that the board objected to his reported high-handed manner; some observers believed the reason was his refusal to keep financing the government with new money, which he had set out in a letter that January saying the government must stop taking new money from the bank to pay its bills. The Appeals Court upheld the minister's right to dismiss him. Apart from that, no government pressure was reported in the 37 quarters from 2010 to 2018. The bank's founding governor, Sir Courtney Blackman, had been an economist at the Wall Street bank Irving Trust Company from 1968 to 1971, led the bank from 1972 to 1987 across three terms while also serving as Barbados' alternate governor to the International Monetary Fund until March 1987, and from 1987 to 1994 worked as an international business consultant and as director general of corporations and financial institutions that the bank's own page does not name. Its five-dollar polymer note, introduced in December 2022, is printed by De La Rue. Barbados scores 28 of 100, where 100 means fully exposed to outside influence, and the bank tells the public little, 3 of 15 on a transparency measure. The bank's accounts are audited by PricewaterhouseCoopers SRL of Bridgetown, whose report for the year ended 31 December 2025 is dated 27 March 2026.

5 documented cases at the central bank:

2020 · unexplained accounts or weak controls · official finding. The Auditor General's 2021 report found that cheques totalling $10,198,548.08 recorded on Treasury cashiers' reports for 22 September 2020 did not agree with the Central Bank of Barbados listing, which recorded $3,536,006.77, a difference of $6,662,541.31 for which no explanation was given. The same report found interest charges of $840,926.31 on the Treasury overdraft account at the Central Bank for August and November 2020 appeared on Central Bank statements but were not entered in the government ledger. Report of the Auditor General for the year 2021 (Barbados Audit Office)
2018-2019 · unexplained accounts or weak controls · official finding. The Auditor General's 2020 report found that bank reconciliations for Treasury accounts at the Central Bank of Barbados were not completed up to 31 March 2019, and that the Central Bank account had last been reconciled in May 2018. The report noted that monthly reconciliation is an internal control that can detect fraud or errors. Report of the Auditor General for the year 2020 (Barbados Audit Office)
2018-2019 · unexplained accounts or weak controls · official finding. The Auditor General's 2020 report examined 29 transactions in which the Accountant General authorises the Central Bank of Barbados by signed memorandum to transfer loan funds to lending agencies. Eight transfers totalling $13.137 million had no supporting memorandum, which the report called a breach of controls that increases the risk of unauthorised payments. Report of the Auditor General for the year 2020 (Barbados Audit Office)
2018 · election or budget financing · official finding. The Auditor General's 2020 report found that the government's overdraft facility at the Central Bank of Barbados exceeded its statutory limit of 10 per cent of estimated revenue ($323.303 million) twice between 1 April 2018 and 31 July 2018, each time by $10 million, contrary to the legislation. Report of the Auditor General for the year 2020 (Barbados Audit Office)
2000-2004 · unexplained accounts or weak controls · official finding. The Auditor General's 2004 report found discrepancies between the Accountant General's records and the records of the Central Bank of Barbados for the government current account, arising between October 2000 and February 2004. Withdrawals not recorded by the Accountant General exceeded $1.8 million and unrecorded deposits exceeded $6.8 million, including a $4.48 million transaction the Auditor General had already said in 2003 warranted investigation. Report of the Auditor General for the year 2004 (Barbados Audit Office)

1 documented career move between the bank and private finance, the IMF or the World Bank:

Sir Courtney Blackman (Economist, Irving Trust Company, 1968-1971; founding Governor of the Central Bank of Barbados, 1972-1987): Wall Street bank economist (Irving Trust Company) into the founding governorship; later unnamed financial-institution directorships after leaving. The Central Bank of Barbados's own page for its past governors states that Sir Courtney Blackman worked from 1968 to 1971 as an economist at Irving Trust Company on Wall Street, which it calls 'now defunct', and that he was the founding Governor of the Central Bank of Barbados from 1972 to 1987, serving three terms, while also acting as Barbados' Alternate Governor to the IMF over the same period until March 1987. The same page says that from 1987 to 1994 he worked as an international business consultant to governments, central banks, multilateral institutions and international corporations, and served as Director General of several corporations and financial institutions, which it does not name. The page does not state whether Irving Trust Company or those later financial institutions were privately or publicly owned. The IMF alternate governorship is a public post.
