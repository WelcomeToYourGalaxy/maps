CENTRAL BANK OF ARUBA
Aruba
The Central Bank of Aruba, created in 1986, issues the Aruban florin and holds it at a peg to the US dollar as of December 2025. It scores 29 of 100, where 0 means shielded from outside influence and 100 means fully exposed; the weakest side is corruption and secrecy around it, with financial secrecy at 67.3 of 100. Written independence from government scores 0.446 of 1, 149th of 191, with the measures for the governor and for policy making both at 0. No documented case at the bank was found. Crane Currency, a banknote maker, is listed as having supplied Aruba's new banknotes in 2019, on a client list in another organisation's announcement rather than a contract notice. BNP Paribas Asset Management runs a fixed income mandate in US dollars for the bank and also gives it advice. Ernst & Young audits the accounts.

1 documented case at the central bank:

2000-2001 · unexplained accounts or weak controls · official finding. Aruba's General Audit Chamber, reviewing the country's 2001-2004 annual accounts, found that contributions paid into an earmarked account held at the Centrale Bank van Aruba in the country's name for the Fondo Desaroyo Aruba foundation were not accounted for completely, correctly and on time. Of a balance of 19.3 million Aruban florins at the end of 2000, only the 3 million paid in by Aruba appeared in the country's accounts, while 16,898,503 florins paid in by the Netherlands were left off the balance sheet. PRESSURE IN PRACTICE

Not covered by Binder's 2010–2018 survey.

1991 · statutory arrangement letting the government direct the bank. Aruba's Central Bank Ordinance (AB 1991 no. GT 32), as amended through AB 2014 no. 5 and published by the Centrale Bank van Aruba in Dutch and in the bank's English translation, keeps the bank under the Governor of Aruba and the Minister of Finance. Section 18 says the president and executive directors are appointed by the Governor from a shortlist of three put up by the Supervisory Board, and that the Governor may suspend or dismiss them by state decree. Section 19(3) says general guidelines for the performance of the duties of the president and executive directors can be set out in a state decree. Section 23 says the chairman of the Supervisory Board is the Government Commissioner, appointed and dismissed by the Governor, that the other supervisory directors are appointed by the Governor, and that they may be suspended or dismissed before the end of their term by the Governor. Section 27(1) says the Government Commissioner supervises the bank's activities on behalf of the Country and acts in accordance with general or specific instructions given by the Minister. This is a standing arrangement with no end date. Central Bank Ordinance (AB 1991 no. GT 32), English translation (Centrale Bank van Aruba)
