BANK OF THE REPUBLIC OF HAITI
Haiti
The Bank of the Republic of Haiti, created in 1979, issues the currency HTG, held at a de facto peg to the US dollar as of December 2025; the dollar is also listed as a currency there. Its exposure score is 35 of 100, where 0 means shielded from outside influence and 100 means fully exposed; that is more exposed than 66% of 194 places. Corruption and secrecy around it scores 82, with a Corruption Perceptions Index score of 16 of 100 and money-laundering risk of 8.12 of 10 (rank 2 of 177, where 1 is riskiest). Written independence from government scores 0.366 of 1 on one index (rank 151 of 155) and 0.376 on another (rank 165 of 191). Government pressure was reported in 0 of 37 quarters from 2010 to 2018. One case is recorded: in April 2004 justice minister Bernard Gousse barred Venel Joseph, a former governor of the bank, from leaving the country, naming him among 37 former officials to be kept available for investigations into crimes alleged against the government of ousted president Jean-Bertrand Aristide, and calling the travel ban an insurance policy. What conduct at the bank, if any, was being looked into is not reported, and no charge and no finding followed. The bank's accounts are audited by KPMG S.A. of France together with the Port-au-Prince firm Merove-Pierre - Cabinet d'Experts-Comptables, whose joint report on the year ended 30 September 2021 was signed on 30 June 2022.
