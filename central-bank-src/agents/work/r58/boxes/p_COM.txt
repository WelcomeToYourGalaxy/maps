CENTRAL BANK OF THE COMOROS
Comoros
The Central Bank of the Comoros issues the Comorian franc, and France stands behind it. A monetary cooperation agreement signed at Moroni and Paris on 23 November 1979 rests on an unlimited French guarantee of the currency the bank issues. That agreement set the rate at 1 Comorian franc to 0.02 French francs; after a devaluation of 33 percent on 11 January 1994, the rate is now 1 euro to 491.968 Comorian francs. The bank must keep 65 percent of its foreign currency reserves on deposit with the French Treasury. Two governments also fill its board. Under statutes signed in Paris on 22 April 2008 it had at most eight members, half named by the French government and half by the government of the Union of the Comoros, with the Governor named by the President of the Union on the finance minister's proposal; the board approved a new draft on 28 June 2024 that still awaited promulgation at the end of 2024. Under statutes in force since March 2025, France names two of the six board members, two of the four members of the banking supervision committee and two of the four members of the committee that sets monetary policy and manages reserves. The notes are printed at the Banque de France, and a new 10,000-franc note was reportedly introduced on 6 January 2026. No documented case at the bank was found, which does not prove none happened. It holds no gold, Mazars Fivoarana audited its 2024 accounts, and it scores 65 of 100, the highest of the 194 places with a figure.

2 documented cases at the central bank:

2015-2016 · embezzlement · official finding. The Comorian parliament's commission of inquiry into the economic citizenship programme reported in December 2017 that, according to his own collaborators, President Ikililou Dhoinine ordered that from January 2015 until the end of his term the programme's funds no longer be paid into the Banque Centrale des Comores, and that he could not explain what became of them. The commission concluded that he was therefore suspected of embezzling public money. Rapport de la Commission d'enquete parlementaire sur la loi relative a la citoyennete economique (Assemblee de l'Union des Comores)
2008-2015 · unexplained accounts or weak controls · official finding. The same parliamentary inquiry recorded that the economic citizenship account TR0044 held at the Banque Centrale des Comores received payments, including four cheques drawn by Comoro Gulf Holding on two commercial banks between March 2009 and April 2010, and that the central bank booked money into the account without knowing the price at which the passports were being sold. The bank's board noted in June 2014 that 6 million euros had been expected before the end of December 2013 while receipts since October 2013 came to only 2.670 million Comorian francs, which the inquiry called a shortfall of more than 2,900,000,000 Comorian francs. The account was closed on 1 September 2015. Rapport de la Commission d'enquete parlementaire sur la loi relative a la citoyennete economique (Assemblee de l'Union des Comores)

2008-04-22 · statutory arrangement seating two governments on the bank's board. Under the statutes of the Banque Centrale des Comores, signed in Paris on 22 April 2008 and still in force, the bank's board has at most eight members, half of them designated by the French Government and half by the Government of the Union of the Comoros, so every seat is filled by one of the two governments. The Governor is named by the President of the Union of the Comoros, on a proposal from the Minister of Finance. This is a standing arrangement with no end date; the bank's board approved a new draft of the statutes on 28 June 2024, but that text still awaited promulgation by the Union's President at the end of 2024. Shares its people: revolving door, board seats, forum members (Government of France)

Source says: Government of France sits on the board of the Central Bank of the Comoros (Comoros). The French government designates members of the Central Bank of the Comoros board. Under the statutes published by Legifrance in 2010, the board had up to eight members, half designated by the French government and half by the Comorian government. New statutes signed in Paris on 3 December 2024 and in force since March 2025 changed the balance: the bank's own organisation chart now gives a board of six, the governor plus three administrators designated by the Comorian government and two designated by the French government. The French-designated seats also sit on the bodies that decide: two of the members of the committee that sets monetary policy and manages the reserves, which is otherwise the governor and one member designated by the Comorian government, and two of the members of the banking supervision committee.
