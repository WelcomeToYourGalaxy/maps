CENTRAL BANK OF MAURITANIA
Mauritania
The Central Bank of Mauritania issues Mauritania's money, the ouguiya, and scores 30 of 100 on exposure to outside influence, where 100 means fully exposed; its weakest side is corruption in the country and money-laundering risk in its banks. One case is recorded, from 2020 and 2021, and it is still an investigation. In July 2020 police working under the Nouakchott-Ouest prosecutor arrested six people, one of them a bank employee, over foreign currency taken from the central bank. Early reports put the amount at about 1 million US dollars and later reports at 2.4 million, plus 500,000 dollars in counterfeit notes. By March 2021 seven people were in pretrial detention, presumed to have taken about 1 million dollars, and supporters held a sit-in demanding a quick trial. No trial or verdict is reported. The governor also signed an agreement with the World Bank treasury for advice on managing reserves. Who prints the ouguiya is not published, but part of what it is printed on is: the 50-ouguiya note marking fifty years of the currency, introduced on 18 June 2023, was issued on three different substrates, Durasafe composite by Landqart, paper with sisal by Oberthur and Hybrid by Louisenthal, while the printer itself is recorded as unknown. A substrate maker is not a printer, so Mauritania still has no printer on record. Central Banking reported on 30 July 2004 that President Maaouiya Ould Taya had sacked the chairman of the Central Bank of Mauritania, the day after sacking his whole economic team, and that Zeine Ould Zeidane, formerly an adviser to the president, was installed in his place. The report names neither the sacked chairman nor any reason and gives no calendar date, saying only that it happened on the Monday. It rests on a single relayed account by IRIN, so it is a claim, not confirmed.

PRESSURE IN PRACTICE

2004-07 · dismissal. Central Banking reported on 30 July 2004 that President Maaouiya Ould Taya had sacked the chairman of the Central Bank of Mauritania, the day after sacking his whole economic team. The report names neither the sacked chairman nor any reason, and gives no calendar date, saying only that it happened on the Monday; the piece it relays, by IRIN, appeared on Wednesday 28 July. Zeine Ould Zeidane, formerly an adviser to the president, was installed in his place. The whole record rests on that one relayed report, so it is a claim, not confirmed.
