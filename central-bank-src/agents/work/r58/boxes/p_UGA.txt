BANK OF UGANDA
Uganda
The Bank of Uganda issues the Ugandan shilling, and the Auditor General audits its accounts. It scores 34 of 100 on exposure to outside influence, where 0 means shielded and 100 fully exposed, and its weakest side is corruption in the country. On paper it is among the less independent central banks, scoring 0.467 of 1 on a 42-rule measure of independence written into law. One case is recorded. A confidential 2018 Auditor General report to parliament said bank managers, including governor Mutebile, acted without guidelines, regulations or policies and may have intentionally neglected aspects of the closure and sale of at least seven commercial banks, including Crane Bank in 2016 and 2017. These are the findings of a report, not court verdicts. Sudhir Ruparelia, dfcu Bank and Nile River Acquisition Company are named in the case, the last two as having received money, favours or contracts from the central bank. Parliament then put the bank through a public inquiry over the same closures. The Auditor General, John Muwanga, presented his special audit to the Speaker of Parliament on 27 August 2018; it had been requested by the chairman of Parliament's Committee on Commissions, Statutory Authorities and State Enterprises the previous November, and the bank had tried to stop it. The committee, chaired by Abdu Katuntu, took evidence from the governor, Emmanuel Tumusiime-Mutebile, his deputy Louis Kasekende and other officials from late 2018 into 2019. On 1 November 2018 Katuntu sent the governor and deputy governor away for arriving without the documents on the closures. Mutebile told the committee on 12 November that the seven banks were put under resolution largely because they were insolvent, said he had never delegated his powers to agree the sale of Global Trust Bank to DFCU Bank, and said liquidation reports had been kept from him by junior staff, which raised the question in public of whether he was in charge of his own institution; he also acknowledged that the inquiry had exposed problems at the bank. The committee's report, finalised in February 2019, recommended individual accountability for the officials implicated. Demands in Parliament for action against them later stalled. Northern Trust supplies the bank with compliance, performance and risk analysis, and a dashboard for watching the outside firms that invest its money; that is analysis and advice, not management of the money.

2018-11-01 · parliamentary inquiry. On 27 August 2018 the Auditor General, John Muwanga, presented to the Speaker of Parliament a special audit of the Bank of Uganda's closure of seven commercial banks between 1993 and 2017 - Teefe Trust Bank, International Credit Bank, Greenland Bank, Co-operative Bank, National Bank of Commerce, Global Trust Bank and Crane Bank. The audit had been requested by the chairman of Parliament's Committee on Commissions, Statutory Authorities and State Enterprises on 28 November 2017, and the central bank had tried to stop it. The report found no documented guidelines, regulations or policies for identifying the purchasers of three of the banks sold under purchase-and-assumption arrangements, and questioned how the bank spent over 400 billion shillings closing Crane Bank, half of it not recovered. The committee, chaired by Abdu Katuntu, then held public hearings from late 2018 into 2019 at which the governor, Emmanuel Tumusiime-Mutebile, his deputy Louis Kasekende and other officials gave evidence; on 1 November 2018 Katuntu sent the governor and deputy governor away for arriving without the documents on the closures, and the committee put bank security officers on oath that month. Mutebile told the committee on 12 November that the seven banks were put under resolution largely because they were insolvent, said in December that he had never delegated his powers to the supervision director to agree the sale of Global Trust Bank to DFCU Bank, and said liquidation reports had been kept from him by junior staff - which raised the question in public of whether he was in charge of his own institution. He also acknowledged that the inquiry had exposed problems at the bank. The committee's report, finalised in February 2019, recommended individual accountability for officials implicated in the irregular closures. Parliamentary demands for action against them later stalled. The COSASE probe of BoU: how events unfolded and what way forward? (Eagle Online)
