### CURRENT PARAGRAPH (being replaced)
The Central Bank of Honduras issues the lempira, kept as of December 2025 at a set level against the US dollar and moved in small steps, an arrangement called a crawling peg. Its accounts are checked from outside by Horwath Central America, for the 2023 financial year. The 200-lempira note is printed by Crane Currency, which won private tender 11/2023 by a resolution of 14 March 2024, worth 1,284,000 US dollars at 6.42 US cents a note. One case is documented: on 3 June 2026 prosecutors charged 11 current and former employees, two of them serving managers, with 100 counts of negligent embezzlement over a scheme said to have removed public funds from the bank, and they were arrested after the court admitted the case. The charges are unproven. Honduras scores 27 of 100 for exposure to outside influence, the heaviest part being corruption and secrecy around the bank, at 67.

(November 1993 to April 1996), credit officer at Intermediaria Financiera, S.A. of Grupo FICOHSA (September to December 1996) and credit officer then agency manager at Banco FICOHSA (January 1997 to February 2003) to President of the Banco Central de Honduras (from 27 January 2018). The central bank's own biography, published by the Central American Monetary Council, lists Cerrato's ten years in Honduran commercial banking before his public career and his appointment as central bank president in January 2018. Ownership: Banco Ficohsa's audited financial statements state the bank is 94.28% owned by Grupo Financiero Ficohsa S.A., domiciled in Panama, which establishes private shareholding; the biography page itself states no ownership.
