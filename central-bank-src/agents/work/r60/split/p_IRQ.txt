### CURRENT PARAGRAPH (being replaced)
The Central Bank of Iraq issues the dinar and keeps it within a band of 2 percent or less against the US dollar. It scores 36 of 100, where 100 means fully exposed to outside influence, and 0.733 of 1 for the independence written into its law, where 1 means the bank holds the power and 0 the government. In 2012 Iraq's Board of Supreme Audit examined the bank's daily auctions of dollars to banks and found that most transactions rested on fraudulent paperwork; the country's top auditor told US officials that up to 800 million dollars a week was leaving Iraq illegally, as reported by the US Special Inspector General for Iraq Reconstruction. In October 2012 the cabinet removed governor Sinan al-Shabibi and named a successor while a parliamentary committee was examining possible manipulation of the dinar and charging that bank officials were abusing the dollar sales. A committee member said arrest warrants had been issued for 30 people, including al-Shabibi and his deputy Mudher Saleh, and the Supreme Judicial Council confirmed warrants without saying what the charges were. The integrity committee's chairman said the inquiry was not about money but about procedures, while a member of the finance committee called it a political decision and not professional; al-Shabibi's defence called the case politically motivated. No court outcome was ever found, so nothing was proven; he died on 8 January 2022, and the bank mourned him as a distinguished former governor. It holds 175.6 tonnes of gold, and the Board of Supreme Audit audits its accounts. Who prints the dinar rests on an encyclopaedia entry rather than anything the bank publishes: it says the 2003 series, still the basis of circulating notes, was printed by De La Rue.
