### CURRENT PARAGRAPH (being replaced)
The Central Bank of the Congo issues the country's money. In April 2021 a report by Congo's state finance inspectorate, the IGF, called a 43 million US dollar transfer from a central bank account at BGFI Bank to the food importer Egal an embezzlement of public funds, and said the bank kept false accounts to cover it: its 2013 accounts showed about 43.1 million dollars in that account when the real balance was about 119,000 dollars. The former governor Deogratias Mutombo, the bank and PricewaterhouseCoopers did not respond to the journalists who reported this from leaked BGFI documents. On 25 October 2023 the accounts of Mutombo and of Albert Yuma, former board chairman of the mining company Gecamines, were frozen over an alleged embezzlement of 25.521 million dollars, with both men required to remain available to the national audit court; that allegation is unproven and still under investigation. Belgium's federal prosecutor also opened a money laundering investigation into Mutombo and five others, with searches in September 2023; as of October 2024 the prosecutor said no suspect had been charged. The bank scores 34 of 100, where 100 means fully exposed to outside influence. Giesecke+Devrient printed the 200-franc note issued on 31 July 2007. The bank's accounts are audited by Ernst & Young RDC of Kinshasa, which gave an unqualified opinion on the year ended 31 December 2024. In a communique that reached the state news agency ACP on Wednesday 23 September 2026 the Cour des comptes, Congo's audit court, announced an audit mission at the Banque Centrale du Congo to examine the bank's treasury operations for the 2025 financial year, as part of checking how the 2025 finance law was carried out. The court's First President, Jimmy Munganga, instructed the bank's services to hand the auditors the documents they need. No start or end date is given, and parliament's review of the 2025 accounts-settlement bill and of the 2027 finance bill waits on the court filing its report.
