The Central Bank of Yemen issues the rial, held close to a fixed rate against the US dollar. It scores 43 of 100, where 100 means fully exposed to outside influence, and the corruption and secrecy around it stand at 86. Government pressure was reported in 6 of 37 quarters from 2010 to 2018, all of it pressing for easier money or for printing money. On 18 September 2016 President Abd-Rabbu Mansour Hadi, announcing from Saudi Arabia, sacked central bank head Mohammed bin Hammam, named Mansar al-Kaiti in his place and ordered the bank moved to Aden, with no official reason given. On 6 December 2021 he dismissed the governor and his deputy, the fourth such sacking since the civil war began. A UN Panel of Experts report in January 2021 said the Aden-based bank broke its foreign-exchange rules and laundered 'a substantial part' of a 2 billion dollar Saudi deposit, giving traders a 423 million dollar windfall. The bank rejected the claims, and on 25 March 2021, after an internal review, the panel withdrew the money-laundering and corruption accusations. Its money is printed in Russia. The Russian foreign ministry confirmed in June 2020 that the state printer Goznak had been printing notes for Yemen's government under a commercial contract signed in 2017, after separatists in Aden seized a convoy carrying 64 billion rials, about 255 million US dollars, of Russian-printed banknotes.
