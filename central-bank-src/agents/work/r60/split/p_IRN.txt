The Central Bank of the Islamic Republic of Iran issues the rial, which is in free fall. Its law leaves most power with government, 0.448 of 1 where 1 means the bank holds it. Its notes and coins come from the Security Printing and Minting Organization, a state printing works owned by the bank since 1962; before 1982 the notes were printed abroad. The government replaced governor Valiollah Seif on 25 July 2018 without reason, and dismissed his successor Abdolnasser Hemmati on 30 May 2021 for standing for president. In October 2021 a Tehran court sentenced Seif to ten years, his deputy Araghchi to eight and the intermediary Aghakhani to thirteen over an illegal foreign-currency scheme; the Supreme Court overturned the verdicts in 2023, and the prosecution of Seif and Araghchi was dropped in March 2025. Abroad, Bahrain's High Criminal Court in November 2020 fined the bank and other Iran-owned banks one million Bahraini dinars each; prosecutors said it planned to launder billions of dollars through Future Bank. Bahrain's top court upheld the conviction in 2021, with confiscation of remittances put at 184 million US dollars, 967 million euros, 53 million dirhams and 235 billion riyals.
