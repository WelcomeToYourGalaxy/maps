### CURRENT PARAGRAPH (being replaced)
The Central Bank of Aruba, created in 1986, issues the Aruban florin and holds it at a peg to the US dollar as of December 2025. Its Central Bank Ordinance of 1991, amended through 2014, keeps the bank under the Governor of Aruba and the Minister of Finance. Section 18 has the Governor appoint the president and executive directors from a shortlist of three put up by the Supervisory Board, and suspend or dismiss them by state decree; section 19(3) allows general guidelines for their duties to be set by decree; section 23 gives him the appointment and dismissal of the supervisory directors; and section 27(1) has the Government Commissioner supervise the bank under the Minister's instructions. One case is recorded: the General Audit Chamber, reviewing the country's 2001-2004 accounts, found contributions paid into an earmarked account at the bank for the Fondo Desaroyo Aruba foundation were not accounted for completely, correctly and on time, with 16,898,503 florins paid in by the Netherlands left off the balance sheet out of a 19.3 million florin balance at the end of 2000. The president who held the post until the end of 2025 spent her working life in public bodies: Jeanette R. (Jane) Semeleer began as a management adviser at Aruba's Department of Economic Affairs, later worked at the Department of External Relations, joined the bank in 1990, was appointed Executive Director in 2000 and President in 2008, and retired on 1 December 2025; no private employer is named. Crane Currency is listed as having supplied new banknotes in 2019, on a client list in another organisation's announcement rather than a contract notice, so that is reported rather than confirmed by the bank. BNP Paribas Asset Management runs a fixed income mandate in US dollars and gives advice, and Ernst & Young audits the accounts. Aruba scores 33 of 100, where 100 means fully exposed to outside influence.

1 documented case at the central bank:

2000-2001 · unexplained accounts or weak controls · official finding. Aruba's General Audit Chamber, reviewing the country's 2001-2004 annual accounts, found that contributions paid into an earmarked account held at the Centrale Bank van Aruba in the country's name for the Fondo Desaroyo Aruba foundation were not accounted for completely, correctly and on time. Of a balance of 19.3 million Aruban florins at the end of 2000, only the 3 million paid in by Aruba appeared in the country's accounts, while 16,898,503 florins paid in by the Netherlands were left off the balance sheet. 1991 · statutory arrangement letting the government direct the bank. Aruba's Central Bank Ordinance (AB 1991 no. GT 32), as amended through AB 2014 no. 5 and published by the Centrale Bank van Aruba in Dutch and in the bank's English translation, keeps the bank under the Governor of Aruba and the Minister of Finance. Section 18 says the president and executive directors are appointed by the Governor from a shortlist of three put up by the Supervisory Board, and that the Governor may suspend or dismiss them by state decree. Section 19(3) says general guidelines for the performance of the duties of the president and executive directors can be set out in a state decree. Section 23 says the chairman of the Supervisory Board is the Government Commissioner, appointed and dismissed by the Governor, that the other supervisory directors are appointed by the Governor, and that they may be suspended or dismissed before the end of their term by the Governor. Section 27(1) says the Government Commissioner supervises the bank's activities on behalf of the Country and acts in accordance with general or specific instructions given by the Minister. This is a standing arrangement with no end date. Central Bank Ordinance (AB 1991 no. GT 32), English translation (Centrale Bank van Aruba)
