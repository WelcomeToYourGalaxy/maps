### CURRENT PARAGRAPH (being replaced)
Lesotho issues the loti, and the South African rand circulates alongside it. Under the Common Monetary Area, which replaced a Rand Monetary Area agreement signed in December 1974 and was followed by a Multilateral Monetary Agreement in February 1992 when Namibia joined, member currencies are pegged to the rand one for one and are legal tender only in the country that issues them; Lesotho must back what it issues fully with rand assets, and South Africa pays it an imputed return on rand circulating there. All five of its current notes, introduced on 23 December 2021, are printed by De La Rue. In February 2024 three South Africans appeared in the Ficksburg Magistrates Court on fraud and money laundering charges over 57 million rand said to have been stolen from the Central Bank of Lesotho. The Hawks, a South African police unit, said Lesotho officials intercepted vouchers meant for service providers and diverted the payments to co-conspirators in South Africa, and that some officials were already before court in Lesotho. The charges are unproven. Its 2023 accounts were audited by Moteane, Quashie & Associates with SizweNtsalubaGobodo Grant Thornton. Lesotho scores 32 of 100, where 100 means fully exposed to outside influence, and corruption and secrecy around the bank is its weakest side, at 60. At a hearing on Monday 8 June 2026, at which the Auditor General presented consolidated financial statements for 31 March 2023 and 2024, the parliamentary Public Accounts Committee called for a forensic audit of the Central Bank of Lesotho to trace missing public money. Its chairperson, Machabana Lemphane-Letsie, said the bank must undergo a forensic audit and say who the money had been paid to. The figures in the report do not agree with each other: its headline puts the gap at M3.9 billion, while the statements it describes show government cash of M5.91 billion at 31 March 2024 against M2.82 billion in the consolidated accounts, a difference of M3.09 billion, which a quotation in the same piece also gives. The committee resolved that the Auditor General's office submit a budget for the audit by 30 June; that office said it has neither the expertise nor any budget allocated for one. The bank resisted, citing its own 2000 Act, which provides for an external auditor and limits the Auditor General to auditing currency balances rather than government payments the bank makes for the state.

OWNERSHIP AND OUTSIDE CONTROL

Common Monetary Area (common monetary area)

The Rand Monetary Area agreement was signed in December 1974 by South Africa, Swaziland and Lesotho; it was replaced by the Common Monetary Area in July 1986 and by the Multilateral Monetary Agreement in February 1992, when Namibia formally joined.
Member currencies have been pegged to the rand at par since their introduction; they are legal tender only in the issuing country.
Lesotho and Namibia must fully back their currency issues with rand assets; Eswatini does not face this requirement.
The rand has not been de jure legal tender in Eswatini since 1986.
South Africa makes compensatory payments representing an imputed return on rand circulating as legal tender in Lesotho and Namibia; Eswatini has received none since 1986.
