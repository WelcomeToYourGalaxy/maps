===== BOX =====
NATIONAL BANK OF THE REPUBLIC OF NORTH MACEDONIA
North Macedonia
North Macedonia's money is the denar, issued by the National Bank of the Republic of North Macedonia and held in practice at a fixed rate to the euro. It scores 24 of 100, where 100 means fully exposed to outside influence, and its weakest side is corruption and secrecy around it. Its notes are printed abroad, by three firms in turn. The bank contracted the French firm Oberthur Fiduciaire to print 100-denar notes, reported on 8 May 2026, for 59,800,000 denars, about 972,358 euros. It had contracted the Russian state company Goznak for the 100-denar notes in 2021, for about 400,000 euros, as part of a 2.2 million euro reissue carrying the state's changed constitutional name. Three separate contracts with De La Rue International cover the 10, 500 and 1,000 denar notes, 45 million notes for 3.775 million euros in total, 20 million of them polymer. One case is recorded, running from 2017 to 2020, and it is unproven: an association of depositors of Eurostandard Bank said in a press release that the governor tipped off the finance minister and other large depositors so they could take their money out before the bank was put under administration, and that financial police reports were kept secret so it could keep operating. The association said Finance Minister Nina Angelovska withdrew more than 200,000 euros months before the central bank acted, and that over 60 million euros were lost; no charges and no official finding are reported. The European Central Bank gave the bank a temporary line to borrow euros against collateral from 15 December 2022 to 15 January 2024, which has expired. One career move is on record and it too stays outside the private sector, so it is listed but not counted: governor Anita Angelovska Bezhoska spent about eight years at the Ministry of Finance, ending as manager of the treasury department, and was state secretary in 2003 and 2004, then worked as an economist at the International Monetary Fund in Washington before joining the bank as chief economist, serving almost eight years as vice-governor and becoming governor in May 2018. The page the bank publishes names no private firm and no commercial bank. Its books are checked from outside by Grant Thornton DOO of Skopje, which signed the independent auditor's report on the bank's 2023 financial statements and gave an opinion without qualifications; the bank publishes that report on its own site and in the Official Gazette.

Anita Angelovska Bezhoska (Governor): from manager of the Treasury Department and State Secretary at the Ministry of Finance, and economist in the IMF's European Department, to Vice-Governor and then Governor of the National Bank. The National Bank of the Republic of North Macedonia's governor page gives Anita Angelovska Bezhoska's full career. At the Ministry of Finance she was an expert associate on the financial system, then head of the project to establish a treasury system, then manager of the Treasury Department, about eight years in total, and State Secretary in 2003 and 2004. At the International Monetary Fund she was an economist in the European Department for about two years and briefly in the Fiscal Affairs Department in Washington, and has served on IMF technical assistance teams for European countries since 2006. She joined the bank as Chief Economist, served almost eight years as Vice-Governor for monetary policy, research and statistics, and became Governor in May 2018. The page names no private firm and no commercial bank: every employer in it is a state body or an international organisation. The European Commission's own speaker profile for Dimitar Bogov states that he led the National Bank of the Republic of Macedonia as Governor between 2011 and 2018, that before that he served four years as vice-governor, and that between 2002 and 2007 he was Chief Economist of Stopanska banka AD Skopje, a member of NBG Group. The same profile records earlier managerial positions in the Ministry of Finance and the State Statistical Office.
