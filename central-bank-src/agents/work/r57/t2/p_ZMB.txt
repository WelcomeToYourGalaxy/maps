BANK OF ZAMBIA
Zambia
The Bank of Zambia issues the kwacha, which floats within a band of about 5 percent against the US dollar. Zambia scores 32 of 100 for exposure to outside influence, where 0 means shielded and 100 fully exposed, and its law scores 0.637 of 1 for written independence from government. The bank launched its Heritage Series of six notes and six coins on 31 March 2025, and the 10 and 20 kwacha notes were printed on Landqart's Durasafe composite substrate; the bank thanked Landqart publicly at the launch and was given a frame of unprinted 10 and 20 kwacha notes. Who printed the series is not published: BanknoteNews records the printer as unknown for every denomination. Denny Kalyalya came to the governorship in February 2015 from the World Bank Group, where he had been executive director for the Africa Group One constituency. On 22 August 2020 President Edgar Lungu dismissed him, less than a week after he warned of economic difficulties, and appointed Christopher Mvunga from the finance ministry; Kalyalya returned as governor in October 2021. In October 2015 the Drug Enforcement Commission arrested a section officer for theft by a public servant and money laundering, accusing him of taking 43,660 US dollars and 20,335 kwacha in cash between January and September 2015; he was charged, and no court outcome is given, so the accusation is unproven. The Auditor General audits the accounts each year under the Bank of Zambia Act No. 5 of 2022. One career move is on record and it does not run through private finance, so it is listed but not counted: Investrust Bank announced on 16 April 2015 that Dr Richard K. E. Chembe, who had held senior management posts at the Bank of Zambia and the International Monetary Fund, would become its managing director from 1 May 2015, subject to the Bank of Zambia's approval. Investrust is not a private employer in the sense this layer counts: ZCCM Investments Holdings, which describes itself as state owned, raised its stake in it from 45.4 to 71.4 per cent in 2018 and was already its largest shareholder at the time of the appointment.

3 documented career moves between the bank and private finance, the IMF or the World Bank:

Denny H. Kalyalya (Governor and Chairperson of the Board, Bank of Zambia, February 2015 to August 2020 and again since October 2021): from Executive Director for Africa Group One at the World Bank Group into the governorship of the Bank of Zambia in February 2015. The Financial Stability Board's published profile of Denny Kalyalya, who co-chairs its Regional Consultative Group for Sub-Saharan Africa, lists his career. It states he was Alternate Executive Director for the Africa Group One Constituency at the World Bank Group from 2010 to 2012 and Executive Director for that constituency from 2012 to 2014, and that he became Governor and Chairperson of the Board of the Bank of Zambia in February 2015, serving until August 2020 and returning to the post in October 2021. The profile also lists him as a Special Appointee at the International Monetary Fund, with no dates, and earlier as Director of Economics and then Deputy Governor for Operations at the Bank of Zambia, and as Head of the Economics Department and Assistant Dean of Post Graduate Studies at the University of Zambia. His outside board seat is at the African Export-Import Bank since 2019, which the profile does not classify. No commercial bank, asset manager, insurer or brokerage appears anywhere in the profile. This record is therefore flagged as a public move; no private-finance revolving door is evidenced for Zambia. Denny Kalyalya, Co-Chair RCG for Sub-Saharan Africa (Financial Stability Board)
Dr Austin Mwape (Deputy Governor of Operations, Bank of Zambia): from Deputy Governor of Operations at the Bank of Zambia to chairman of the board of directors of Stanbic Bank Zambia (from December 2016). The report states that Stanbic Bank Zambia welcomed Dr Austin Mwape as the new chairman of its board of directors, and identifies him as having served as deputy governor of operations at the Bank of Zambia; it gives no dates for the central bank role. Stanbic Bank Zambia's own announcement of his successor records that Mwape held the chair from December 2016 until 1 September 2020, and describes the bank as part of the Standard Bank Group, Africa's largest bank by assets. Stanbic Bank Zambia welcomes Dr Austin Mwape as new board chairman (Lusaka Times)
Dr Richard K. E. Chembe (senior management, Bank of Zambia): from senior management positions at the Bank of Zambia and the IMF to Managing Director of Investrust Bank Plc, effective 1 May 2015. Investrust Bank Plc's directorate announcement of 16 April 2015 states that Dr Richard K. E. Chembe, who held senior management positions at the Bank of Zambia and the International Monetary Fund and was special assistant for economic and development affairs to Zambia's fourth president, was appointed managing director effective 1 May 2015 subject to Bank of Zambia approval, replacing Mr Friday Ndhlovu who was to retire on 31 May 2015; the hiring employer is NOT private, because ZCCM Investments Holdings Plc, which its own 22 May 2018 release describes as 'State owned ZCCM IH', raised its Investrust stake from 45.4% to 71.4% that year, meaning the Zambian state mining holding company was already the bank's largest shareholder when Chembe was appointed, so this is recorded as a public move and does not evidence a move through private finance.
