BERMUDA MONETARY AUTHORITY
Bermuda
Bermuda's money is the Bermudian dollar, issued by the Bermuda Monetary Authority, which is the central bank. As of December 2025 the dollar sat in a de facto peg to the US dollar, meaning it is held at a fixed rate in practice. Bermuda scores 33 of 100 for exposure to outside influence, where 100 means fully exposed. The weakest part is corruption and secrecy around the bank, at 50, mainly because the island's laws hide who owns money: financial secrecy is 71.3 of 100, 54th of 141 places, while the World Bank's measure of control of corruption puts it at 72 of 100. Independence written into law is almost absent, 0.06 of 1 and second from last of 191 places, with no reform since 1970, and the bank scores 3.5 of 15 for what it tells the public. No documented case at the bank was found, which does not prove none happened. The Office of the Auditor-General audits the accounts, as it did for 2021. The notes are printed abroad: the authority announced on 30 October 2024 that its new 2 and 5 dollar notes, printed by De La Rue on polymer, would enter circulation that autumn, Bermuda's first polymer notes and among the first anywhere to carry King Charles III's effigy.
