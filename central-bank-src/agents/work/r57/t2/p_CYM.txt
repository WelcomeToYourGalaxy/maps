CAYMAN ISLANDS MONETARY AUTHORITY
Cayman Islands
The Cayman Islands Monetary Authority acts as the central bank of the Cayman Islands, and the currency is the Cayman Islands dollar. In June 2015 it said it would commission an internal review of the involvement of local people and financial institutions in the FIFA corruption case, after the Caymanian businessman Jeffrey Webb was arrested in Switzerland on 27 May. The husband of its managing director Cindy Scotland, the former minister Mark Scotland, had worked for the Cayman Islands Football Association under Webb and was with Webb at the arrest; Gloria Glidden, deputy head of the authority's banking division, is the wife of the former minister Cline Glidden, who was also in Switzerland with Webb. The chairman, George McCarthy, said Scotland would remain managing director and that there was no evidence any Cayman bank had done anything wrong. That review is still under investigation. A commemorative 70-dollar note for the Platinum Jubilee of Queen Elizabeth II, introduced on 12 October 2023, was printed by De La Rue and is not part of the circulating series. The accounts are audited by the Auditor General of the Cayman Islands, Patrick O. Smith. The authority scores 35 of 100, where 100 means fully exposed to outside influence.

Greg Swart (Director, board of the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority): from Managing Member and Chief Financial Officer of Farallon Capital Management LLC (2002-2019), and about twelve years at Goldman Sachs before that, to Director on the board of the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority. The Authority's own board page says Swart was Managing Member and Chief Financial Officer of Farallon Capital Management LLC, which it describes as a global alternative asset management firm, from 2002 to 2019, and consulted for the firm after retiring from full-time work in 2019. It also records about twelve years at Goldman Sachs, where he ran a unit providing accounting and administration services to hedge funds within the prime brokerage business and was President of GS Financial Products from 1991 to 1996, after six years at Ernst & Young in New Zealand.
