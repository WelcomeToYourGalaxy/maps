===== BOX =====
CENTRAL BANK OF BELIZE
Belize
The Central Bank of Belize issues the Belize dollar, held at a fixed rate to the US dollar. In August 2021 governor Gustavo Manuel Vasquez lost his post. Prime Minister John Briceno said the board had recommended he resign because of a lot of policy differences. Vasquez wrote that his appointment was terminated on the instructions of the Ministry of Finance, that he faced opposition from it, and that he had tried to restore the bank's integrity and operational autonomy. Castillo Sanchez & Burrell, LLP, Chartered Accountants audited the 2013 accounts, and De La Rue printed the new 100-dollar note introduced on 4 August 2025. Belize scores 32 of 100, where 100 means fully exposed to outside influence, and it tells the public little, 4 of 15 on a transparency measure in 2019. Two career moves are recorded, both out of the bank and back into it. The Central Bank of Belize's own Office of the Governor page gives Kareem Michael as Monetary Economist in 2000 and Deputy Director of Research in 2008, says he resigned in 2014 to become Senior Business Analyst at Belize Electricity Limited, and records him returning as Deputy Governor in November 2017, Acting Governor on 13 August 2021 and Governor on 1 December 2021. Who owns Belize Electricity Limited is not stated there, and it is a utility rather than a financial firm. The second move does run through private finance, in the bank's own words: the same page says Deputy Governor Marina Laetitia Murillo joined the bank as a research officer in August 1991, worked from 1998 in project management in the non-government sector and in management positions in the private financial sector, and returned in November 2011 as Economist I, becoming assistant manager of the financial system development unit in October 2018 and Deputy Governor on 1 September 2022. The firms are not named. Moving between jobs is not wrongdoing.

2 documented cases at the central bank:

2016-2026 · public accounts committee finding: material differences between central bank balances and government records · official finding. The Joint Public Accounts Committee of Belize's National Assembly, reporting on the Auditor General's report for April 2016 to March 2017, listed as a priority area 'Significant material differences between Central Bank and SmartStream balances', with discrepancies totalling 42 million, 19 million, 3 million and 1.4 million dollars. At the Committee's hearings in March 2026 Auditor General Maria Rodriguez said that not even the Central Bank accounts were being reconciled, and referred to differences noted between confirmation letters received from the Central Bank and the government's records. The Committee recorded that the 19,489,652.02 dollar difference shown for the Debt Service Escrow Account was a typographical error with no actual difference, which the Auditor General confirmed. Report from the Joint Public Accounts Committee on the Report of the Auditor General for the Year April 2016 to March 2017 (National Assembly of Belize)
2011-2012 · audit finding: unreconciled government accounts at the central bank and unreconciled public debt records it manages · official finding. The Auditor General of Belize reported for the 2011/2012 financial year that the Treasury could not show that 25 bank accounts held by the Central Bank of Belize on behalf of the Government of Belize had been reconciled for the audited period. The same report found that the Commonwealth Secretariat public debt recording system managed by the Central Bank of Belize did not reconcile with the Accountant General's financial statements, and that interest payments recorded by the Central Bank differed from the payments recorded in the government's Smart Stream system. No amounts were given for these findings. Report of the Auditor General 2011-2012 (Office of the Auditor General, Belize)

2 documented career moves between the bank and private finance, the IMF or the World Bank:

Kareem Michael (Monetary Economist from 2000, Deputy Director of Research from 2008, Deputy Governor from November 2017, Acting Governor from 13 August 2021, Governor from 1 December 2021): from Deputy Director of the Research Department at the Central Bank of Belize to Senior Business Analyst at Belize Electricity Limited (2014) ; from Belize Electricity Limited back to the Central Bank of Belize as Deputy Governor (November 2017), Acting Governor (13 August 2021) and Governor (1 December 2021). The bank's own Office of the Governor page states the career as follows. He began at the Central Bank as Monetary Economist in 2000, led the development and oversight of the Bank's Statistical Unit, and was promoted to Deputy Director of the Research Department in 2008. In 2014 he resigned from the Central Bank to take up the position of Senior Business Analyst at Belize Electricity Limited. He returned to the Central Bank in November 2017 as Deputy Governor, overseeing the Financial System Supervision Department, the Research Department and the Compliance Unit. He served as Acting Governor from 13 August 2021 and assumed the substantive position of Governor on 1 December 2021. The outside employer is an electricity utility, not a financial firm, and the page does not say who owns Belize Electricity Limited, so the record does not establish a private-sector move; the central bank side of the career is the public side. The Governor (Central Bank of Belize)
Marina Laetitia Murillo (Deputy Governor, Monetary and Financial Systems): from management positions in the private financial sector (employers not named) back to the Central Bank of Belize as Economist I. The Central Bank of Belize's own Office of the Governor page states that Marina Laetitia Murillo began at the Bank as a Research Officer in August 1991, that from 1998 she worked in project management in the non-government sector and in management positions in the private financial sector, and that she returned to the Bank in November 2011 as Economist I, rising to Assistant Manager of the Financial System Development Unit in October 2018 and to Deputy Governor effective 1 September 2022; the outside employers are financial and the page itself characterises them as private, so the private-finance fact comes from the central bank's own published biography rather than from any inference, though the firms are not named.
