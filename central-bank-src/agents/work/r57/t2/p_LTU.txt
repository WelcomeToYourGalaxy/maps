BANK OF LITHUANIA
Lithuania
Lithuania uses the euro, so the Bank of Lithuania does not set interest rates itself: the European Central Bank sets them for the 21 countries that share the currency, a group Bulgaria joined on 1 January 2026. Lithuania's own case ended in acquittal. An official who monitored the commercial bank Snoras was sacked in November 2011 and charged with letting Snoras keep information from the bank's leadership, including on assets in Swiss banks; a guilty verdict in March 2014 was annulled by a Vilnius court, which found that no crime had been committed, and that ruling can still be appealed to the Supreme Court. Two further cases sit at the European Central Bank. In case 1697/2016/ANA the European Ombudsman found maladministration and in January 2018 recommended that president Mario Draghi suspend his membership of the Group of Thirty, a forum where central bankers sit alongside private financiers such as Citi's chief executive Jane Fraser, saying it was not in keeping with the bank's independence; the bank declined, and Draghi did not suspend it. On 18 May 2015 one of its officials, Cœuré, told a private evening event in London that the bank would bring forward its asset purchases, and the speech was published only the next morning, which the bank put down to an internal procedure error; Draghi said no market-sensitive information had been revealed. Six of that bank's officials have moved to or from private finance, among them Otmar Issing, its former chief economist, who in 2006 took what he called a loose advisory position with Goldman Sachs, and Luis de Guindos, who headed Lehman Brothers and Nomura Securities in Iberia from 2006 to 2008 and became vice-president in 2018. Lithuania's 5.8 tonnes of gold, 466 bars, are all stored at the Bank of England as custodian, none of it at home. UAB Ernst & Young Baltic audited its accounts for 2018 to 2021, UAB ROSK Consulting for 2022 to 2024. It scores 20 of 100, where 100 means fully exposed to outside influence.
